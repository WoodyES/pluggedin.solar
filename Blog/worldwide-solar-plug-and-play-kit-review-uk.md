Scroll through the [live ENA compliant devices registry](/blog/uk-compliant-plug-in-solar-devices-live) and one name now appears more often than any other: **Worldwide Solar Ltd**. In the space of a few days it has listed system after system — Hoymiles HMS-800-2WB microinverters paired with JA Solar panels, VaySunic VM800WE-P2 inverters paired with AIKO Neostar panels, with-mount and without-mount variants of each — all sitting at or near the 800W regulatory ceiling.

If you've been comparing plug-in solar kits and keep seeing "Worldwide Solar Plug & Play Solar Kit" pop up with a different panel and inverter combination each time, this is why. It's worth understanding what's actually going on before you buy one.

## What "Worldwide Solar" actually sells

Worldwide Solar isn't a manufacturer of inverters or panels — it's an assembler. Each of its ENA listings takes an already-approved microinverter (so far, mostly Hoymiles and VaySunic units that separately hold their own [G98](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk) approvals) and pairs it with a couple of solar panels from established panel makers like JA Solar and AIKO. Some listings include a mounting system, others explicitly say "mounting system not included" — so read the model name carefully, because two otherwise-identical listings can differ only in whether a bracket kit ships in the box.

This "kit of parts" approach isn't unusual — several UK plug-in solar sellers do the same thing — but the sheer number of near-identical SKUs makes comparison shopping harder than it should be. The underlying hardware (the microinverter itself) is doing the regulatory heavy lifting; the panels and mounting are largely interchangeable across the range.

## Why this matters for compliance

The ENA registry only certifies the **inverter**, tested to [G98](/blog/g98-notification-guide-uk-homeowners) for grid connection. A "Worldwide Solar Plug & Play Solar Kit" listing is really a bundled system reference — you'll see the underlying inverter's own ENA reference quoted in the comments field (for example, `HOYMZ/14724/V1/A2` for the Hoymiles HMS-800-2WB). That's useful: it means the safety-critical component has already passed independent testing on its own, separately from whichever panels get bundled with it.

Before buying any Worldwide Solar kit — or any bundle from a smaller assembler — check the exact model number against the registry entry, not just the seller's product page title. Model names are long and easy to mix up, and the difference between "with mounting system" and "mounting system not included" changes what else you need to budget for.

## What's missing from the ENA listing

Being on the register tells you the inverter passed type testing. It tells you nothing about:

- **UK retail availability** — none of the Worldwide Solar entries currently list a stocking retailer among the [major UK sellers](/blog/retailers-stocking-plug-in-solar-uk-2026) like B&Q, Currys, Screwfix or Amazon UK
- **Warranty terms** — no published warranty length for the assembled kit, separate from whatever the underlying Hoymiles or VaySunic inverter warranty covers
- **App and monitoring support** — Hoymiles has its own app ecosystem (S-Miles Cloud) which should carry over, but this isn't confirmed for the Worldwide Solar-branded bundle
- **Panel degradation and BOM quality** — JA Solar and AIKO are legitimate panel manufacturers, but panel tier and cell technology varies significantly between "monofacial" and "bifacial" variants in the listings

Compare that against an established option like [EcoFlow's STREAM range](/blog/ecoflow-stream-review-uk), where retailer stock, warranty terms and app support are all independently confirmed and reviewed.

## Should you buy one

If you're comparing a Worldwide Solar bundle against a better-known kit on price alone, remember that ENA compliance is the floor, not the ceiling — see our guide on [how to spot a non-compliant plug-in solar kit](/blog/how-to-spot-non-compliant-plug-in-solar-kit-uk) for the checks that matter beyond the registry listing. A compliant inverter bundled with unfamiliar panels and no confirmed retailer or warranty is a different risk profile from a [known, retailer-stocked kit](/blog/best-plug-in-solar-kit-uk), even if both are legally identical on paper.

For most first-time buyers, especially anyone still working through their [G98 notification](/blog/g98-notification-guide-uk-homeowners), the safer starting point is a kit with a confirmed UK warranty and support line — check our [warranty guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-warranty-guide-uk) before you commit to any assembler you haven't heard of before.

### Is a Worldwide Solar kit safe to install?

If the specific model number matches an ENA-listed entry with G98 approval, it's legally compliant to install and notify to your DNO in the same way as any other plug-in microgenerator. Safety compliance and product support are separate questions, though — verify warranty and retailer backing before buying.

### Why are there so many near-identical Worldwide Solar listings?

Each listing represents a different combination of inverter, panel type (monofacial vs bifacial), and whether a mounting system is included. They aren't duplicates — they're distinct SKUs — but the underlying inverter technology is often shared across several listings.
