**AS/NZS 4777.2** is the Australian/New Zealand standard for grid-connected inverters. It sets the technical requirements every inverter must meet before the Clean Energy Council (CEC) will approve it for grid-tied installation in Australia. Understanding what it does and doesn't cover is essential for anyone shopping for plug-in solar down under.

## What AS/NZS 4777.2 requires

The 2020 revision (currently enforced) requires:

- **Anti-islanding** — the inverter must disconnect from the grid within 2 seconds of grid loss
- **Volt-VAR / Volt-Watt** — dynamic power/reactive-power support based on grid voltage
- **Frequency response** — controlled ramp during frequency deviations
- **Communication readiness** — DRM (Demand Response Mode) capability for future flexible-services participation

Every inverter used for grid-tied residential solar in AU must be tested to this standard AND CEC-listed. No exceptions in mainstream utility interconnection paths.

## Where plug-in solar sits

Plug-in kits with inverters that plug into a domestic power point technically fall outside standard AS/NZS 4777 interconnection scope — because they don't "install" through the traditional switchboard route with a licensed electrician's sign-off. There is no mainstream Australian equivalent to the UK's SI 2026/848 that formally legalises plug-in solar.

The practical outcome: **grid-tied plug-in kits are widely available for import** (Amazon AU, eBay, specialist retailers), but they lack the CEC-approved status needed for feed-in tariffs, formal interconnection or DNSP notification. Owners use them anyway, treating them like an appliance.

For details on legal off-grid alternatives see [off-grid solar for remote AU properties](/au/blog/off-grid-solar-remote-au-properties).

## The off-grid route is genuinely legal

Portable solar setups that don't backfeed the grid — panels feeding an EcoFlow, Anker or Jackery battery — don't require AS/NZS 4777 compliance at all. The battery's inverter is a UPS-class device serving isolated loads. This is why the AU market is heavily skewed toward portable/off-grid rather than grid-tied plug-in.
