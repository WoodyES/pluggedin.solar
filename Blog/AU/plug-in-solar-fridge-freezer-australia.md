Fridges and freezers are near the top of the list for "can plug-in solar actually run this" questions in Australia — and unlike a lot of other appliances, the honest answer is a qualified yes, provided you understand how compressor loads and battery buffering actually work together.

## Fridge wattage is lower than people assume

A typical household fridge-freezer draws **100–250W** while the compressor is actively running, but compressors cycle on and off — averaged across a full day, most modern fridges pull closer to **30–60W** continuous equivalent. A chest freezer is similar or slightly lower once it reaches temperature. That's well within range of a single portable solar panel plus a battery-based power station, which is the realistic path for off-grid fridge power in Australia since plug-in kits here aren't CEC-regulated grid-tied devices — they're portable, battery-paired setups.

## Why the battery matters more than the panel

This is the key difference versus the UK's plug-in microgenerator rules, which explicitly ban batteries in the circuit. In Australia, there's no such restriction on a portable kit — in fact, a battery is essential for fridge duty, because:

- A compressor fridge needs **continuous power**, day and night. Solar panels alone stop producing the moment the sun sets or a cloud rolls over.
- A **CEC-recognised battery** (LiFePO4 is standard in current portable power stations) stores daytime generation and discharges it smoothly overnight, keeping the compressor cycle uninterrupted.
- Compressor **startup surge** (briefly 2–3× running wattage) needs an inverter rated to handle the spike without tripping — most 1,000W+ portable power stations handle this easily; very small sub-300W units sometimes don't.

See our [battery storage vs no battery](/au/blog/battery-storage-vs-no-battery-plug-in-solar-australia) comparison for the broader tradeoffs between battery-backed and panel-only setups.

## Sizing a fridge-capable setup

For a standard household fridge-freezer running continuously:

- **Daily energy need:** roughly 1–1.5 kWh/day for an efficient modern fridge
- **Panel size:** 400–800W of panels gives you enough daylight charging margin to replenish the battery even on an overcast day
- **Battery size:** 1–2 kWh minimum to bridge overnight hours; 2–3 kWh gives a comfortable buffer for a run of cloudy days
- **Inverter:** look for a continuous rating of at least 500W with a surge rating of 1,000W+ to handle compressor startup cleanly

Use our [battery sizing guide](/au/blog/solar-battery-sizing-guide-australia) to match a specific battery capacity to your fridge's actual draw — check the compliance plate on the back of your unit for its rated wattage rather than guessing.

## Where this makes the most sense

Running a fridge on plug-in solar isn't really a "save money on your main house fridge" play for most grid-connected Australian homes — it's more valuable for:

- **Off-grid and remote properties** where there's no mains power to begin with — see our [off-grid solar guide](/au/blog/off-grid-solar-australia-guide) for full-property sizing beyond just a fridge
- **Granny flats, sheds, and garages** with a bar fridge or chest freezer but no easy mains run
- **Caravans and camping setups**, where a 12V or 240V portable fridge is standard gear for [Grey Nomads](/au/blog/camping-caravan-plug-in-solar-grey-nomad-au) doing extended remote travel
- **Blackout resilience** — keeping perishables cold during the kind of extended outages that follow storms or bushfire-related grid faults

## CEC approval and what it actually covers

If you're running a grid-tied rooftop system, the inverter must be a [CEC-approved product](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-cec-approved-inverters-au) under AS/NZS 4777, installed by a licensed electrician. A portable plug-in kit powering a standalone fridge via battery — not connected to your switchboard — sits outside that grid-tied framework entirely, which is exactly why portable setups are the practical route for this use case rather than a DIY grid-tied installation.

## Heat is the other variable

Australian summers push ambient temperatures that make compressors work harder and longer per cycle, increasing real-world draw above the nameplate average. If you're sizing a fridge setup in Queensland, inland NSW, or WA's north, pad your battery and panel sizing upward by 20–30% versus the baseline numbers above, and check our [air conditioning](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-air-conditioning-australia) guide for how heat-driven load more broadly affects portable solar sizing.

### Will a cheap 100W panel keep a fridge running?

Not reliably on its own. A 100W panel produces too little daily energy to keep a battery topped up against continuous fridge draw, especially through a run of overcast days. Pair any panel below 300W with a modest battery and treat it as a top-up source rather than your sole supply.

### Does running a fridge drain the battery faster than expected?

If your fridge is older or poorly insulated, yes — older compressor fridges can draw 2–3× the wattage of a modern energy-efficient model. Check the energy rating label; a fridge with a low star rating will eat into your battery runtime much faster than the sizing figures above assume.

### Can I run a fridge and other appliances on the same portable kit?

Yes, as long as total continuous draw stays under your inverter's rated output and your battery capacity covers the combined daily energy use. Adding a second compressor appliance (like a chest freezer) roughly doubles your sizing requirements, so recalculate rather than assuming the same kit stretches to cover both.
