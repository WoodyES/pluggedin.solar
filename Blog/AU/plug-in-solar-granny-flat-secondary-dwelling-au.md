Granny flats, secondary dwellings and DA-approved backyard studios are a fast-growing housing category across Australia. Most are separately metered from the main dwelling, which means their electricity costs run on standard residential tariffs. A plug-in solar setup is a near-perfect fit — small footprint, no structural modification, portable if the tenant moves out.

## Why granny flats suit plug-in solar

- **Small floor area** = small daily consumption (5–10 kWh) that 400–800W of solar can meaningfully offset
- **Separately metered** = savings accrue directly to the granny-flat electricity bill
- **Typically not owner-occupied** = tenants may not fund permanent installations, portable is neutral
- **Often single-story with clear roof access** = simple deployment

## The typical setup

- **200–400W panels** on tilt frame in the small garden or courtyard
- **1kWh portable power station** (EcoFlow DELTA 2 or similar) inside the granny flat as backup + storage
- **Panels connected to power station via MC4** — off-grid style, no need for AS/NZS 4777 compliance

Annual generation in Sydney: ~500–800 kWh. At typical peak tariff of 50c/kWh (self-consumed), that's $250–$400/year saved on the granny flat electricity bill.

## Airbnb / short-stay units

For Airbnb operators, plug-in solar becomes a marketing point ("eco-conscious accommodation, solar-powered") as well as an operating expense reducer. Guests are often willing to pay 5–10% premium for sustainability claims.

## Regulatory notes

Off-grid setups (panels → battery → loads) sit outside AS/NZS 4777 and don't require any approval. Grid-tied setups feeding back to the main switchboard do need CEC-accredited install. Most granny-flat plug-in setups take the off-grid route.

For the AS/NZS 4777 background see the [regulatory plain English guide](/au/blog/as-nzs-4777-plain-english-plug-in-solar-au).
