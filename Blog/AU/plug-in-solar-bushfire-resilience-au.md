Australia's bushfire seasons stretched from October to April in the Black Summer of 2019-20 and have not shortened since. Rural and semi-rural residents across VIC, NSW, SA and WA are increasingly configuring portable solar + battery kits as bushfire resilience. This guide covers what actually keeps working when the grid drops for 3–7 days during a fire event.

## What plug-in solar can and can't do

**Can:** run essential loads (fridge, lighting, phones, CB radio, medical devices, small fan) for days.
**Cannot:** power air conditioning, hot water systems, electric ranges, or run a well pump without careful sizing.

For fire-active periods, essentials-only is the right frame.

## The resilience setup

- **400W foldable or rigid solar panels** (deploy after front passes, not during — burning ash destroys panels)
- **2 kWh LiFePO4 portable power station**
- **Solar MPPT input** already built into modern power stations
- **Pre-storm: fully charge the battery** 48 hours ahead when smoke is visible on horizon

## Typical daily draw during evacuation-ready mode

| Load | Wh/day |
|---|---|
| Fridge | 800 |
| Lighting | 100 |
| Phone/laptop | 200 |
| CB radio (essential in bushfire areas) | 100 |
| Fan (evening) | 300 |
| CPAP if applicable | 300 |
| **Total** | **~1,800 Wh/day** |

2 kWh battery sustains ~1 day; 400W solar recharges 1,800 Wh/day even through smoke-filtered light. Sustainable indefinitely as long as sun eventually penetrates.

## Panel storage during the fire itself

Bring panels indoors or into a fire-proof shed during active fire threat — flying embers destroy exposed panels and can start secondary fires from panel-frame heating. Redeploy once immediate threat has passed.

## What NOT to skip

- **Battery indoors**, not in garage — LiFePO4 handles heat OK but ash intake is bad for cooling vents
- **Backup phone charger** independent of the main battery — always keep one 20,000mAh USB battery charged separately
- **Written evacuation plan** — solar setup is Plan A; drive-out is Plan B
