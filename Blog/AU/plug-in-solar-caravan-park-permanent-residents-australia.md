Permanent and long-stay residents in Australian caravan and residential parks are a genuinely different audience to the touring Grey Nomad crowd most portable solar content is written for. If you live year-round, or for extended stretches, in a park home, cabin, or fixed-site caravan, your power setup, your park agreement, and your actual day-to-day loads all look more like a small permanent dwelling than a holiday rig — and that changes what portable solar is actually useful for.

## How this is different from touring solar

Most Australian content about caravan solar assumes a traveling setup: panels that fold away, a system sized for a few nights off-grid between powered sites, built around mobility first. Our [Grey Nomad guide](/au/blog/camping-caravan-plug-in-solar-grey-nomad-au) covers that case well. Permanent park residents have the opposite priority — the site doesn't move, mains power is already connected and metered, and the question is whether a small portable or semi-permanent solar setup can meaningfully cut a power bill that, in many parks, is billed at a fixed rate well above standard retail tariffs.

## The site power reality

Most Australian caravan and residential parks meter and on-sell electricity to permanent residents, and park power rates are frequently higher than a standard retail plan — sometimes substantially so, since the park operator is effectively acting as a reseller with less competitive pressure than the open retail market. This is the single biggest reason plug-in solar makes more financial sense for a permanent park resident than for an average suburban homeowner: every kWh of self-generated daytime use is avoiding a rate that might be 40-60c/kWh rather than the state averages covered in our [solar panel cost guide](/au/blog/solar-panel-cost-australia-2026).

Because the site is grid-connected and generally fixed in place, the practical setup looks closer to the AS/NZS 4777 grid-tied framework than the purely off-grid approach covered in our [off-grid remote properties guide](/au/blog/off-grid-solar-remote-au-properties) — though whether a grid-tied microinverter kit is permitted on your specific site depends entirely on the park's own rules, covered next.

## Check the park agreement before buying anything

This is the step most guides skip, and it's the one that actually matters most. Residential park and caravan park site agreements vary enormously between operators, and many include clauses covering:

- **Structural alterations to the site or dwelling**, which can extend to permanent panel mounting on a park home roof
- **Appliance and equipment approval**, sometimes requiring anything connected to the site's power supply to be disclosed or inspected
- **Insurance and liability conditions**, particularly around anything mounted externally that could be storm or wind damage risk to neighbouring sites

Treat this the same way our [landlords guide](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-landlords-australia) treats a rental tenancy: get written confirmation from park management before installing anything beyond a genuinely portable, free-standing panel that isn't fixed to the dwelling or site structure. A portable ground-mounted or A-frame panel sitting in your own yard area, connected via a standard socket, is far less likely to raise a flag than anything bolted to a roof.

## What actually works well on a permanent site

- **A 400-800W portable panel array on a ground frame or A-frame stand**, positioned in the site's own yard space rather than mounted to the dwelling
- **A grid-tied microinverter feeding back through a standard outlet**, where park rules permit it — this is the closest equivalent to the UK/EU plug-in solar model, though always confirm with park management first given the AS/NZS 4777 compliance questions covered in our [CEC-approved inverters guide](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-cec-approved-inverters-au)
- **A portable power station charged by a standalone panel**, which sidesteps grid-tie questions entirely and works for residents in parks where fixed electrical modifications aren't permitted at all

Our [tiny homes guide](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-tiny-homes-australia) covers a very similar decision tree for small, fixed-footprint dwellings, and is worth reading alongside this one — a permanent park home shares more in common with a tiny home electrically than with a touring caravan.

## Security and theft in a shared park environment

Caravan parks are shared living environments with higher foot traffic than a typical suburban street, which raises the stakes on securing any portable equipment left outdoors. Our [theft and security guide](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-theft-security-australia) covers practical steps — locking cable connections, marking equipment, and where possible keeping panels within sight of the dwelling rather than at the yard's edge — that are worth taking more seriously in a park setting than they might be in a fenced suburban backyard.

## Heat, storms, and site exposure

Permanent sites are often more exposed than a standard suburban block, with fewer established trees and buildings providing wind shelter, particularly in newer residential parks. Secure A-frame mounts properly and bring portable panels in during severe weather warnings — the same principle covered in our [bushfire resilience guide](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-bushfire-resilience-au) for a different hazard type applies broadly: portable gear needs an actual storage plan, not just a storage intention.

## Bottom line

Permanent caravan park living sits in an odd gap between "renter" and "homeowner" content, and most portable solar guides default to the touring use case instead. The financial case is often stronger than average thanks to above-retail park power rates, but the park agreement — not the technology — is almost always the real gatekeeper. Get that sorted first, then a modest, free-standing portable setup is usually the safest and most flexible way in.

### Can I install solar panels on my caravan park site?

It depends entirely on your park's site agreement. Many parks restrict structural changes to dwellings and may require disclosure or approval of anything connected to site power. A free-standing, portable panel not fixed to the dwelling is generally the lowest-friction option — confirm with park management in writing before buying anything more permanent.

### Is plug-in solar worth it on a permanent caravan park site?

Often more so than for an average homeowner, because many parks charge above-retail rates for on-sold power. Every kWh of daytime self-generated use avoids that higher rate, which can shorten payback noticeably compared with standard suburban electricity pricing.

### What's the difference between this and a Grey Nomad touring setup?

A touring setup prioritises mobility and short off-grid stretches between powered sites. A permanent resident's site doesn't move and already has metered mains power, so the better comparison is a small fixed dwelling — the goal is ongoing bill reduction, not independence from the grid.
