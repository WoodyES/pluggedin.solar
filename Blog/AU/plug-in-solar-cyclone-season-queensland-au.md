The Australian cyclone season runs officially from 1 November to 30 April, and for households across North and Central Queensland — Cairns, Townsville, Mackay, Rockhampton, and everywhere in between — that's six months of watching the Bureau of Meteorology and keeping the emergency kit topped up. Grid outages following a cyclone can run anywhere from a few hours to over a week in badly affected areas, and portable solar paired with a battery has become a genuinely practical part of that preparation, alongside (not instead of) the standard evacuation and emergency-kit planning.

## What a plug-in setup can and can't do

Be clear-eyed about the limits before you buy anything. **A grid-tied plug-in solar kit cannot power your home during a grid outage.** AS/NZS 4777-compliant inverters are required to disconnect from the grid the moment it goes down — this is a safety feature that protects line workers repairing storm damage, not a flaw. If you want power during an outage, you need a setup that works independently of the grid: a portable power station charged by a standalone solar panel, not a grid-tied microinverter kit.

For the regulatory detail on why grid-tied plug-in kits work the way they do, our [AS/NZS 4777 in plain English](/au/blog/as-nzs-4777-plain-english-plug-in-solar-au) explainer covers the standard. For cyclone resilience specifically, you want the off-grid, standalone portable route, which our [CEC-approved inverters guide](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-cec-approved-inverters-au) and [off-grid remote properties guide](/au/blog/off-grid-solar-remote-au-properties) both cover in more depth.

## The practical cyclone-resilience setup

- **400-800W of foldable or rigid solar panels**, stored indoors or in a secured shed until the cyclone threat has passed, then deployed post-landfall
- **A 1-2kWh LiFePO4 portable power station** with MC4 solar input
- **A spare solar charge cable** — buy two; the first one to fail always does so at the worst possible time
- **A battery-powered or hand-crank radio** as a non-negotiable backup, independent of the main power station

Total cost is typically $800-1,600, well below a hardwired backup system, and it covers the loads that matter most in a multi-day outage: fridge, phone charging, lighting, a fan, and medical devices like CPAP machines.

## Sizing for a realistic outage

| Load | Wh/day |
|---|---|
| Fridge/freezer (cycling average) | 1,000 |
| Lighting | 150 |
| Phone/laptop charging | 200 |
| Fan (Queensland heat makes this essential) | 400 |
| CPAP if applicable | 250 |
| Radio/comms | 50 |
| **Total** | **~2,050 Wh/day** |

A 2kWh battery covers roughly a day on stored charge alone. A 400W panel in Queensland's strong post-cyclone sun (skies often clear fast once the system passes) can recharge close to that full amount in a single good day, meaning essentials can run indefinitely once the immediate storm threat clears — the main vulnerability is the 12-24 hours of the cyclone itself and its immediate aftermath, when skies are too overcast or panels are stowed for safety.

## Before, during, and after the cyclone

**Before (when a watch or warning is issued):**
- Fully charge the power station from the grid — don't rely on solar alone in the final hours
- Bring panels indoors or into a secured, non-flooding location
- Charge all phones, torches, and backup battery packs fully

**During:**
- Keep the power station itself away from windows and in the most structurally sound room available
- Do not deploy panels outdoors during active cyclonic winds — airborne panels become projectiles and a serious hazard to people and property

**After:**
- Wait for an "all clear" before redeploying panels — secondary wind gusts and debris are common even after the eye passes
- Check panels and cables for storm damage before powering on
- Prioritise fridge and medical loads over convenience loads like device charging for the first 24-48 hours if the outage runs long

## Heat changes the equation too

North Queensland cyclone season overlaps entirely with the hottest, most humid part of the year. Panels lose some efficiency in extreme heat (silicon panels typically lose output as cell temperature climbs well above 25°C), which our dedicated piece on the [temperature coefficient and Queensland heat](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-queensland-heat-temperature-coefficient-au) covers in detail. Factor a small performance discount into your sizing rather than assuming nameplate output on a 35°C-plus day.

### Can plug-in solar keep my fridge running if the grid goes down in a cyclone?

Not a grid-tied kit on its own — AS/NZS 4777 requires those to disconnect during an outage. But a standalone panel charging a portable power station works independently of the grid and can run a fridge, lights, and phone charging for days, recharging from solar once skies clear.

### When should I bring my solar panels inside before a cyclone?

As soon as a cyclone watch or warning is issued for your area, well before winds pick up. Panels left outdoors during active cyclonic winds can become dangerous flying debris, and most emergency guidance recommends securing or stowing all loose outdoor items at the watch stage, not the warning stage.

### Does Queensland heat reduce solar output during cyclone season?

Yes, modestly. Solar panels lose some efficiency as cell temperature rises well above 25°C, which is common through the Queensland build-up and cyclone season. It's a real but manageable effect — factor roughly a 10-15% reduction into your outage-sizing calculations on very hot days rather than assuming full nameplate output.
