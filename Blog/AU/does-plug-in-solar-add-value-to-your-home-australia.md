# Does Plug-in Solar Add Value to Your Home in Australia?

Rooftop solar has a well-documented effect on Australian property values — CoreLogic and various state valuer-general studies have put the uplift at anywhere from 1-4% depending on system size and location. Naturally, anyone buying a plug-in or portable solar kit wants to know whether the same logic applies. The honest answer is: not in the same way, and understanding why matters before you spend money expecting a resale bump.

## Why plug-in solar is valued differently to rooftop systems

The value uplift from rooftop solar comes largely from its permanence and its paperwork trail. A CEC-approved grid-tied system installed under [AS/NZS 4777](/au/blog/as-nzs-4777-plain-english-plug-in-solar-au) by a licensed electrician is registered, inspected, and comes with a Certificate of Compliance that a buyer's conveyancer or building inspector can actually verify. It's a fixed improvement to the property, like a renovated kitchen — it stays when you sell, and it's listed as an asset in the contract of sale.

Plug-in solar kits don't work that way. As we cover in our guide to [CEC-approved inverters](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-cec-approved-inverters-au), portable plug-in setups generally sit outside the formal grid-connection framework altogether — they're not CEC-registered, not inspected, and in most states they're designed for off-grid or local-load use rather than formal export. That means they don't show up on any register a valuer or buyer's agent would check, and they're legally a portable chattel rather than a fixture — the same category as a free-standing air conditioner or a garden shed you bought at Bunnings.

## What this actually means for a sale

In practice, a plug-in solar setup is far more likely to be treated the way whitegoods are treated in an Australian sale contract: something you can take with you, or something you can offer to leave behind as a minor inclusion, but not something a valuer will add a line item for. If you're selling and want to capture some value from your kit, the realistic options are:

- **Take it with you.** Since it's portable by design — this is the whole point covered in our [off-grid solar guide](/au/blog/off-grid-solar-australia-guide) — you can simply unplug it and move it to your next property.
- **List it as an inclusion.** Mention it in the listing as a value-add (similar to including a dishwasher or garden furniture) rather than expecting it to move the appraised value.
- **Don't expect it to influence the bank valuation.** Lenders' valuers work from fixtures and comparable sales, not portable equipment sitting in the yard.

## Where it does help: buyer perception, not valuation

There's a real difference between formal valuation and what actually moves a buyer during an open house. Energy costs are a genuine concern for Australian buyers right now, and a visibly functioning solar setup — even a portable one — signals that a property has lower running costs. That's a soft selling point, not a hard valuation line, but agents in high-energy-cost states (see our [Queensland heat guide](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-queensland-heat-temperature-coefficient-au) and [NSW Solar Sponge piece](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-nsw-solar-sponge-au)) report that buyers increasingly ask about energy setup during inspections.

If you're a landlord rather than an owner-occupier, the calculation is different again — see our [landlords guide](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-landlords-australia) for how a plug-in kit can be framed as a tenant amenity rather than a capital improvement.

## Rooftop solar remains the stronger long-term value play

If building equity through solar is actually your goal — rather than just cutting your own power bill in the short term — a full CEC-approved rooftop installation remains the better financial decision for a property you plan to sell. ### Will a plug-in solar kit increase my home's valuation?

Not directly — plug-in kits aren't CEC-registered or treated as fixtures, so bank and valuer-general valuations won't add a line item for one the way they would for a registered rooftop system.

### Should I leave my plug-in solar kit behind when I sell?

It's optional. Since it's portable, most sellers take it with them, though offering it as an inclusion (like a garden shed or whitegoods) can be a minor selling point during negotiations.

### Does rooftop solar add more resale value than a plug-in kit?

Yes — CEC-approved rooftop systems are registered, inspected, and treated as permanent fixtures, which is why they show a measurable valuation uplift that portable plug-in kits generally don't.
