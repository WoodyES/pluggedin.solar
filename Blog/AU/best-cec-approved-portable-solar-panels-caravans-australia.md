Portable solar is one of the few corners of the Australian solar market where the rules are genuinely simple: take it with you, plug it into your caravan's battery system or a portable power station, and there's no grid connection, no inverter compliance paperwork, and no installer required. But "CEC-approved" still comes up constantly in forums and retailer listings, and it means something different here than it does for a rooftop system — this guide covers what actually matters when you're choosing a portable panel for caravanning, camping, or a Grey Nomad trip.

## What CEC approval actually covers

The Clean Energy Council maintains an approved products list primarily for grid-connected inverters and panels used in rebate-eligible rooftop installations under [AS/NZS 4777](/au/blog/as-nzs-4777-plain-english-plug-in-solar-au). A portable panel charging a caravan battery or a power station through a DC connection sits entirely outside that framework — there's no grid interaction, so there's no compliance requirement in the way our [CEC-approved inverters guide](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-cec-approved-inverters-au) describes for actual plug-in microgenerator kits. That said, reputable portable panel brands still use cells and components that meet recognised standards, and buying from a brand with a genuine Australian warranty and support network is the practical substitute for a formal compliance list when you're off-grid.

## Folding panels vs. fixed rigid panels for caravans

Most caravan and camping setups use one of two formats. Folding/briefcase-style panels (typically 120W–200W per panel) pack flat for storage and are the standard choice for [Grey Nomad](/au/blog/camping-caravan-plug-in-solar-grey-nomad-au) trips where you're setting up and breaking down a site regularly. Fixed rigid panels mounted permanently on a caravan roof suit people doing longer stints at one site or wanting passive charging while driving. Our [portable solar panels guide](/au/blog/best-portable-solar-panels-australia) compares both formats in more detail, including weight and durability trade-offs that matter more on corrugated outback roads than they do in a suburban backyard.

## Matching panel wattage to your setup

For a weekend away with lights, a fridge, and phone charging, a single 120–200W folding panel paired with a decent portable power station is usually enough. For longer off-grid stints — the kind covered in our [off-grid remote properties guide](/au/blog/off-grid-solar-remote-au-properties) and relevant to extended caravan touring in the same way — stepping up to 400W or running two panels in parallel gives meaningfully more buffer on overcast days. [Queensland's heat](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-queensland-heat-temperature-coefficient-au) and much of the country's high UV actually works in your favour here: panel temperature coefficients matter less for short-duration portable use than they do for a fixed rooftop system running all day.

## Pairing with a portable power station

A folding panel is only half the setup — most caravan and camping users pair theirs with a portable power station or their van's existing battery management system. Our [EcoFlow Australia review](/au/blog/ecoflow-australia-review) and [Jackery vs EcoFlow comparison](/au/blog/jackery-vs-ecoflow-australia) both cover the leading options for pairing with a portable panel, and the [best portable power stations](/au/blog/best-portable-power-stations-australia) roundup is worth checking before committing to a brand, since compatible charge controllers and connector types vary between manufacturers.

## What to check before buying

- **Connector compatibility** — confirm the panel's output connector (commonly Anderson or MC4) matches your power station or battery system before you buy, or budget for an adapter
- **IP rating** — look for at least IP65 given exposure to dust and rain on outback trips; our [portable solar accessories guide](/au/blog/best-portable-solar-accessories-australia) covers protective cases and cable options
- **Weight and fold size** — matters more for 4WD and remote touring setups with limited storage than for caravans with dedicated bays
- **Real Australian warranty support** — a panel that's cheap upfront but has no local service network can be a false economy if a cell fails mid-trip

## Why this is different from a rooftop or plug-in system

Unlike a grid-tied [plug-in microgenerator kit](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-australia-complete-guide), which currently [sits outside Australia's compliance framework entirely](/au/blog/is-plug-in-solar-legal-australia) and needs a licensed electrician and CEC-approved inverter for any grid-connected use, a portable panel charging a battery is unambiguously legal everywhere in the country with zero paperwork. That's precisely why portable and off-grid setups — not grid-tied plug-in kits — remain the practical mainstream option for Australian buyers wanting solar they can actually use today.

### Do portable solar panels for caravans need CEC approval?

No. CEC approval applies to grid-connected inverters and panels used in rebate-eligible installations under AS/NZS 4777. A portable panel charging a battery or power station has no grid connection, so it falls outside that compliance framework entirely.

### What size portable panel do I need for a caravan?

A single 120–200W folding panel suits short trips with modest power needs (lights, fridge, charging). For longer off-grid stints, 400W or more — often as two panels in parallel — gives a meaningful buffer against cloudy days.

### Can I use a portable solar panel at a caravan park with mains power?

Yes, there's no restriction on using a portable panel alongside mains power at a powered site — many Grey Nomads use theirs to top up a battery bank or extend run time between charges, with no compliance or approval requirements either way.
