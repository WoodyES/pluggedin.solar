Plug-in solar kits are marketed overseas as something you plug into a wall socket and walk away from. In Australia, that framing doesn't hold up, and it's the single most common source of confusion we hear from readers. The honest answer is split: physically setting up a portable panel and power station is genuinely DIY, but connecting any inverter to your home's wiring to feed the grid is not, and conflating the two is how people end up with a non-compliant or illegal setup. This article draws that line clearly.

## What's genuinely DIY in Australia

Standalone portable power stations — the battery-plus-panel kits popular for camping, [grey nomad touring](/au/blog/camping-caravan-plug-in-solar-grey-nomad-au) and [off-grid use](/au/blog/off-grid-solar-australia-guide) — are entirely DIY. You unfold the panel, point it at the sun, and it charges a battery that you then draw from via its own output sockets. Nothing here touches your home's electrical wiring or the grid, so there's no licensing requirement, no CEC approval needed, and no inspection. This is the setup most Australians buying a "plug-in solar kit" from a camping retailer actually end up with, and it's legal everywhere in the country without qualification.

## Where it stops being DIY

The UK- and Europe-style plug-in microgenerator — a small grid-tied inverter that plugs into a household power point and feeds solar generation back into your home's wiring — is a different animal under Australian law, and this is where [AS/NZS 4777](/au/blog/as-nzs-4777-plain-english-plug-in-solar-au) comes in. Any inverter that exports power into your switchboard, even through an ordinary wall socket rather than a hardwired connection, falls under grid-connection rules that require a [CEC-approved inverter](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-cec-approved-inverters-au) and installation or sign-off by a licensed electrician. This isn't a grey area the way it is in some other markets — unlicensed grid-tied work is a safety and insurance issue, and it's why [plug-in solar in the UK/EU sense isn't broadly legal in Australia yet](/au/blog/is-plug-in-solar-legal-australia).

## Why the rule exists

Australia's grid-connection standards are stricter than most comparable countries partly because of voltage and frequency characteristics specific to the local grid, and partly because unlicensed DIY grid-tied work has caused real safety incidents here in the past — fires, shock hazards from incorrectly isolated circuits, and inverters that don't disconnect properly during a blackout, which is dangerous for utility workers restoring power. A licensed electrician isn't just a compliance checkbox; they verify earthing, isolation, and that the inverter will actually shut down safely if the grid goes down, none of which a portable kit buyer can verify themselves.

## What this means if you're buying

If your goal is to offset household grid electricity by feeding solar generation back through a power point, that path currently requires a CEC-approved inverter and a licensed electrician, the same as any other grid-tied solar installation — there's no cheaper DIY shortcut that's also legal. If your goal is simply to run specific loads — a fridge, lighting, device charging, a caravan's 12V system — off solar without touching the grid at all, a portable power station kit does that job entirely within DIY territory today, and for many households it covers the same practical use case (keeping essentials running, cutting peak-time draw) without any of the compliance overhead.

## Importing kits doesn't change the rule

Some buyers look at overseas plug-in microgenerator kits and consider importing one directly, reasoning that Australian retailers simply haven't caught up yet. ### Can I install a grid-tied solar inverter myself in Australia?

No. Any inverter that exports power into your home's wiring, including through an ordinary power point, must be CEC-approved and connected or signed off by a licensed electrician under AS/NZS 4777. This applies regardless of how the kit is marketed or where it was purchased.

### Is a portable power station with a solar panel legal to set up myself?

Yes. A standalone power station that charges from its own panel and supplies power through its own output sockets doesn't touch the grid or your home wiring, so it carries no licensing requirement and is legal to set up yourself anywhere in Australia.

### Does buying an imported plug-in solar kit avoid the electrician requirement?

No. The legal requirement is based on what the inverter does — whether it exports to the grid — not on where it was bought. An imported grid-tied inverter still needs AS/NZS 4777 compliance and licensed electrician installation to be used legally.
