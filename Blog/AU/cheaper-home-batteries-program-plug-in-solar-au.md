Australia's federal **Cheaper Home Batteries Program** has been running since mid-2025, knocking roughly 30% off the upfront cost of a home battery system through a certificate-based subsidy administered alongside the existing small-scale renewable energy scheme. For households weighing up a full rooftop-plus-battery installation against a smaller, portable plug-in setup, it raises an obvious question: does the rebate change the maths for plug-in solar too?

The short answer is no — but understanding exactly why matters, because it clarifies what plug-in and portable solar owners should actually be comparing themselves against.

## What the rebate actually covers

The Cheaper Home Batteries Program applies to **fixed, grid-connected battery systems** installed by a licensed provider, typically paired with an existing or new [CEC-approved rooftop solar system](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-cec-approved-inverters-au). Eligible batteries generally sit in the 5–100 kWh range, need to be new, listed on the Clean Energy Council's approved product list, and installed under [AS/NZS 4777](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-cec-approved-inverters-au) by an accredited installer. The subsidy is delivered as a discount at point of sale, calculated per kWh of usable capacity.

None of that describes a portable power station bought off a shelf. A plug-in or portable solar-plus-battery kit — the kind covered throughout this site — isn't installed by a licensed electrician, isn't wired into switchboard-level grid export, and isn't the kind of product the scheme's certificate system was built to subsidise. If you're comparing quotes for a full rooftop-and-battery system against a $600 portable power station, the rebate is a real reason the former looks more attractive than it used to — but it's not a discount you can claim on the latter.

## Why this still matters for plug-in solar buyers

Even though the rebate doesn't apply directly, it changes the environment plug-in solar competes in three ways:

**1. It makes fixed battery systems more price-competitive.** A household on the fence between a portable panel-and-battery setup and a proper CEC-compliant installation may now find the full system cheaper than expected, especially in states that stack the federal rebate with their own scheme (South Australia's Home Battery Scheme history is a good example of this stacking effect).

**2. It's accelerating time-of-use and Solar Sponge-style tariffs.** As more fixed batteries connect to the grid, retailers are leaning harder into tariffs like the [NSW Solar Sponge](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-nsw-solar-sponge-au) that reward shifting consumption to the middle of the day. A portable battery charged from a plug-in panel benefits from exactly the same tariff structures, even without the rebate.

**3. It raises the bar on "is it worth it."** With whole-home batteries getting cheaper, the pitch for a portable setup increasingly rests on flexibility and low upfront cost rather than raw economics — see our [battery vs no-battery comparison](/au/blog/battery-storage-vs-no-battery-plug-in-solar-australia) for how the numbers stack up without a subsidy in play.

## Who should still consider a portable setup

The Cheaper Home Batteries Program mostly benefits homeowners who already qualify for a full rooftop installation — owner-occupiers with suitable roof space and the cash flow to cover the remaining 70% of a fixed system. It does nothing for renters, apartment dwellers, or anyone in a property unsuitable for a permanent install, which is most of the audience [portable and off-grid solar](/au/blog/off-grid-solar-australia-guide) actually serves. If a licensed, grid-connected installation [isn't legally available to you](/au/blog/is-plug-in-solar-legal-australia) or isn't financially sensible for your situation, a portable panel-and-battery combo remains the practical route regardless of what federal rebates apply to fixed systems.

Check current [battery pricing](/au/blog/solar-battery-cost-australia-2026) and [feed-in tariff rates](/au/blog/solar-feed-in-tariff-australia-2026) in your state before deciding which path makes more sense for your household.

### Can I use the Cheaper Home Batteries Program rebate on a portable power station?

No. The scheme is restricted to fixed, CEC-listed battery systems installed by an accredited provider under AS/NZS 4777 — portable power stations and plug-in kits fall outside its eligibility criteria.

### Does the battery rebate mean plug-in solar is no longer worth it in Australia?

Not necessarily. It narrows the price gap for households who could already afford and qualify for a full installation, but it doesn't change the calculation for renters, apartment residents, or anyone without a suitable roof — where portable options remain the only realistic route to any solar savings.

### Will state-based battery rebates stack with the federal program?

Some states have run their own battery incentives alongside the federal scheme in the past, though availability and rules change frequently — check your state's current energy department page before assuming a specific rebate still applies.
