Searches for "solar battery Australia" increasingly surface two very different products side by side: a portable plug-in solar panel costing a few hundred dollars, and a Tesla Powerwall costing well into five figures installed. They get compared because both involve solar and storage, but in practice they serve almost entirely different households and different problems.

This guide lays out what each one actually does in the Australian context, where AS/NZS 4777 compliance requirements fundamentally separate the two categories.

## The regulatory line is the real difference

In Australia, any inverter feeding solar generation back into your home's wiring and the grid must be [CEC-approved and compliant with AS/NZS 4777](/au/blog/as-nzs-4777-plain-english-plug-in-solar-au), installed by a licensed electrician. A Tesla Powerwall, paired with a rooftop solar array, falls squarely inside this framework — it's a grid-connected system requiring full compliance, inspection, and typically a Clean Energy Council-accredited installer.

Plug-in solar kits, by contrast, generally sit **outside** the grid-connected framework altogether in Australia. Unlike the UK, Australia has no legal pathway for a consumer to simply plug a mains-wired solar panel into a household power point — doing so would mean backfeeding an uncertified inverter into household wiring, which isn't compliant. That's why the practical plug-in solar route in Australia is portable and off-grid: a panel charging a standalone battery power station (EcoFlow, Jackery, Anker) that you then draw from using the power station's own outlets, never backfeeding into your switchboard. Our [guide to why Australia doesn't yet allow true plug-in solar](/au/blog/why-australia-doesnt-allow-plug-in-solar-yet) covers this distinction in more depth.

## Cost and scope comparison

| | Portable plug-in + battery kit | Tesla Powerwall (with rooftop solar) |
|---|---|---|
| Typical cost | A$600–A$1,800 | A$14,000–A$20,000+ installed |
| Installation | DIY, no electrician | CEC-accredited installer required |
| Grid-connected | No — standalone/off-grid only | Yes |
| Annual saving | A$150–A$350 | A$800–A$1,800+ |
| Blackout backup | Yes, for whatever's plugged into it | Yes, whole-home or selected circuits |
| Compliance framework | None required (off-grid use) | Full AS/NZS 4777 + CEC |

The gap in both cost and scope is enormous, which is really the point — these aren't competing purchases for the same household budget. A portable kit is an entry-level, low-commitment way to offset some daytime load or have backup power for essentials. A Powerwall is a serious infrastructure investment tied to a full rooftop solar system.

## Can you run both?

Plenty of Australian households do, for different parts of the property. A rooftop-plus-Powerwall system covers the main house, while a separate portable kit powers a shed, a caravan on the driveway, or serves as standalone blackout backup that doesn't depend on the home's main system being operational. Because the portable kit never touches the switchboard, there's no compliance overlap or conflict between the two — they're simply independent systems.

For most first-time buyers without an existing solar array, the sensible starting point is a portable kit: it's a fraction of the cost, requires no electrician, and gives you a real sense of how much a bit of extra solar generation actually changes your bills before deciding whether a full rooftop-and-Powerwall investment makes sense. ## FAQ

### Is it legal to plug a solar panel directly into a wall socket in Australia?

No — unlike the UK, Australia has no compliant pathway for backfeeding solar generation into household wiring through a standard power point. Any grid-connected inverter must be CEC-approved and installed by a licensed electrician under AS/NZS 4777. The compliant portable route is a panel charging a standalone battery power station, with power drawn from the station's own outlets rather than fed into the switchboard.

### Does a portable solar kit reduce my electricity bill the way a Powerwall does?

Only indirectly — it reduces the load you'd otherwise draw from devices plugged directly into the power station, rather than offsetting your whole-home meter reading the way a grid-connected system with a Powerwall does. The savings are real but smaller in scale, typically a few hundred dollars a year rather than close to a thousand or more.

### Which is the better first step if I'm new to solar?

A portable plug-in and battery kit, for most households. It costs a fraction of a full system, needs no electrician or CEC paperwork, and lets you learn how much solar generation actually changes your energy habits before committing to a five-figure rooftop-and-Powerwall installation.
