## Why Australians Are Looking Overseas for Plug-In Kits

Unlike the UK, where plug-in "microgenerator" kits became legal under [SI 2026 No. 848](/au/blog/why-australia-doesnt-allow-plug-in-solar-yet) and are now sold in mainstream retailers, Australia hasn't created an equivalent simplified category for grid-connected plug-in solar. Any inverter feeding power into a household circuit here still needs to meet [AS/NZS 4777](/au/blog/as-nzs-4777-plain-english-plug-in-solar-au) and be installed by a licensed electrician with a CEC-approved inverter — there's no DIY plug-and-play exemption like the UK's 800VA rule.

That gap is exactly why a steady stream of Australians look at UK and US retailers selling EcoFlow STREAM kits, Anker SOLIX balcony systems, and similar products, and wonder whether they can just order one and have it shipped. The short answer: you can import the hardware, but using it as a grid-tied plug-in system the way UK or US buyers do is not legal here without an AS/NZS 4777-compliant inverter and proper installation. This guide covers what importing actually involves if you still want the hardware for an **off-grid or portable use case**.

## What You Can Legally Do With an Imported Kit

The practical, legal path for most imported plug-in kits in Australia is to run them as a **portable power station charging setup** rather than a grid-tied system:

- Pair the solar panels with a portable battery/power station (EcoFlow DELTA, Anker SOLIX, Jackery) via DC input — this never touches your household wiring and needs no electrician or compliance certificate
- Use the setup for camping, caravans, sheds, or as blackout backup — see our [Grey Nomad camping guide](/au/blog/camping-caravan-plug-in-solar-grey-nomad-au) for how this works in practice
- Avoid plugging any imported microinverter directly into a wall outlet to feed the grid — this is the part that's illegal in Australia regardless of where the hardware came from

If your goal is genuinely to offset household grid consumption, the legal route is a small grid-tied system installed by a licensed electrician with a [CEC-approved inverter](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-cec-approved-inverters-au) — our [off-grid solar guide](/au/blog/off-grid-solar-australia-guide) and [complete AU guide](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-australia-complete-guide) cover both paths in more detail.

## Customs Duty and GST

Solar panels and inverters imported into Australia for personal use are generally subject to:

- **GST (10%)** on the declared value of the goods plus shipping and insurance, charged at the border for consignments over AUD $1,000, or collected at checkout by the overseas retailer for lower-value consignments under the GST-on-low-value-imports rules
- **Customs duty** — most solar panels and inverters fall under tariff concessions that mean little or no duty applies, but battery packs (lithium-ion) can attract different treatment, so check the current Australian Border Force tariff classification before ordering
- **Biosecurity/import permit checks** rarely apply to solar hardware itself, but packaging materials (timber crates, certain packing fill) can occasionally trigger inspection

A $799 EcoFlow STREAM kit shipped from the UK will typically land with an extra 10% GST plus shipping, pushing the real cost closer to AUD $1,300-$1,500 once currency conversion and freight are factored in — often more expensive than buying an equivalent portable power station and panel combo already sold within Australia.

## Voltage and Plug Differences

This is the detail that catches most importers out. UK and European kits are designed around 230V/50Hz and BS 1363 or Europlug connectors; US kits are designed around 120V/60Hz with NEMA plugs. Australia runs **230V/50Hz with Type I (AS/NZS 3112) plugs** — close to UK voltage but a different plug standard entirely, and US kits are the wrong voltage altogether for anything beyond battery charging via DC.

A UK-spec EcoFlow STREAM microinverter is electrically close to Australian mains voltage, but the microinverter's safety certification (G98/G99, the UK/EU grid code) is not equivalent to Australia's AS/NZS 4777.2, and the unit won't carry CEC approval. Even if you fit an adaptor plug, using it as a grid-tied device would not be compliant or insurable. A US kit is simply the wrong voltage for Australian mains and would need a step-up transformer to run even as a standalone load, let alone a grid-feed device.

## Warranty and Support Risk

Most manufacturers' warranties are region-locked or require return shipping to the country of purchase, which can make a battery or inverter fault expensive and slow to resolve on an imported unit. Freight-damaged panels are a common and costly issue, since standard courier insurance rarely covers the full replacement value of solar hardware. For anyone relying on the system daily (off-grid cabins, caravans), buying locally — even at a higher sticker price — usually works out cheaper once you account for warranty risk, as covered in our [off-grid remote properties guide](/au/blog/off-grid-solar-remote-au-properties).

## Is It Worth Importing?

For a **portable, off-grid use case** — charging a power station for camping, blackout backup, or a shed — importing can occasionally make sense if a specific panel or battery model genuinely isn't sold in Australia. But for anything you want **grid-tied** (actually offsetting your household bill), importing doesn't solve the underlying legal and compliance gap — see [why Australia doesn't allow plug-in solar yet](/au/blog/why-australia-doesnt-allow-plug-in-solar-yet) for the regulatory reasons this hasn't changed. In most cases, buying a locally-sold portable kit or booking a licensed installer for a small CEC-approved grid-tied system will cost less and cause fewer headaches than importing.

### Can I legally plug an imported solar microinverter into an Australian wall socket?

No. Any inverter feeding power back into household wiring in Australia must be CEC-approved and comply with AS/NZS 4777, installed by a licensed electrician. An imported UK or US microinverter does not carry this certification and using it this way breaches electrical safety law and will void home insurance.

### How much GST will I pay on an imported solar kit?

10% GST applies to most imported solar hardware, charged either at checkout by the overseas retailer (for consignments under AUD $1,000) or at the border by customs for larger shipments, on top of the item price, shipping and insurance.

### What's the legal alternative to an imported plug-in kit in Australia?

Pair imported or local solar panels with a portable power station via DC charging (no compliance issue, works for camping/off-grid), or install a proper CEC-approved grid-tied inverter through a licensed electrician if you want to offset household grid usage.
