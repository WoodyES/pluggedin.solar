Solar warranties in Australia are more layered than most buyers expect, and the gap between what a glossy brochure promises and what you're actually covered for tends to show up exactly when you least want it to — after hail damage, after a heatwave, or three years in when output has quietly dropped. Understanding the layers before you buy is the difference between a straightforward claim and a months-long argument with an installer who's since gone out of business.

## The three layers of a full-system warranty

A grid-connected rooftop system carries three separate, stackable warranties, each covering something different:

- **Product warranty** — covers manufacturing defects in the panel itself (delamination, cell cracking, junction box failure). Typically 10–25 years depending on brand and tier.
- **Performance warranty** — guarantees the panel won't degrade in output faster than a specified curve, usually something like "at least 90% of rated output at year 10, at least 80% at year 25." This is separate from the product warranty and covers gradual efficiency loss, not defects.
- **Workmanship warranty** — covers the installer's work: mounting, wiring, sealing, and whether the system was installed correctly. This typically runs 5–10 years and comes from the installer or the installation company, not the panel manufacturer.

Inverters carry their own separate warranty, usually shorter than the panels — commonly 5–10 years, sometimes extendable — because inverters are the component most likely to fail first in a full system.

## Where plug-in and portable kits differ

Portable plug-in kits and power stations — the kind covered throughout this site — sit outside the CEC-approved framework that governs grid-connected installations, and their warranties look completely different from a rooftop system's. Expect:

- **1–5 years product warranty** on the panel or panel-and-battery kit as a whole, not the 10–25 year tiers of fixed rooftop panels
- **Cycle-based battery warranty** for the LiFePO4 cell inside most portable power stations — commonly rated to something like 3,000 cycles to 80% capacity, which in practice means years of daily use rather than a fixed calendar term
- **No separate workmanship warranty**, since there's no licensed installer involved — you're the installer, and any mounting or wiring you do yourself isn't covered by anyone

This shorter warranty window is one of the trade-offs of portability and DIY setup against a full CEC-compliant install — see our [off-grid setup guide](/au/blog/off-grid-solar-australia-guide) for how the two approaches compare more broadly.

## Australian Consumer Law sits above all of it

Regardless of what a manufacturer's warranty document says, Australian Consumer Law (ACL) guarantees apply automatically to anything sold in Australia. Goods must be of acceptable quality and fit for the purpose sold, and must last a reasonable time given the price paid and how the product is described. This means a panel that fails well within what a "reasonable" buyer would expect it to last can potentially be pursued under ACL even if the printed warranty period has technically lapsed — though in practice, sticking to the stated warranty window makes any claim far more straightforward.

Importantly, ACL rights can't be excluded or reduced by a manufacturer's terms and conditions, no matter what the fine print says. If a seller tells you "no refunds, no exceptions," that statement itself may be misleading conduct under the ACL.

## Why CEC approval still matters, even for warranty purposes

Panels and inverters on the Clean Energy Council's approved products list have already cleared a baseline of testing against Australian conditions — relevant given how hard the climate is on equipment, from Queensland heat (see our [heat performance guide](/au/blog/solar-panel-heat-performance-australia)) to hail in the southern states (covered in our [hail damage guide](/au/blog/solar-panel-hail-damage-australia)). Buying CEC-listed gear, even for a non-STC-eligible portable setup, gives you a stronger footing if a warranty dispute ever escalates, since the product has an established Australian compliance history rather than being an unlisted import with no local track record.

## Common warranty exclusions to check before you buy

- **Installation errors** — a manufacturer's product warranty almost never covers damage caused by incorrect mounting, wrong fuse rating, or DIY wiring mistakes
- **Environmental damage** — bird strike, falling branches, and most hail damage beyond a stated impact-resistance rating are typically excluded from product warranty and instead sit with home or contents insurance
- **Unauthorised modification** — opening a sealed battery pack or junction box, or using a non-approved charge controller, typically voids the relevant section of the warranty immediately
- **Corrosion in marine/coastal environments** — several manufacturers specifically exclude or limit coverage for salt-air corrosion unless the product is rated for coastal use

## Making a claim

Keep your proof of purchase, register the product with the manufacturer where a registration option exists (this sometimes extends the warranty term), and photograph the installation early so you have a clear "before" reference if something changes later. If your claim is with an installer who's since ceased trading, the workmanship warranty may be void in practice even though it's technically still "in force" — this is one of the strongest arguments for choosing an established installer in the first place, covered in our [choosing an installer guide](/au/blog/how-to-choose-solar-installer-australia).

### Does a 25-year performance warranty mean the panel is guaranteed to last 25 years?

Not exactly — it guarantees the panel won't degrade in output faster than the stated curve over that period, not that the physical hardware is fault-free for 25 years. That's what the separate, usually shorter, product warranty covers.

### Can a seller refuse a refund once the manufacturer's warranty period has ended?

Not automatically. Australian Consumer Law guarantees can extend beyond a manufacturer's stated warranty term if the product failed well before a "reasonable" buyer would expect, though claims get harder to argue the further past the stated period you go.

### Do portable plug-in kits need CEC-approved components to get warranty support?

No — CEC approval is primarily about eligibility for government incentive schemes like STCs, not a prerequisite for a manufacturer to honour its own warranty. That said, CEC-listed gear generally has a stronger local compliance and support history if something does go wrong.
