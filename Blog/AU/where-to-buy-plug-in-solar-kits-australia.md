## There's No "Plug-In Solar Aisle" in Australia — Yet

Unlike the UK, which legalised a specific category of plug-in microgenerator kit in 2026, Australia has no equivalent legal framework letting a panel-and-inverter setup feed straight into a wall socket. As our [legality explainer](/au/blog/is-plug-in-solar-legal-australia) covers in detail, any grid-connected inverter here needs to be [CEC-approved and installed under AS/NZS 4777](/au/blog/as-nzs-4777-plain-english-plug-in-solar-au) by a licensed electrician — there's no DIY, plug-and-go path to grid-tied solar the way there now is in Britain.

What Australians buy instead, in large numbers, is portable solar: panels paired with a standalone power station that charges from the sun and discharges through its own outlets, entirely independent of the household meter. That's legal to set up yourself with no electrician and no DNSP paperwork, and it's what most people searching for "plug-in solar" in Australia actually end up buying. The question is where.

## Bunnings: Good for Accessories, Patchy for Full Kits

Bunnings is the default stop for Australian households buying almost anything home-related, and its solar range has grown — entry-level folding panels, small portable power stations, and all the mounting brackets, extension leads and cable glands you'd need to set up a system are reliably in stock. Where Bunnings falls short is selection at the higher-wattage end: most stores carry only one or two power station brands, and stock of specific panel wattages (particularly 400W+ panels) can be inconsistent store to store. It's a solid choice for topping up accessories or buying a basic starter unit in person, less reliable if you've already decided on a specific model.

## Jaycar: Best for Components, Not Complete Kits

Jaycar is the go-to for anyone building or modifying a setup rather than buying one off the shelf — MC4 connectors, charge controllers, inverters, wiring and monitoring gear are all well stocked and reasonably priced. It's less useful if you want a complete, ready-to-use panel-and-power-station kit, since Jaycar's own-brand solar range is limited and mostly aimed at caravan and 12V enthusiasts rather than household balcony or garden setups. Worth a visit if you're after a specific component rather than a full system.

## Camping and 4WD Stores: Where Grey Nomads Actually Shop

BCF, Anaconda, Supercheap Auto and specialist caravan retailers carry a genuinely strong range of portable folding panels, often rated for the vibration and repeated setup/pack-down cycles that household kits aren't designed for. If your plug-in solar interest is driven by caravan, camper or [grey nomad travel](/au/blog/camping-caravan-plug-in-solar-grey-nomad-au) rather than a fixed home setup, these stores are usually a better match than Bunnings or a generic household kit, since the panels and cases are built for that use case specifically.

## Buying From Overseas Manufacturers Directly

A smaller number of buyers import kits directly from manufacturers overseas, chasing lower prices or specific models not sold through Australian retail channels. This can work, but it comes with real risk around warranty support, Australian plug and compliance standards, and freight damage with no local recourse. Our [importing plug-in solar kits guide](/au/blog/importing-plug-in-solar-kits-australia) covers what to check before going this route — for most households, the savings rarely justify the added risk and hassle compared to buying from an Australian-stocked retailer.

## What About Dedicated Solar Retailers

Dedicated solar and battery retailers exist in every state, but they're almost exclusively oriented toward full CEC-approved, AS/NZS 4777-compliant rooftop installations with a licensed electrician — not portable kits. If you're weighing a full grid-tied system against a cheaper portable setup, it's worth starting with our [CEC-approved inverters guide](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-cec-approved-inverters-au) to understand what that path actually involves before contacting an installer.

### Can I buy a grid-connected plug-in solar kit at Bunnings?

No — Bunnings sells portable panels and power stations, not grid-tied inverters. Any grid-connected system in Australia must use a CEC-approved inverter installed by a licensed electrician under AS/NZS 4777, which isn't a retail shelf product.

### Is it cheaper to import a plug-in solar kit from overseas?

Sometimes, but the savings often don't account for freight damage risk, lack of local warranty support, and plug or compliance mismatches. Buying from an Australian-stocked retailer like Amazon AU is usually the safer trade-off.

### Which retailer has the best range of portable solar kits in Australia?

Amazon AU generally offers the widest brand and wattage selection with the easiest side-by-side comparison, though Bunnings and camping-specific retailers like BCF are worth checking for in-person purchases or caravan-grade panels.
