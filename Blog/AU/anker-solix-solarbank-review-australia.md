Anker's SOLIX Solarbank range has built a strong reputation in Europe as a balcony-friendly solar storage system, pairing small, portable panels with a battery unit that stores generated power for use after dark. It's a genuinely clever product category, and Australian buyers researching portable or off-grid solar options keep asking whether it's worth importing or whether local alternatives make more sense. Here's an honest breakdown.

## What the Solarbank actually is

Unlike a typical plug-in solar kit, the SOLIX Solarbank isn't designed to feed power directly into a home circuit the way [EcoFlow's and Jackery's portable stations](/au/blog/jackery-vs-ecoflow-australia) do. It's built around a battery hub that stores solar input from small panels (commonly 400–800W depending on configuration) and discharges it later, either to run specific appliances or, in markets where it's legally supported, to offset grid draw through a smart plug. That distinction matters a lot in Australia, where the regulatory pathway for anything grid-interactive is stricter than in Europe.

## Where it fits against Australian rules

Australia's [AS/NZS 4777 standard](/au/blog/as-nzs-4777-plain-english-plug-in-solar-au) governs grid-connected inverters, and anything that exports power into household wiring needs a [CEC-approved inverter](/au/blog/plug-in-solar-cec-approved-inverters-au) installed by a licensed electrician. The Solarbank's battery-storage design sidesteps a lot of that: used purely to charge the battery from its own panels and discharge through a dedicated outlet or to run DC appliances directly, it functions more like a large portable power station than a grid-tied system, which is exactly the loophole that's kept [plug-in solar in a gray area in Australia](/au/blog/why-australia-doesnt-allow-plug-in-solar-yet) more broadly.

If you're hoping to use it to genuinely offset your main household circuit the way a compliant system does, you're back in CEC-inverter-and-licensed-electrician territory — the Solarbank doesn't change that equation, whatever marketing in other markets implies.

## How it compares to Jackery and EcoFlow

Our [Jackery vs EcoFlow comparison](/au/blog/jackery-vs-ecoflow-australia) covers the two brands Australian buyers reach for most often, and the honest answer is that the Solarbank occupies a slightly different niche than either. Jackery and EcoFlow portable power stations are built as general-purpose battery packs with solar charging as one input option among several (wall, car, generator). The Solarbank is built specifically around solar-to-storage, with less emphasis on being a general backup battery you'd also charge from the wall overnight.

If what you actually want is backup power for blackouts as the primary use case, our [best solar batteries for blackouts](/au/blog/best-solar-batteries-blackouts-australia) guide and the broader [best portable power stations](/au/blog/best-portable-power-stations-australia) roundup are more directly relevant than a solar-specific unit like the Solarbank. If solar charging genuinely is your primary use case — running it off-grid at a shed, cabin, or remote property — the Solarbank's purpose-built solar input and charge management make it a stronger fit.

## Where it makes the most sense in Australia

The clearest use case we'd recommend it for is off-grid or semi-off-grid: a granny flat, shed, caravan, or remote property where you're not trying to interact with the main grid connection at all. Our [off-grid solar guide](/au/blog/off-grid-solar-australia-guide) covers the broader category of equipment this fits into, and the Solarbank's compact, battery-centric design suits smaller off-grid loads (lighting, device charging, small appliances) better than it suits whole-property backup.

For apartment and balcony users specifically, it's worth being realistic about capacity. A single Solarbank unit with a standard panel set won't come close to meaningfully reducing a typical Australian household's daytime grid draw — it's a supplementary, self-consumption tool, not a replacement for a proper rooftop system.

## Pricing and import considerations

Pricing varies significantly depending on whether you're buying through official Australian retail channels or importing directly, and warranty support differs accordingly — a unit bought through an authorized Australian seller gives you local warranty recourse, while a grey import may not. If cost is the deciding factor, it's worth comparing against the broader [best portable solar panels](/au/blog/best-portable-solar-panels-australia) and power station roundups to see whether a cheaper, more established setup covers your actual use case just as well.

### Is the Anker SOLIX Solarbank legal to use in Australia?

Yes, as a standalone battery-storage and portable-charging device it doesn't require CEC approval or licensed installation, because it isn't exporting power into household wiring. If you try to wire it into your main circuit to offset grid consumption directly, you're back in grid-tied inverter territory and need a CEC-approved system installed by a licensed electrician.

### Is it better than EcoFlow or Jackery for an Australian buyer?

It depends on your use case. For pure off-grid solar charging and storage, the Solarbank's purpose-built design is a genuine advantage. For general backup power with multiple charging options (wall, car, solar), [EcoFlow and Jackery](/au/blog/jackery-vs-ecoflow-australia) remain the more flexible choice for most Australian households.

### Can I use the Solarbank to reduce my main electricity bill?

Only in a limited way — it can offset small, specific loads you plug directly into it, but it can't legally or practically replace the role of a CEC-approved, grid-tied system for whole-of-home savings. Treat it as a supplementary tool rather than a bill-reduction strategy on its own.
