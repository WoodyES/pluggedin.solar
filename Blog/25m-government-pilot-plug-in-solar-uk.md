## What Is the £25m Pilot?

On 21 April 2026, the UK government announced a **£25 million pilot programme** to provide plug-in solar kits to low-income households. The scheme aims to help those most affected by high energy costs benefit from the [legalisation of plug-in solar](/blog/plug-in-solar-legal-27-august-2026-uk) on 27 August 2026.

Minister for Energy Consumers **Martin McCluskey** described it as ensuring that plug-in solar "isn't just for those who can afford to buy a kit on day one."

## Who Is Expected to Qualify?

Full eligibility criteria haven't been published yet, but based on the announcement and existing government energy support schemes, likely qualifiers include:

- Households on **Universal Credit**, **Pension Credit**, or other means-tested benefits
- Households in **fuel poverty** (spending more than 10% of income on energy)
- [Council housing](/blog/plug-in-solar-council-housing-uk) and [housing association](/blog/plug-in-solar-housing-association-uk) tenants
- Residents of [supported and sheltered housing](/blog/plug-in-solar-supported-housing-uk)
- Households already receiving **Warm Home Discount** or **Winter Fuel Payments**

## How Will It Work?

The pilot is expected to:

1. **Fund the purchase of certified plug-in solar kits** — likely at no cost to qualifying households
2. **Provide installation support** — though plug-in solar doesn't require an electrician, some households may need help with mounting and setup
3. **Partner with approved retailers** — the government has briefed B&Q, Currys, Amazon, Lidl, Asda, Screwfix, and Wickes
4. **Cover [DNO notification](/blog/g98-dno-notification-step-by-step)** as part of the installation process

## How Much Could This Save?

For a household on the Ofgem cap rate (24.5p/kWh), an 800W plug-in solar kit can save **£100–£200+ per year** depending on location and self-consumption. Over 10 years, that's **£1,000–£2,000** in reduced energy bills.

For low-income households spending a disproportionate share of income on energy, this represents a significant and sustained reduction in costs.

## How to Apply

The application process hasn't been announced yet. Based on similar schemes (ECO4, Great British Insulation Scheme), applications will likely be available:

- **Online** via gov.uk or a dedicated portal
- **Through local authorities** who may administer the scheme regionally
- **Via referral** from energy suppliers or advice agencies

We'll update this article as soon as application details are published. In the meantime, check if you're eligible for other [plug-in solar grants and funding](/blog/plug-in-solar-grants-funding-uk-2026).

## What About the Devolved Nations?

The pilot applies to **England** initially. Scotland, Wales, and [Northern Ireland](/blog/plug-in-solar-northern-ireland-guide) may have separate schemes — the Scottish Government has previously run its own energy support programmes.

### Is the £25m enough?

At approximately £500–800 per kit including installation support, the £25m could fund 30,000–50,000 installations. The government has indicated this is a pilot, with potential expansion based on results.

### Can I get a kit if I already have rooftop solar?

This is unclear. The pilot is aimed at households without existing solar generation, but final eligibility criteria will determine this.

### What if I'm a private renter?

[Private renters](/blog/plug-in-solar-for-renters-uk) may qualify if they meet the income criteria. The portable nature of plug-in solar means you can take the kit when you move, which aligns with the government's intention to support transient households.
