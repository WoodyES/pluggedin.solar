The micro inverter is the most important component in any UK plug-in solar system. It sits between your panels and the wall socket, converting DC to grid-frequency AC, syncing with the grid, and enforcing every safety cutoff that keeps the system compliant with [SI 2026 No. 848](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk). It also determines whether your kit actually generates the wattage on the label — or leaves 5–10% on the table through poor MPPT tracking. This guide covers what a micro inverter does, the three brands worth considering in the UK, the models certified for the 800VA cap, and the price ranges you should expect in 2026.

## What a solar micro inverter actually does

Every certified micro inverter performs three critical jobs:

**DC-to-AC conversion.** Solar panels produce direct current. Your house runs on alternating current. The inverter makes the conversion, and its peak efficiency determines how much generated power reaches your sockets. Modern units convert at 96–97% efficiency. Cheap or older units may drop to 93–94% — losing 30–40 kWh a year on an 800W system.

**Grid synchronisation.** The inverter matches the grid's 50Hz frequency and 230V nominal voltage exactly. If the grid trips or voltage drifts outside the G98/G99 window, the inverter shuts off within a few hundred milliseconds. This is the anti-islanding requirement that every UK-certified inverter meets and is why plug-in solar cannot backfeed a dead grid.

**Maximum power point tracking (MPPT).** This is the clever bit. Panel output changes constantly as light, temperature and shading shift. MPPT continuously adjusts the electrical load on the panel to sit at the sweet spot on its I-V curve. Better MPPT means measurably more kWh in variable UK weather with passing clouds. The [self-consumption tips guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-self-consumption-tips-uk) explains why this matters for household savings.

The alternative to a micro inverter is a string inverter — one big central unit converting DC from all panels at once. Plug-in solar in the UK uses micros because they are small, panel-level, and cheap enough to build into an all-in-one kit.

## UK regulatory fit — what your micro inverter must do

For any UK plug-in installation from 27 August 2026 onwards, your micro inverter must:

- Cap output at **800VA / 3.5A** total, matching the [interim product specification](/blog/plug-in-solar-interim-product-specification-uk)
- Meet **G98 / G99** grid-code requirements (anti-islanding, ROCOF, voltage-frequency ride-through)
- Carry **UKCA or UKCA+CE** marking
- Be **type-tested** to EN 50549-1 or equivalent

Every model we recommend below meets these criteria. If a listing does not specify the certifications, do not buy it.

## The three brands that matter in the UK

### Hoymiles HMS-800W-2T — the volume workhorse (~£120–160)

Hoymiles has become the default in European plug-in solar. The HMS-800W-2T (also sold as HMS-800-2T in some markets) is the flagship dual-panel 800W micro inverter shipped in the majority of UK and German kits.

- **Peak efficiency:** 96.7%
- **MPPT efficiency:** 99.8% — the highest in its class
- **Warranty:** 12 years standard, extendable to 25
- **Monitoring:** S-Miles Cloud app (functional, not flashy). Requires a separate Hoymiles DTU-WLite gateway for cloud logging, or works standalone with local Bluetooth
- **Certifications:** G98, EN 50549-1, UKCA

The [Hoymiles HMS-800 review](/blog/hoymiles-hms800-review-uk) has the deeper dive. This is the safe default for a DIY build or any kit where the vendor discloses "Hoymiles inside".

### Enphase IQ8AC — the premium pick (~£145–180 per unit)

Enphase has been shipping micro inverters since 2008 and has installed over 60 million units globally. The IQ8AC is their latest UK-certified model. Where Hoymiles pairs one inverter with two panels, Enphase uses **one inverter per panel** — so an 800W setup needs two IQ8AC units.

- **Peak efficiency:** 97.0% — the highest of the three
- **MPPT efficiency:** 99.0%
- **Warranty:** 25 years standard — industry-leading
- **Monitoring:** Enphase Enlighten app. Per-panel data, consumption tracking, proactive fault alerts. Genuinely best-in-class software
- **Certifications:** G98, EN 50549-1, UKCA

The trade-off: two-per-system doubles hardware cost and adds installation complexity, but you get panel-level MPPT (huge advantage under partial shading — see the [shading solutions guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-shading-solutions-uk)) and a 25-year warranty that is genuinely honoured.

### APsystems EZ1-M — the value option (~£100–130)

APsystems is a smaller player than Hoymiles or Enphase but has a solid reputation in European balcony solar since 2021. The EZ1-M is purpose-built for plug-in / [Balkonkraftwerk](/blog/what-germany-teaches-uk-about-plug-in-solar) applications.

- **Peak efficiency:** 96.5%
- **MPPT efficiency:** 99.5%
- **Warranty:** 10 years standard
- **Monitoring:** EMA app. Bluetooth for setup, WiFi for ongoing data. Basic but functional
- **Certifications:** G98, EN 50549-1, UKCA

The [APsystems EZ1-M review](/blog/apsystems-ez1-m-review-uk) has the full breakdown. Cheapest of the three and completely adequate for most UK setups.

## What to avoid

- **Unbranded Amazon/eBay units.** No published spec sheet, no G98 certification path, and no realistic warranty. The inverter is the safety-critical component in your system. Do not cheap out.
- **EU-listed inverters without UK certification.** A CE mark alone is not enough for post-27-August-2026 UK installs. You need UKCA or UKCA+CE.
- **Deye.** Popular in some European markets but has had firmware regression issues and inconsistent support. The three brands above are safer picks.
- **Inverters rated over 800VA.** They physically will not comply with the UK cap. Yes, even "just" 900W is non-compliant.

## Testing your inverter after install

Once you have wired everything up and filed your [G98 with the DNO](/blog/g98-dno-notification-step-by-step), verify the inverter is working:

- Use a [Fluke 117 multimeter](https://amzn.to/4bSN4aq) (~£199) to confirm AC output at the socket sits at 230–253V and 50Hz ± 0.5Hz.
- Plug a [TP-Link Tapo P110 smart plug](https://amzn.to/4m9Yh9U) (~£15) between inverter and wall socket for independent kWh logging — useful cross-check against the inverter's own app.
- If output is intermittent or the inverter keeps tripping, work through the [micro inverter error codes guide](/blog/micro-inverter-error-codes-uk) and the [not-generating troubleshooting checklist](/blog/plug-in-solar-not-generating-troubleshooting-uk).
- Browse [Amazon UK for solar multimeters and clamp meters](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=solar+multimeter) if you do not already own one.

## The bottom line

For most UK plug-in solar buyers, the micro inverter decision is made for you by the kit you buy. All the major branded kits use proven inverter designs. If you are building your own system, Hoymiles is the safe default, Enphase is worth the premium for shaded sites or long-term peace of mind, and APsystems handles budget builds without cutting safety corners.

Whichever you choose, buy from a UK-market listing with clear UKCA certification and a warranty path you can actually invoke in year eight. The inverter will outlive most of the rest of the system — it needs to work quietly for 15+ years.
