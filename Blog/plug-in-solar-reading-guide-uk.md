Reading doesn't get talked about as a solar town the way Oxford or Cambridge do, but it should. It sits in the Thames Valley, one of the sunnier corners of England, and its housing stock is unusually varied for a town its size — Victorian terraces around Caversham and East Reading, 1990s–2000s estate housing out towards Lower Earley and Woodley, and a growing stack of new-build flats near the station aimed at the town's large tech and finance workforce. Each of those housing types changes what a [plug-in solar kit](/blog/plug-in-solar-uk-complete-guide) can realistically do for you.

## Sunshine and generation in the Thames Valley

Reading sees around 1,500–1,550 hours of sunshine a year, comfortably above the UK average of roughly 1,400 hours and in the same band as most of the South East. For a typical [800W plug-in kit](/blog/how-much-electricity-does-800w-solar-generate-uk), that translates to somewhere in the range of 550–700 kWh a year with a reasonably unobstructed south-facing position — enough to make a real dent in a household's daytime electricity use, particularly for anyone working from home.

Orientation matters more than postcode here. Reading's terraced streets around Caversham and West Reading run in all directions, so plenty of gardens face east-west rather than due south. If that's your situation, our guide on [east vs west facing installations](/blog/east-west-facing-plug-in-solar-uk) and the broader [south-facing vs east-west comparison](/blog/south-facing-vs-east-west-solar-uk) are worth reading before you buy — an east-west split array often outperforms a shaded south-facing one anyway.

## Victorian terraces: Caversham, East Reading, West Reading

These streets are classic [plug-in solar terrace territory](/blog/plug-in-solar-terraced-houses-uk): narrow gardens, outbuildings, and in many cases no off-street parking to complicate panel placement. A single 400W or dual 800W panel set mounted on a garden frame or against a rear wall is the usual approach. Watch for overshadowing from neighbouring extensions, which are common in this part of Reading as households have added space over the decades.

None of these areas are conservation areas in the way parts of Oxford or Bath are, so there's generally no planning constraint to worry about — plug-in solar sits outside the planning system entirely as a plug-and-play appliance, but it's still worth a quick check if your specific street has an Article 4 direction removing permitted development rights.

## New-build estates: Lower Earley, Woodley, Green Park

Reading's outer estates built from the 1990s onward tend to have larger, more open gardens and fewer overshadowing issues, which makes them straightforward sites for a [larger 800W-plus setup](/blog/400w-vs-800w-plug-in-solar-uk-guide). Many of these homes also have garages or sheds with suitable south-facing roof pitches, though under the current rules a plug-in kit's panels need to connect through a standard 13A socket circuit rather than being wired directly — see our breakdown of [where you can and can't install plug-in solar](/blog/where-to-install-plug-in-solar-uk) for the detail.

## Flats near the station and Thames-side developments

Reading's redevelopment around the station and along the Thames has brought thousands of new flats, many aimed at employees at the town's big tech-sector employers. If you're renting or own a flat with a balcony, our [balcony solar guide](/blog/balcony-solar-uk-guide) covers the specifics — balcony rail mounting, weight limits, and the fact that a [DNO notification](/blog/g98-dno-notification-step-by-step) is still required even for a compact balcony kit. If you're renting, check our [guide for renters](/blog/plug-in-solar-for-renters-uk) and, if you're a landlord weighing whether to allow tenants to install kits, the [landlord-specific guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-landlords-uk) covers the practical and liability questions.

## Getting started

Before you order, run through our [starter kit checklist](/blog/plug-in-solar-starter-kit-checklist-uk) to confirm your socket circuit, fuse rating, and mounting surface are all suitable. Reading's position in the wider South East puts it alongside towns covered in our [South East England guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-south-east-england-guide), which has regional performance context if you want to compare notes with nearby Oxford, Basingstoke, or Guildford households.

### Is Reading a good location for plug-in solar?

Yes. With above-average sunshine hours for the UK and a housing stock that includes plenty of unshaded gardens and balconies, Reading is well-suited to plug-in solar, though actual output depends heavily on your specific garden's orientation and shading.

### Do I need planning permission for plug-in solar in Reading?

No, in almost all cases. Plug-in solar is treated as a portable appliance rather than a building alteration, so it falls outside the planning system. The main exception would be a property with an Article 4 direction or listed status, which is uncommon in Reading's newer and Victorian housing stock alike.

### Can renters in Reading flats install plug-in solar?

Yes, provided the landlord agrees and the balcony or outdoor space can safely support the panel and mounting hardware. A DNO notification is still required regardless of tenancy status.
