Devon sits right alongside Cornwall in the South West's sunshine league table, but it has its own character: two coastlines, Dartmoor and Exmoor inland, and one of the largest static caravan and holiday-let markets in the country. If you're weighing up a plug-in solar kit for a home in Exeter, Plymouth, Torquay, Barnstaple, or a holiday cottage near the moors, here's what actually matters locally.

## Sunshine and generation potential

Devon averages around 1,550–1,650 hours of sunshine a year, comfortably above the UK average of roughly 1,400 hours, though slightly behind Cornwall's best coastal spots. The South Hams and English Riviera (Torquay, Paignton, Dartmouth) tend to sit at the sunnier end of the county, while inland areas near Dartmoor see more cloud and rainfall driven by the moor's elevation.

For an unshaded, south-facing 800W kit, expect annual generation broadly in line with the wider [South West England guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-south-west-england-guide) — typically 10–15% above the national average, with the coastal strip performing best. Our piece on [Cornwall's generation numbers](/blog/plug-in-solar-cornwall-guide-uk) gives a useful benchmark, since the climate and latitude are very similar across the Tamar.

## Two coastlines means two sets of salt exposure

Devon is unusual in having both a south coast (English Riviera, South Hams) and a north coast (Ilfracombe, Woolacombe, Croyde) exposed to Atlantic weather. Both bring salt-laden air that accelerates corrosion on exposed brackets, screws, and connectors faster than an inland fitting would experience. If you're within a mile or two of either coast, our guide to [coastal areas and salt corrosion](/blog/plug-in-solar-coastal-areas-salt-corrosion-uk) covers the marine-grade fixings and IP-rated connectors worth prioritising.

Inland, around Dartmoor and Exmoor, the concern shifts from salt to wind and rain. Exposed upland areas see genuinely high average wind speeds, so a ground-mounted A-frame needs more ballast than the same setup in a sheltered Exeter garden, and fixings are worth re-checking after the first few autumn storms.

## Devon's caravan and holiday-let market

Devon has one of the highest concentrations of static caravan parks and holiday cottages in England, and this is where plug-in solar has a genuinely useful niche beyond ordinary homeowners. A compliant 800VA kit plugged into an existing outdoor socket can meaningfully offset a static caravan's daytime electricity draw without any of the complexity of a full off-grid conversion — see our dedicated guide to [plug-in solar for caravans and motorhomes](/blog/plug-in-solar-caravans-motorhomes-uk) for the specifics of mounting on a static unit versus a touring van.

Holiday-let owners considering this for guest properties should also check our piece on [plug-in solar for Airbnb hosts](/blog/plug-in-solar-airbnb-hosts-uk) — the economics differ from a primary residence because you're offsetting daytime guest usage rather than your own consumption pattern.

## Legal status and DNO notification

Plug-in solar became legal across the whole of the UK, Devon included, on 27 August 2026 under SI 2026 No. 848, which created the legal category of "plug-in microgenerator." The specification is the same regardless of location: 800VA/3.5A maximum output, a standard BS 1363 plug, a 5A fuse, no battery in the plug circuit, and no extension leads. Our explainer on [SI 2026/848](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk) covers the full legal text in plain English.

Devon sits within National Grid Electricity Distribution's network area for most of the county, with Western Power Distribution's South West licence area covering some pockets. Whichever DNO you fall under, a G98 notification is the standard route for a compliant plug-in kit — our [step-by-step G98 guide](/blog/g98-dno-notification-step-by-step) walks through the online form and typical turnaround time.

If you're buying a kit and want to double-check it's on the approved list, the [live ENA-compliant device registry](/blog/uk-compliant-plug-in-solar-devices-live) is updated regularly and is the definitive source — cross-check the manufacturer and model before you buy, particularly with imported kits sold through third-party marketplaces.

## Older housing stock and period properties

Devon has a large stock of Victorian terraces in Exeter and Plymouth, plus a substantial number of listed longhouses and converted farm buildings in rural areas. None of this affects whether plug-in solar is legal to use, but older wiring is worth having checked by an electrician before you plug in a new load, and listed buildings may have additional constraints on visible fixtures — our guide to [plug-in solar and listed buildings](/blog/plug-in-solar-listed-buildings-uk) covers what to check before mounting anything on a protected structure.

## What it costs and what it saves

Kit prices in Devon are the same as anywhere else in the UK, since supply chains don't vary regionally, but above-average sun hours on the coastal strip mean payback tends to land at the faster end of the typical range. Our [800W payback calculator guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-payback-calculator-800w-uk) lets you run your own numbers based on your postcode and orientation, and our roundup of the [best plug-in solar kit for UK conditions](/blog/best-plug-in-solar-kit-uk) is a sensible starting point if you haven't settled on a kit yet.

### Is Devon a good location for plug-in solar?

Yes — Devon's sun hours are comfortably above the UK average, particularly along the south coast and English Riviera. The main considerations are salt exposure on both coastlines and wind loading on higher ground near Dartmoor and Exmoor, both manageable with the right fixings.

### Can I use plug-in solar on a static caravan in Devon?

Yes, provided the caravan has a suitable outdoor socket and the kit meets the same 800VA/3.5A specification as a house installation. See our [caravan and motorhome guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-caravans-motorhomes-uk) for mounting specifics.

### Do I need to notify my network operator in Devon?

Yes, in almost all cases. Devon falls mainly within National Grid Electricity Distribution's area, with some Western Power Distribution coverage. A standard G98 notification applies either way — see our [step-by-step guide](/blog/g98-dno-notification-step-by-step).
