Of all the questions we get asked about plug-in solar, "will it run my fridge?" comes up more than almost anything else. It's an understandable question — a fridge-freezer runs continuously, 24 hours a day, which makes it feel like the perfect match for a device that generates electricity all day long. The honest answer is more nuanced than yes or no, and understanding why matters before you buy a kit expecting it to specifically cover this one appliance.

## How a fridge-freezer actually draws power

A typical UK fridge-freezer doesn't run constantly at full power. The compressor cycles on and off, drawing somewhere between 100W and 200W when it's actually running, with an average draw across a full day usually landing between 1 and 2 kWh — roughly 40-80W averaged over 24 hours. That's well within what an [800W plug-in solar kit](/blog/best-plug-in-solar-kit-uk) can generate during a decent summer day in most of the UK, which is why the question seems to have an obvious "yes" answer at first glance.

The catch is that plug-in microgenerators under SI 2026/848 don't send power straight to one named appliance. They feed into your home's existing wiring through a standard BS 1363 socket, and the electricity goes wherever your house happens to be drawing power from at that instant — see our explainer on [self-consumption](/blog/plug-in-solar-self-consumption-tips-uk) for how this actually works in practice. There's no way to dedicate your panel output specifically to the fridge; you're reducing your overall household draw, and the fridge is just one part of that draw, all day and all night.

## The day/night problem

This is where the fridge-freezer question runs into the rule that matters most for plug-in solar: **no battery is permitted in the plug-in circuit** under the current UK framework. Your panel generates electricity only while there's daylight hitting it, and that electricity either gets used by your house in that exact moment or it's lost — it can't be stored for use after dark. A fridge that needs to keep running at 2am is drawing from the grid at 2am, full stop, regardless of how much your panel generated at 2pm.

What this means in practice: your plug-in solar kit covers a meaningful share of your fridge-freezer's running cost during daylight hours — potentially most of it on a bright day, if your panel's generation exceeds your other daytime household draw — but it contributes nothing overnight. Over a full year, most UK households see plug-in solar offset somewhere between a third and a half of a fridge-freezer's total annual running cost, depending on orientation, daylight hours, and how much else is drawing power in your home during the day. For the fuller picture on realistic savings, see our guide on [how much plug-in solar actually saves](/blog/how-much-does-plug-in-solar-save-uk).

## Why fridge-freezers are still a good match, even with that caveat

Even with the overnight gap, a fridge-freezer is one of the better "always-on" loads to think about when sizing a plug-in solar kit, for one simple reason: it provides a reliable daytime [base load](/blog/plug-in-solar-base-load-coverage-uk) that soaks up generation your panel would otherwise export to nobody. Since plug-in microgenerators under the current framework are self-consumption only — there's no export payment mechanism for a plugged-in appliance circuit — any generation that isn't used in the home at that moment is simply wasted. A fridge-freezer's steady daytime draw means less of your panel's output goes to waste on days when nothing else in the house happens to be running.

This is different from, say, an electric oven or a kettle, which draw far more power but only briefly and unpredictably. The fridge's low, constant draw is exactly the kind of load that benefits from a modest, steady generation source like an 800W panel — it's not going to blow past your fridge's needs on a sunny afternoon, but it reliably chips away at it hour after hour.

## Does winter change this?

Yes, noticeably. UK daylight hours shrink dramatically between November and February, and panel output drops with them — our [winter performance guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-in-winter-uk) covers the numbers in more depth. A fridge-freezer's power draw doesn't change much between seasons, but the hours during which your panel can offset it shrink to roughly 7-8 hours of usable daylight around the winter solstice, compared to 16+ hours in June. Expect your fridge-freezer offset to drop to perhaps 15-20% of its running cost in deep winter, climbing back toward 50% or more by midsummer.

## What kit size actually makes sense

You don't need to oversize a kit specifically for a fridge-freezer — its draw is modest enough that a standard 400W or 800W setup comfortably covers it alongside other daytime use. If a fridge-freezer is genuinely your main concern (for example, in a home office or outbuilding with a standalone drinks fridge or chest freezer), a single-panel 400W kit is often enough on its own. ### Can plug-in solar run my fridge-freezer overnight?

No. The current UK framework bans battery storage in the plug-in circuit, so your panel only generates electricity while there's daylight on it. Overnight, your fridge-freezer draws entirely from the grid as normal.

### How much will plug-in solar actually save on my fridge-freezer running costs?

Typically a third to half of its annual running cost across the year, varying from as little as 15-20% in deep winter to 50%+ in midsummer, depending on your panel's orientation and how much daylight your area gets.

### Is it legal to just plug my kit in near the fridge?

Yes — a plug-in microgenerator feeds your home's wiring through any standard socket, not a dedicated circuit for one appliance. It doesn't matter which socket you use; the electricity goes into your home's general supply and offsets whatever is drawing power at that moment, fridge included.
