Hampshire is one of the more varied counties in the South for plug-in solar, stretching from the busy ports of Portsmouth and Southampton through the New Forest's rural villages to the commuter towns of Basingstoke, Andover and Fareham. Since [SI 2026 No. 848](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk) made "plug-in microgenerators" legal across Great Britain on 27 August 2026, households across all of these areas have been asking the same question with slightly different answers depending on where exactly they live. Here's what actually matters if you're in Hampshire.

## Good, reliable South Coast sun

Hampshire sits firmly in the sunnier southern band of England, benefiting from broadly similar conditions to the rest of the South East and South West — our [South East England guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-south-east-england-guide) and [South West England guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-south-west-england-guide) both cover the regional averages you can expect. The coastal strip around Southsea, Gosport and Southampton Water sees slightly higher average daylight hours than inland parts of the county around Basingstoke and Andover, though the difference is modest compared to the gap between southern and northern England generally.

## Housing stock varies sharply across the county

Portsmouth's dense Victorian terraces and Southampton's mix of post-war semis and high-rise flats present very different mounting challenges to the detached and New Forest cottages further west. If you're in a terraced house with a small rear yard in Portsmouth or central Southampton, a fence-mounted or wall-mounted kit is usually more practical than a ground-mount array — our [fence-mounted panels guide](/blog/fence-mounted-plug-in-solar-panels-uk) and [wall-mount guide](/blog/wall-mount-plug-in-solar) both apply directly. Out in the New Forest villages and the commuter belt around Basingstoke, Winchester and Alton, larger gardens make ground-mounted kits like the [EcoFlow STREAM Garden Ground Kit](/blog/ecoflow-stream-review-uk) the more common choice.

## Flats are common in the port cities

Southampton and Portsmouth both have a substantial share of flats and maisonettes, particularly near the waterfronts and in post-war estates. If that's your situation, our [flats and no-garden guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-flats-no-garden-uk) and [renters guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-for-renters-uk) are essential reading before buying anything — balcony space, leaseholder permissions and the practicalities of running a cable back indoors all matter more than panel wattage in that setting. Our [Southampton-specific guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-southampton-guide-uk) goes into more detail on the city's particular housing mix if that's where you're based.

## Who covers your network connection

Most of Hampshire sits within Scottish and Southern Electricity Networks' (SSEN) distribution area, which is worth confirming before you submit your [G98 DNO notification](/blog/g98-notification-guide-uk-homeowners) — sending the paperwork to the wrong network operator simply delays registration without actually completing it. SSEN's postcode checker will confirm your specific network in under a minute if you're near a county boundary or unsure.

## Checking compliance before you buy

Wherever you are in Hampshire, every kit you consider should carry a micro-inverter listed on the [ENA's live G98 compliant device register](/blog/uk-compliant-plug-in-solar-devices-live). New manufacturers are added regularly — recent listings include additional Worldwide Solar and Solarfy kit variants, covered in our [newly ENA-approved kits roundup](/blog/new-ena-approved-plug-in-solar-kits-october-2026-uk) — so it's worth a quick check even if you looked a few weeks ago. Our [best plug-in solar kit](/blog/best-plug-in-solar-kit-uk) roundup is a good starting point if you want a specific recommendation rather than a general shopping list.

## Smart meters and monitoring

Hampshire has had a fairly typical SMETS2 rollout, so most households will already have a smart meter capable of registering the change once a plug-in kit starts generating. Our [smart meter and solar panels guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-smart-meter-uk) explains what to expect on your in-home display, and how to tell genuine under-performance from the meter simply lagging behind real-time generation.

## Coastal corrosion is a real consideration near the water

Anything mounted within a mile or two of Southampton Water, Portsmouth Harbour or the Solent coastline is exposed to salt-laden air that accelerates corrosion on exposed metal fixings, cable glands and connectors faster than an equivalent inland installation around Basingstoke or Alton. Our [coastal corrosion guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-coastal-areas-salt-corrosion-uk) covers the extra maintenance checks worth building into your routine if you're anywhere near the water.

## Comparing against neighbouring counties

If you're near a county boundary, it's worth reading the guide either side too. Dorset and the New Forest fringe share very similar coastal conditions to western Hampshire, while the commuter towns around Basingstoke and Fleet have more in common with Surrey and the wider South East belt than with the maritime south of the county.

### Is plug-in solar legal in Hampshire right now?

Yes. Plug-in microgenerators have been legal across the whole of Great Britain, Hampshire included, since 27 August 2026 under SI 2026 No. 848, provided the kit meets the 800VA/3.5A output limit, uses a G98-listed micro-inverter, and you notify your DNO (SSEN for most of the county) before switching it on.

### Which network operator do I notify in Hampshire?

Most of Hampshire falls under Scottish and Southern Electricity Networks' (SSEN) distribution area. Use SSEN's postcode lookup to confirm your exact network before submitting your G98 notification, particularly if you're near the Dorset or Surrey border.

### Does living near the Hampshire coast affect performance?

It makes little difference to generation, but it does raise the maintenance bar. Salt-laden air around Southampton Water and Portsmouth Harbour accelerates corrosion on exposed fixings and connectors, so coastal installations need more frequent cleaning and checks than inland ones around Basingstoke or Winchester.
