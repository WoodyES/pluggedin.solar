# Does Argos Sell Plug-In Solar Panels in the UK? (2026 Buyer's Guide)

If you've searched "Argos solar panels" or "Argos plug-in solar" expecting to click and collect a kit this weekend, you're not alone — Argos is still many people's default stop for home and garden electricals, and plug-in solar has been legal in the UK since [27 August 2026 under SI 2026 No. 848](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk). The short answer is no: as of October 2026, Argos does not list plug-in solar panels, plug-in microgenerator kits, or anything resembling the EcoFlow STREAM-style products now sold by its rivals. This guide explains why, what Argos does sell that's adjacent, and exactly where to buy a compliant kit instead.

## Why Argos Hasn't Followed B&Q, Currys and Screwfix

Argos built its catalogue around small electricals, toys, furniture and click-and-collect convenience — not around DIY, electrical or garden hardware in the way B&Q, Wickes and Screwfix have. Those three trade and home-improvement retailers moved quickly on plug-in solar precisely because it sits in their existing category: garden power, electrical accessories, and DIY install kit. Currys followed because it already sells portable power stations and home energy gadgets. Amazon UK listed kits from day one because it's a marketplace, not a curated retailer with a fixed category structure.

Argos, part of Sainsbury's, has stayed out of the category entirely. There's no public statement explaining this, but the pattern fits: Argos has been narrowing its own-brand and general merchandise range over the past few years rather than expanding into new regulated electrical product lines. A product that requires BS 1363 plug compliance, a 5A fuse rating, and DNO notification under [G98](/blog/g98-notification-guide-uk-homeowners) isn't a natural fit for a catalogue retailer that typically avoids products needing any install guidance beyond "plug it in."

That could change. If plug-in solar keeps growing through winter and into 2027, don't be surprised if Argos eventually lists a basic kit under its own-brand Habitat or George ranges. For now, though, it's simply not stocked.

## What Argos Does Sell (and Why It's Not the Same Thing)

Search Argos today and you'll find:

- **Portable power stations** (various brands) — useful for camping and power cuts, but these are batteries, not generation kits, and pairing one with an unapproved solar input can breach the no-battery-in-circuit rule that applies to compliant [plug-in microgenerator](/blog/plug-in-microgenerator-uk-what-is-it) kits.
- **Garden solar lighting and small off-grid solar chargers** — genuinely solar, but output in single-digit watts, designed to trickle-charge a light or phone, not to offset household electricity use.
- **Camping solar panels** — portable folding panels intended to charge power banks or leisure batteries off-grid, not to connect to a UK socket under G98.

None of these are a substitute for a proper 400-800W plug-in solar kit. If what you actually want is to reduce your electricity bill by feeding generated power into your home circuit, you need a kit built and tested for that specific use case — which means buying from a retailer that actually stocks them.

## Where to Buy Instead

Four UK retailers currently carry genuine plug-in solar kits:

- **B&Q** — the widest range of mounting hardware and bundled kits, good for first-time buyers who want everything in one trip.
- **Screwfix** — trade-focused, often cheaper on bare components if you're happy to source your own mounting brackets.
- **Currys** — best for kits bundled with a portable power station, and for in-store advice on the EcoFlow STREAM range.
- **Amazon UK** — widest selection and fastest delivery, but check the seller is listing a [G98-compliant model](/blog/uk-compliant-plug-in-solar-devices-live) rather than an imported kit that doesn't meet UK spec.
- **Lidl** — runs occasional limited-stock promotions; see our [Lidl plug-in solar review](/blog/lidl-plug-in-solar-review-uk) for what to expect if you catch one.

## What to Check Before You Buy, Wherever You Shop

Whichever retailer you end up using, the compliance checklist is identical:

- Confirm the kit is listed on the [ENA's compliant device registry](/blog/uk-compliant-plug-in-solar-devices-live) — this is the definitive source for what's actually legal to connect under G98, and it's updated as new models are approved.
- Check the output is capped at 800VA / 3.5A, with no option to "unlock" higher output.
- Make sure there's no battery wired into the generation circuit itself — pairing a separate portable power station on its own socket is fine, but a battery inline with the panel isn't.
- Avoid extension leads between the kit and the wall socket — it must plug in directly.

If a listing is missing any of this detail, or the seller can't confirm G98 compliance, treat it the same as an Argos search that comes up empty: walk away and buy from one of the retailers above instead. Our guide on [spotting a non-compliant kit](/blog/how-to-spot-non-compliant-plug-in-solar-kit-uk) covers the warning signs in more detail, and our [buying checklist](/blog/plug-in-solar-buying-checklist-uk) is worth running through before you commit.

## What If You've Already Bought Something Faulty or Wrong?

If you've ordered a kit from a marketplace seller and it arrives without a BS 1363 plug, without a fuse rating printed on the unit, or without any documentation at all, you have full consumer rights to reject it. Our guide to [faulty returns and consumer rights](/blog/plug-in-solar-faulty-return-consumer-rights-uk) walks through exactly how to handle that, including what counts as "not as described" under the Consumer Rights Act.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will Argos start selling plug-in solar panels?

There's no confirmed plan as of October 2026. Argos has historically avoided categories requiring install guidance or electrical compliance documentation, which plug-in solar falls squarely into. If demand keeps growing through 2027, a basic own-brand listing isn't impossible, but for now the retailer sits outside the category entirely.

### Does Argos sell anything similar to plug-in solar?

Argos stocks portable power stations and small off-grid garden solar lights and chargers, but neither is designed to connect to your home's electrical circuit for bill-offsetting generation. For that you need a dedicated, G98-compliant plug-in microgenerator kit from B&Q, Screwfix, Currys, Amazon UK or Lidl.

### Is it cheaper to buy plug-in solar from Amazon than B&Q or Currys?

It varies by model and promotion period, and Amazon's marketplace structure means pricing moves faster than high-street retailers. The bigger risk on Amazon is accidentally buying an imported kit that isn't G98-compliant, so always check the listing against the [ENA compliant device registry](/blog/uk-compliant-plug-in-solar-devices-live) before ordering, regardless of price.
