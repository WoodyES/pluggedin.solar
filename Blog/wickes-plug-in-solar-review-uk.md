Since plug-in solar became legal across the UK on 27 August 2026 under SI 2026 No. 848, Wickes has been one of the quieter retail entrants — not pushing the hardest marketing push like some rivals, but keeping a steady, reasonably priced range on its shelves and website. If you're a Wickes regular who's already there for fencing, decking, or a trade account, it's worth knowing exactly what they stock and whether it's actually worth buying there rather than driving to B&Q or ordering from Amazon.

This isn't a hypothetical "what to expect" piece. Wickes has had compliant kits in stores since early autumn, and there's now enough real stock and real pricing to assess properly.

## What Wickes actually stocks

Wickes' plug-in solar range sits firmly in the practical, trade-adjacent end of the market rather than the premium consumer-tech end. Expect:

- **Entry 400W single-panel kits**, usually paired with a Hoymiles or similarly established microinverter, aimed at first-time buyers or small gardens
- **800W dual-panel kits** at the regulatory ceiling, the most popular configuration and the one most comparable to EcoFlow's STREAM range
- **Mounting hardware sold separately** in most cases — Wickes' DIY heritage means they expect you to buy ground stakes, wall brackets, or rail clamps as needed rather than bundling everything
- Basic in-store signage confirming ENA G98 listing, which is a genuinely useful detail — not every retailer makes this easy to verify at point of sale

What you won't find at Wickes is anything approaching EcoFlow's STREAM Ultra or Anker's SOLIX ecosystem — no integrated battery options, no smart-home app ecosystem beyond basic generation monitoring. This is a "buy the panels, plug them in, check the app occasionally" proposition, not a premium energy-management system.

## Pricing versus the rest of the market

Wickes tends to sit in the middle of the market — cheaper than EcoFlow and Anker's flagship kits, but not quite as aggressively priced as some of the budget options appearing from newer ENA-listed manufacturers. An 800W kit typically runs somewhere in the £550–£700 range depending on promotions, which puts it broadly in line with Currys and slightly below B&Q on equivalent specs. Our [full retailer comparison](/blog/bandq-vs-screwfix-vs-currys-wickes-plug-in-solar-uk) tracks this across all four major stockists if you want exact current pricing before you commit.

Wickes also runs periodic trade discounts if you hold a Wickes trade account, which is worth checking if you're already buying other materials there — it can shave a meaningful amount off an 800W kit that a one-off retail customer wouldn't see.

## Checking compliance before you buy at Wickes

Every kit Wickes stocks under its own plug-in solar range should carry a visible ENA reference number on the box or product page, but it's worth checking it yourself rather than taking signage on trust, particularly with clearance or end-of-line stock. Our continuously updated [ENA compliant devices registry](/blog/uk-compliant-plug-in-solar-devices-live) lists every legally sellable kit by manufacturer and reference number — cross-check the model before you buy, especially if the in-store price looks unusually low.

Some of the kits turning up on the registry recently come from smaller UK manufacturers like Solarfy and Worldwide Solar rather than the big established brands, often pairing a Hoymiles or VaySunic microinverter with panels from AIKO or JA Solar. Wickes hasn't picked these up as own-range stock yet, but it's a sign the budget end of the market is getting more crowded — worth watching if you're happy to shop around rather than stick to one retailer. Our round-up of [newly ENA-approved kits](/blog/new-ena-approved-plug-in-solar-kits-october-2026-uk) tracks this as new manufacturers get listed.

## Returns and after-sales

Wickes' standard 30-day return policy applies to plug-in solar kits bought in-store or online, which is useful if a panel arrives damaged or an inverter doesn't power up — but beyond that window you're into manufacturer warranty territory rather than Wickes' own customer service, since Wickes is reselling rather than manufacturing the inverter and panels. If something fails after month two, check the specific microinverter brand's warranty terms rather than expecting Wickes itself to resolve it. Our guide to [faulty kit returns and consumer rights](/blog/plug-in-solar-faulty-return-consumer-rights-uk) covers exactly what you're entitled to and from whom.

## Who Wickes suits

**Good fit if:**
- You already have a Wickes trade account or shop there regularly for other materials
- You're comfortable sourcing your own mounting hardware
- You want a mid-price kit without paying for app ecosystem extras you won't use
- You're installing on a garden or ground mount rather than a tricky balcony fixing

**Less ideal if:**
- You want an all-in-one box with mounting hardware included
- You're after battery expansion down the line
- You want the deepest brand support network if something goes wrong

If you're still deciding between retailers entirely, it's worth reading our guide to [kits under £500](/blog/best-plug-in-solar-kits-under-500-uk) alongside the comparison piece above — Wickes occasionally undercuts that threshold on its entry 400W range during promotions.

### Does Wickes sell a complete plug-in solar kit with mounting included?

Not always. Wickes' 400W and 800W kits typically include the panels and microinverter, but mounting hardware — ground stakes, wall brackets, or rail clamps — is often sold separately. Check the specific product listing before assuming everything's in one box.

### Is Wickes plug-in solar cheaper than B&Q or Currys?

Generally yes for equivalent specs, though pricing moves around with promotions at all four major retailers. Check our [retailer comparison](/blog/bandq-vs-screwfix-vs-currys-wickes-plug-in-solar-uk) for current pricing before buying.

### How do I know a Wickes kit is actually ENA compliant?

Check the model and manufacturer against our [live ENA registry](/blog/uk-compliant-plug-in-solar-devices-live) rather than relying on in-store signage alone, especially for clearance or end-of-line stock.
