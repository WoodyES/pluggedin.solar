## Why Self-Consumption Matters

Every kWh you use directly from your solar panels saves you the full import rate (24.5p at the Ofgem cap). Every kWh you export earns only 3-6p via the [SEG](/blog/plug-in-solar-export-payments-seg-uk). Maximising self-consumption can **double your annual savings**.

## 1. Run the Washing Machine During Peak Solar

Set your washing machine to run between 11am-2pm when generation peaks. A typical cycle uses 1-1.5 kWh — easily covered by your 800W system.

## 2. Run the Dishwasher Mid-Day

Same principle — 1-1.5 kWh per cycle. Use the delay-start function if you're not home at peak solar hours.

## 3. Charge Devices During the Day

Laptops (60W), phones (10W), tablets (15W) — plug them in during solar hours. Small loads add up over a year.

## 4. Use Smart Plugs with Timers

A [Tapo P110](/blog/tapo-p110-solar-monitoring-review-uk) or [Shelly Plus Plug](/blog/shelly-plus-plug-uk-review) can schedule appliances to run during solar hours and monitor how much energy they use.

## 5. Set Your Immersion Heater Timer

If you have an immersion heater, running it for 30-60 minutes during peak solar can use 1-3 kWh — a significant portion of daily generation. This only makes sense if you'd otherwise heat water with imported electricity.

## 6. Pre-Cool/Pre-Heat Your Home

On sunny days, pre-cool with fans or AC, or pre-heat in spring/autumn. Your home stores the thermal energy for hours after solar generation ends.

## 7. Work From Home

[Home office users](/blog/plug-in-solar-home-office-uk) typically achieve 70-80% self-consumption versus 35-50% for commuters. The base load of a home office (PC, monitor, router, lights) aligns perfectly with solar generation hours.

## 8. Use a Smart Home System

[Home Assistant](/blog/solar-panel-monitoring-home-assistant-uk) or similar can automatically trigger appliances when solar generation exceeds base load. Advanced users can create automations that start the washing machine only when generation is above a threshold.

## 9. Time Your EV Charging

If you have an EV, even 2-3 hours of daytime charging at 2.3kW (standard socket) uses 5-7 kWh. With an 800W system generating 3-4 kWh, you won't cover the full charge, but you'll offset a meaningful chunk. See our [EV charging guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-ev-charging-uk).

## 10. Monitor and Adjust

Use your [inverter app](/blog/ecoflow-stream-app-setup-monitoring-uk) or an [energy monitor](/blog/best-energy-monitor-plug-in-solar-uk) to see when you're exporting. Adjust your habits to shift loads into those periods.

## What Self-Consumption Rate Can You Expect?

| Household Type | Typical Self-Consumption | Annual Savings (800W, Midlands) |
|---------------|-------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Out all day | 30-40% | £63-84 |
| Mixed (in/out) | 50-60% | £105-126 |
| Mostly home | 65-80% | £136-168 |
| Optimised (smart home) | 80-90% | £168-189 |

### Is 100% self-consumption possible?

Practically no, because generation peaks don't perfectly match demand. But 85-90% is achievable with deliberate effort and smart automation.

### Does a battery improve self-consumption?

Significantly. A [battery](/blog/battery-storage-vs-no-battery-plug-in-solar-uk) lets you store daytime generation for evening use, potentially reaching 90%+ self-consumption.
