If you've been refreshing the [ENA compliant devices registry](/blog/uk-compliant-plug-in-solar-devices-live) looking for new names, you'll have spotted Zendure Technology's **SolarFlow 800** appear in a cluster of six separate listings in late September 2026 — one for each mounting configuration. That's an unusually thorough submission, and it's worth asking why Zendure bothered, and whether the kit itself is any good.

Zendure isn't a new name in UK home energy — their SolarFlow balcony battery system has sold steadily in Germany and the Netherlands for two years. The SolarFlow 800 is their first product built specifically for the UK's [SI 2026/848](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk) plug-in microgenerator framework, and the six ENA entries (`ZENDU/21469` through `ZENDU/21474`) cover wall, floor, and balcony bracket combinations, with and without an extra 4m MC4 extension cable bundled in.

## What's actually in the box

The core system across all six variants is identical:

- SolarFlow 800 micro-inverter (800W, the UK regulatory ceiling)
- Two solar panels (unspecified wattage per panel in the ENA filing, but totalling the 800W inverter capacity)
- A UK 13A plug fitted as standard — no Schuko adapter needed
- Mounting hardware that varies by SKU: wall brackets, floor/ground brackets, or balcony rail brackets
- Optional 4m MC4 extension cable bundle on three of the six SKUs

This mirrors the approach [EcoFlow's STREAM range](/blog/ecoflow-stream-review-uk) took at launch — sell the electrical core once, then let the mounting kit decide which physical install you're doing. It's a sensible way to pass G98 Type Testing once and cover garden, balcony, and wall installs without three separate certifications.

## Is it G98 compliant?

Yes — all six SKUs carry `g98GB: true` on the ENA register as of the most recent sync, published 21 September 2026. That means you can lawfully [notify your DNO](/blog/g98-dno-notification-step-by-step) using any SolarFlow 800 variant. None of the six listings carry G99, which is normal for an 800W single-phase kit — G99 only matters if you're pushing past that cap.

Crucially, Zendure submitted the *system reference*, not just the inverter in isolation. That's the detail to check whenever you're told a kit is "compliant" — a bare microinverter model number passing G98 doesn't automatically make a specific panel-and-bracket bundle compliant. Our guide on [how to spot a non-compliant plug-in solar kit](/blog/how-to-spot-non-compliant-plug-in-solar-kit-uk) covers this distinction in more depth, because it's the single most common way sellers mislead buyers.

## Mounting options compared

| SKU variant | Mounting | Extension cable | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ZENDU/21472` | 2× floor brackets | No | Garden/patio, ground-level panels |
| `ZENDU/21470` | 2× wall brackets | Yes (4m) | South wall with some distance to inverter |
| `ZENDU/21473` | 2× wall brackets | No | South wall, inverter nearby |
| `ZENDU/21469` | 2× floor brackets | Yes (4m) | Garden with inverter mounted further away |
| `ZENDU/21471` | 2× balcony brackets | Yes (4m) | Flats and balconies, panels further from socket |
| `ZENDU/21474` | 2× balcony brackets | No | Flats and balconies, panels near socket |

Note the rule on extension leads: the UK's plug-in microgenerator spec doesn't permit *generic* extension leads between the inverter and the wall socket, but a manufacturer-supplied, tested cable that's part of the certified system (like Zendure's bundled 4m MC4 cable, which runs between panel and inverter, not inverter and socket) is a different thing entirely. If you're unsure where the line sits, our [balcony solar installation guide](/blog/balcony-solar-installation-guide-uk) walks through exactly what's permitted.

## How it stacks up against the established players

Zendure enters a market that already has EcoFlow, Anker, and a fast-growing list of smaller Chinese manufacturers (Worldwide Solar, VaySunic-badged kits, TSUNESS, Perlight) all competing on the same 800W ceiling. At this wattage cap, differentiation mostly comes down to three things: warranty length, app quality, and mounting flexibility.

On mounting flexibility, Zendure's six-SKU approach is genuinely the most thorough of any manufacturer on the register — most competitors ship one or two bracket options and expect you to improvise. If you're outfitting a balcony *and* a garden shed with separate kits, having six purpose-built variants from one brand with one app is a real convenience.

On warranty and app maturity, it's too early to say — Zendure's UK retail listings hadn't gone live as of this review, and pricing wasn't yet public. Their European SolarFlow balcony battery product has a reasonable track record, which is a point in their favour. For a head-to-head against the two most established 800W kits, see our [EcoFlow vs Anker SOLIX comparison](/blog/ecoflow-stream-vs-anker-solix-rs40p-uk-2026) — Zendure will likely slot into a similar price bracket once retail listings appear.

### Does the bundled MC4 extension cable break the no-extension-lead rule?

No. The no-extension-lead rule applies to the lead between the inverter's plug and your wall socket — not to manufacturer-supplied, tested cabling between the solar panel and the inverter itself, which is part of the certified system.

### How does the SolarFlow 800 compare to EcoFlow's STREAM range on warranty?

Zendure hadn't published UK warranty terms at review time. EcoFlow's STREAM carries a 10-year inverter warranty, which is currently the benchmark to beat in this category.
