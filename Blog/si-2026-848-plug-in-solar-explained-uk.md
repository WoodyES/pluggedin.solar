## What Is SI 2026 No. 848?

SI 2026 No. 848 is a **statutory instrument** — a piece of secondary legislation made under powers granted by the Consumer Rights Act 2015. It was made on **16 July 2026** and comes into force on **27 August 2026**.

Its full title is *The Plugs and Sockets etc. (Safety) (Amendment) Regulations 2026*, and it amends the Plugs and Sockets etc. (Safety) Regulations 1994 to create a legal framework for plug-in solar devices.

Before this change, plugging any generating device into a standard 13A socket was technically illegal under UK product safety law. SI 2026/848 creates a specific exemption for devices meeting the [Interim Product Specification](/blog/interim-product-specification-v2-plug-in-solar-uk).

## What Does It Change?

The original 1994 regulations only contemplated plugs as *consumers* of electricity. SI 2026/848 introduces the concept of a [plug-in microgenerator](/blog/plug-in-microgenerator-uk-what-is-it) — a device that *generates* electricity and feeds it back through a BS 1363 plug.

Key provisions:

- **Definition**: A "plug-in microgenerator" is a device that generates electricity from a renewable source and connects to the fixed wiring via a BS 1363 plug
- **Power limit**: Maximum 800VA / 3.5A AC output
- **Product standard**: Must comply with the DESNZ Interim Product Specification (v2, published 16 July 2026)
- **Fuse requirement**: 5A fuse in the BS 1363 plug (not the standard 13A)
- **One per household**: Only one plug-in microgenerator per domestic premises

## How Does This Relate to BS 7671?

[BS 7671 Amendment 4](/blog/bs7671-amendment-4-explained) (published April 2026) updated the wiring regulations to accommodate plug-in solar at the electrical installation level. SI 2026/848 addresses the *product safety* side — making it legal to sell and use the plug itself.

Together, these two changes create the complete legal framework:

- **BS 7671 Amendment 4**: Allows the electrical installation to accept power from a plug-in device
- **SI 2026/848**: Makes the plug-in device itself legal to sell and use

## The Interim Product Specification v2

SI 2026/848 references the [DESNZ Interim Product Specification v2](/blog/interim-product-specification-v2-plug-in-solar-uk), which sets out the detailed technical requirements for compliant kits. This is not a British Standard — it's a government-published specification that will eventually be replaced by a full BSI product standard.

Key requirements from the IPS v2:

- Maximum AC output: 800VA / 3.5A
- Maximum DC input: 2000W
- BS 1363 plug with 5A fuse
- No battery storage in the plug circuit
- No connection via extension lead
- Built-in anti-islanding protection
- Automatic disconnection on power failure

## Who Made This Decision?

The statutory instrument was made by **Martin McCluskey**, Minister for Energy Consumers at DESNZ, following a public consultation that ran from June to 30 June 2026.

The [government's March 2026 announcement](/blog/uk-government-plug-in-solar-announcement-2026) set the direction, and the consultation process refined the technical details. The final instrument reflects input from industry, consumer groups, and electrical safety bodies.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is SI 2026/848 the same as the BSI product standard?

No. SI 2026/848 is the *law* that makes plug-in solar legal. The Interim Product Specification v2 is the *technical standard* that kits must meet. BSI will eventually publish a full product standard to replace the interim spec.

### Does this apply in Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland?

SI 2026/848 applies to England, Wales, and Scotland. [Northern Ireland](/blog/plug-in-solar-northern-ireland-guide) has separate product safety regulations and may need its own legislative change.

### What happens if I buy a non-compliant kit?

It remains illegal to sell a non-compliant plug-in solar device in the UK after 27 August. Enforcement focuses on retailers, not individual consumers. However, non-compliant kits won't carry the required safety certifications.
