## The Case for Seasonal Adjustment

The optimal panel angle changes with the seasons because the sun's height changes:

| Season | Optimal tilt (52°N latitude) | Sun height at noon |
|--------|----------------------------|-------------------|
| Summer (Jun) | 15-25° | 62° |
| Spring/Autumn (Mar/Sep) | 35-40° | 38° |
| Winter (Dec) | 55-65° | 15° |

A fixed panel at 35° tilt is a compromise. Adjusting seasonally can boost annual output by **10-15%**.

## Practical Adjustment Schedule

You don't need to adjust monthly. Three changes per year capture most of the benefit:

| When | Set tilt to | Reason |
|------|------------|--------|
| March | 30° | Spring sun getting higher |
| June | 20° | Summer sun at peak height |
| October | 55° | Autumn/winter sun low on horizon |

## How to Adjust

If using [adjustable tilt brackets](/blog/best-adjustable-solar-panel-tilt-brackets-uk):

1. Loosen the tilt adjustment bolts (usually wing nuts or quick-release)
2. Adjust the panel to the desired angle
3. Use a phone inclinometer app to verify the angle
4. Retighten the bolts firmly — panels can catch wind

## Is It Worth the Effort?

| Strategy | Annual kWh (800W, Midlands) | vs fixed at 35° |
|----------|---------------------------|------------------|
| Fixed at 35° (year-round) | 850 | — |
| 3× seasonal adjustment | 935-960 | +10-13% |
| Monthly adjustment | 960-980 | +13-15% |

Three seasonal adjustments add roughly **85-110 kWh/year** — worth £20-27 in extra savings. Monthly adjustment adds little beyond the three-times approach.
