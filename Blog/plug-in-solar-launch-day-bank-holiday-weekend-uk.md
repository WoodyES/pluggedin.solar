The timing is almost too neat to be an accident. [SI 2026 No. 848](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk) — the statutory instrument that makes plug-in solar legal across the UK — comes into force on **Thursday 27 August 2026**. Four days later, on **Monday 31 August**, most of the country gets the last bank holiday of the summer. If you've been waiting for the law to change before buying a kit, that four-day gap is your window to go from "not yet legal" to "generating your own electricity" in a single long weekend.

This isn't a coincidence you need to plan around carefully — it's a gift of a schedule. Retailers have had stock sitting in warehouses for weeks, waiting for the go-ahead. The moment the legislation takes effect, [B&Q, Currys, Screwfix, Wickes, Lidl and Amazon](/blog/retailers-stocking-plug-in-solar-uk-2026) can sell compliant kits without any legal ambiguity, and you'll have a free Thursday-to-Monday stretch (plus the weekend either side, depending on your work pattern) to unbox, mount and switch on a system before autumn properly sets in.

Here's how to actually use those four days.

## Thursday 27 August: legal day

This is the day the law changes — not the day you need to panic-buy. If you've already picked your kit during the run-up (see our [13-day countdown checklist](/blog/plug-in-solar-13-days-countdown-checklist-uk) if you're catching up late), Thursday is simply the day you can legally place the order and have a retailer legally sell it to you as a **plug-in microgenerator** rather than in the odd grey area kits have occupied until now.

Realistically, most people won't have a system on their wall by Thursday evening. Click-and-collect slots, courier delivery windows and stock levels at your local store all play a part. Treat Thursday as "order day," not "install day," unless you're collecting in person from a store with confirmed stock.

What to do on the day itself:
- Place your order online or collect in-store if stock allows
- Double-check the kit's spec sheet lists 800VA / 3.5A max output, a BS 1363 plug, and a 5A fuse — these are the legal limits under the new rules, not marketing numbers
- Confirm there's no battery built into the plug circuit itself (battery storage has to sit downstream of a separate, compliant connection, not spliced into the microgenerator plug)
- If you're going straight to an outdoor socket, read our guide on [which socket to actually use](/blog/plug-in-solar-which-socket-to-use-uk) before you commit to a run of cable

## Friday: the mounting day

Friday is when most kits will actually land, especially if you ordered for delivery rather than collection. This is your prep and mounting day — the unglamorous half of the job that's much easier done without weekend crowds or rain forecasts hanging over you.

Use Friday to:
- Check the delivery is complete and undamaged before you start drilling or clamping anything
- Confirm mounting location and angle — south-facing is ideal at this point in the year, but [east/west-facing setups](/blog/east-west-facing-plug-in-solar-uk) still perform reasonably well through early autumn
- Fit brackets, ground mounts or balcony rail clamps according to the manufacturer's instructions
- Route the cable to your chosen socket without any extension leads — the rules are explicit that the panel-to-plug cable supplied with the kit is the only cable permitted, full stop

Leave the actual plugging-in for Saturday if you're not confident you'll finish mounting with daylight to spare. Rushing the last hour of a mounting job in the dark is how brackets end up loose.

## Saturday: switch-on and DNO notification

Saturday is switch-on day. Plug the unit into your chosen socket, open the manufacturer's app (if the kit has one — the [EcoFlow STREAM](/blog/ecoflow-stream-review-uk) and most competing systems do), and confirm it's registering generation. On a clear late-August day you should see meaningful output within minutes.

The other job for Saturday — and it's a legal one, not optional — is your **G98 notification** to your electricity network operator (DNO). This is a standard form confirming a small-scale generator has been connected to the network. It's a formality for a system this size, but it's still required. Our [step-by-step G98 guide](/blog/g98-dno-notification-step-by-step) walks through exactly what to submit and to which DNO, based on your postcode.

Don't skip this because it's the weekend. Most DNO notification portals are online and accept submissions any day of the week — there's no reason to leave it until Monday.

## Sunday: settle in and check the app

Sunday is a good day to do nothing more than watch how the system behaves through a full day of UK late-summer weather — cloud cover, a few sunny spells, maybe a shower. This is useful baseline data. If output looks unexpectedly low compared to what the kit's rating suggests, it's much easier to troubleshoot mounting angle or shading now than in three weeks when you've stopped paying attention.

Cross-check what you're seeing against your smart meter if you have one — our guide on [reading your smart meter alongside plug-in solar](/blog/how-to-read-smart-meter-plug-in-solar-uk) explains what the export and import figures should actually mean.

## Monday 31 August: bank holiday, and your system's first full week begins

By the bank holiday itself, your kit should be mounted, registered and generating. Monday is a good day to do the small jobs that don't need urgency: cable tidying, checking IP68 gland seals are properly seated, and reading through our [first week setup guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-first-week-setup-guide-uk) for what to expect over the next seven days as the novelty wears off and the system settles into normal operation.

It's also worth a final compliance check now that everything's connected. With demand for kits about to spike, some sellers will inevitably try to move older, non-compliant stock. Our guide on [spotting a non-compliant plug-in solar kit](/blog/how-to-spot-non-compliant-plug-in-solar-kit-uk) is worth ten minutes if you bought from anywhere other than one of the major retailers.

## Why this weekend specifically matters

Late August isn't peak solar season — that was June and July — but it's still a strong window. Days are shorter than midsummer but the panels aren't yet fighting low winter sun angles either, and with the [Ofgem price cap sitting around 24.5p/kWh](/blog/plug-in-solar-energy-price-cap-q4-2026-uk), every kWh a plug-in system generates through September is a kWh you're not paying for at that rate. Getting installed this particular weekend, rather than waiting until October, buys you a full extra month of decent generation before the clocks change.

If budget is a concern, it's also worth checking whether you qualify for the [£25m government pilot scheme](/blog/25m-government-pilot-plug-in-solar-uk) aimed at low-income households — applications don't need to wait for the bank holiday and can be submitted in parallel with a self-funded purchase decision.

### Do I have to install on launch day itself?

No. The law simply makes plug-in solar legal to sell and use from 27 August 2026 onward — there's no deadline attached to when you personally have to buy or install one. The bank holiday weekend is just a practical, convenient window for people who want to move quickly, not a requirement.

### What if my chosen retailer is out of stock over the bank holiday?

Demand is expected to spike hard in the first week. If your first-choice retailer sells out, check our [retailer stock guide](/blog/retailers-stocking-plug-in-solar-uk-2026) for alternatives, or wait a week or two — stock typically replenishes quickly once initial demand settles, and a slightly later install still captures most of September's generation.

### Do I still need to submit a G98 notification if I only install for the weekend and don't use it much at first?

Yes. The G98 notification requirement is based on the system being connected to your home's electrical network, not on how much you use it. Submit it as soon as the unit is plugged in and generating, regardless of how quickly you get around to relying on it day to day.
