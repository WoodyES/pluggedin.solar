The **Energy Networks Association (ENA)** maintains the official register of grid-code compliant plug-in solar devices for Great Britain. Every kit that has passed G98 or G99 testing appears here — and if a kit *isn't* here, your DNO has grounds to object to your G98 notification. This page auto-syncs from the [ENA Connect Direct registry](https://connect-direct.energynetworks.org/device-databases/search-gen?device_type_id=14&compliance_status_id=Compliant) every day so what you see is what the network operators actually accept.

## What "compliant" means (in plain English)

To sell a plug-in solar kit into the UK market under [SI 2026 No. 848](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk), the inverter must pass:

- **G98 Type Test** — the grid-code compliance test for single-phase generators up to 16A per phase. The overwhelmingly common route for plug-in solar's 800VA / 3.5A cap.
- **G99** (optional) — the grid-code test for larger generators; some plug-in kits carry it too for future-proofing above 800W.

The manufacturer submits test evidence to a Type Test Verification body, and if the kit passes it goes on this ENA register. **Only kits on this list can be lawfully notified via G98 to a DNO in Great Britain.** Buying a kit not on the list means your DNO can refuse the notification and require removal.

For the wider legality picture see the [SI 2026/848 explainer](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk), the [G98 step-by-step notification guide](/blog/g98-dno-notification-step-by-step) and the [interim product specification](/blog/plug-in-solar-interim-product-specification-uk).

## What to do with the registry

**Before you buy any UK plug-in solar kit**, do this 60-second check:

1. Note the exact model name and part number from the vendor's product page.
2. Ctrl-F the table above (or search the [official ENA registry](https://connect-direct.energynetworks.org/device-databases/search-gen?device_type_id=14&compliance_status_id=Compliant)) for the manufacturer name.
3. Match the specific model + inverter combination against a listed entry.
4. Save the registry reference (e.g. `SOLAW/21640/V1`) — you'll enter it on the G98 form when you notify your DNO.

For the actual notification process see the [G98 step-by-step guide](/blog/g98-dno-notification-step-by-step). If the DNO ever pushes back on your submission, the ENA registry reference is your first line of defence.

For the wider context see the [UK plug-in solar complete guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-uk-complete-guide) and the [interim product specification](/blog/plug-in-solar-interim-product-specification-uk).

### What happens if I buy a kit that isn't on the ENA register?

Your DNO can refuse the G98 notification, in which case you'd be running the kit without permission — a technical breach of your electricity supply agreement. Practical consequence varies: some DNOs actively check, most don't unless there's a service issue. Buying only listed kits is the safe path; buying unlisted (like EcoFlow STREAM currently) is a calculated risk that resolves the moment the manufacturer completes certification.

### How do manufacturers get on the ENA registry?

They submit type-test evidence (G98 pass) to a Type Test Verification body, who submits to ENA on their behalf. Process typically takes 2–4 months from initial submission. Big-name brands like EcoFlow and Anker are currently working through this pipeline for their UK-specific SKUs.
