If [November felt thin](/blog/plug-in-solar-november-generation-uk), December is where UK plug-in solar generation bottoms out completely. The winter solstice falls in the final week of the month, daylight hours are at their shortest of the year, and low winter sun combined with the UK's cloudiest, greyest weeks means even a well-sited 800W kit can look like it's barely working. It isn't broken — this is simply the trough of a very predictable annual curve. Here's what to expect and how to keep getting value out of your system until generation climbs again from late January.

## What an 800W system actually produces in December

Across most of the UK, a well-sited 800W plug-in kit typically produces between 0.3 and 0.8 kWh per day in December, noticeably below even November's reduced figures covered in our [November generation guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-november-generation-uk). The [January guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-january-uk-generation) shows output starting to recover slightly by month-end as days lengthen again, but December itself is usually the single lowest month of the year.

| Region | Daily average (kWh) | Monthly total (kWh) |
|---|---|---|
| South England | 0.6–0.8 | 18–25 |
| Midlands | 0.5–0.7 | 15–21 |
| North England | 0.4–0.6 | 12–18 |
| Wales | 0.45–0.65 | 14–20 |
| Scotland | 0.3–0.5 | 9–15 |

These figures assume a clean, unshaded, south-facing panel at a steep winter angle. Cloudy, east/west-facing or partially shaded installs will sit at or below the lower end. If you want numbers specific to your postcode rather than regional averages, the site [calculator](/calculator) is worth running again now that the season has changed.

## Why December is the hardest month, not just the shortest

It isn't only the shorter daylight hours — December combines the year's lowest sun angle with the UK's most persistent cloud cover and, in many regions, the highest rainfall of the year. The sun in southern England barely climbs above 15 degrees at midday around the solstice, which means shadows from fences, neighbouring buildings and even bare trees reach further across a garden or balcony than at any other point in the year. A panel that sat in full sun all summer and autumn can end up partially shaded for hours during December without anything having physically changed except the sun's position. If your numbers look worse than the regional averages above even accounting for the season, it's worth a quick check against our [troubleshooting guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-not-generating-troubleshooting-uk) before assuming it's simply winter.

## Get the angle as steep as it goes

This is the one month where a steep tilt bracket setting earns its keep most clearly. At UK latitudes in December, an angle of 55–65° from horizontal tracks the low solar arc far more effectively than the 30–35° that suits summer months. If your kit's bracket has a maximum steep setting, December is when you want it fully engaged — our [panel angle guide](/blog/how-to-angle-solar-panels-maximum-output-uk) covers the adjustment in more detail, and it's a five-minute job that can make a genuine difference to a month where every fraction of a kWh counts.

## Is a battery worth anything in December?

With daily generation this low, a battery adds relatively little value in December compared to the shoulder seasons — on heavily overcast days there's often barely enough surplus to make banking it worthwhile. That said, the UK does still get the occasional crisp, clear, cold December day with surprisingly strong output for the season, and on those days a battery lets you shift the midday surplus to cover evening lighting and standby loads rather than losing it to export. ## Check your kit is still ENA-compliant before any January upgrades

If you're thinking about adding a second panel or swapping components over the Christmas break, it's worth checking that whatever you're buying still appears on the [ENA-compliant plug-in solar device registry](/blog/uk-compliant-plug-in-solar-devices-live) before you order. New manufacturers have continued to gain G98 approval since launch, and buying something that isn't listed risks falling foul of the SI 2026 No. 848 compliance rules even if the retailer doesn't flag it.

## Don't disconnect anything over the holidays

It can be tempting to assume a system producing under half a kWh a day isn't worth the bother, but there's no reason to disconnect a plug-in kit over winter. Even at the Ofgem price cap of roughly 24.5p/kWh, 0.4 kWh a day is still free energy that costs nothing once the kit is installed, and leaving it connected means you're ready to capture the clear, cold December days that do turn up most years. Keep the glass clean, keep the angle steep, and treat this month as the floor of the annual cycle rather than a sign something's wrong. If you want a longer view of what to expect through the rest of the season, our [winter preparation guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-winter-preparation-2026-uk) covers the full picture.

### Is December the worst month of the year for plug-in solar in the UK?

Yes, in most regions December produces the lowest daily and monthly output of the year, thanks to the combination of the shortest daylight hours, the lowest sun angle, and typically heavy cloud cover. Generation begins recovering gradually from late January onward.

### Should I bother cleaning my panels in December if output is already so low?

Yes — proportionally, dirt and grime block a larger share of December's already-weak light than they would on a bright summer day, so a quick wipe after any heavy rain, leaf fall or frost is still worth doing for the output you do get.

### Will cold weather damage my plug-in solar panel over December?

No. Photovoltaic panels are designed to handle UK winter temperatures and typically perform slightly more efficiently in the cold than in summer heat. The main December risks are physical — secure mounting against winter winds and clearing any snow or ice buildup matter more than the cold itself.
