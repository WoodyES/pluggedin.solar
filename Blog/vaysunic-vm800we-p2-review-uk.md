If you've been tracking the [ENA compliant device registry](/blog/uk-compliant-plug-in-solar-devices-live) over the past couple of months, you'll have noticed a new micro-inverter name appearing alongside the usual Hoymiles and APsystems entries: the VaySunic VM800WE-P2. It's not sold as a standalone product to most buyers — instead it ships bundled inside Worldwide Solar's plug-in kits, paired with AIKO Neostar panels — but since it's the actual grid-interactive component doing the compliance work, it's worth understanding on its own terms before you buy a kit built around it.

## What the VaySunic VM800WE-P2 actually is

The VM800WE-P2 is an 800W-class dual-channel micro-inverter, functionally similar in role to the more established [Hoymiles HMS-800-2WB](/blog/hoymiles-hms800-review-uk) and [APsystems EZ1-M](/blog/apsystems-ez1-m-review-uk) units already on the market. It accepts input from two solar panels, synchronises with UK mains frequency and voltage, and limits output to the 800VA / 3.5A ceiling required under the plug-in microgenerator spec. It's G98-listed for Great Britain, which means it's passed the Energy Networks Association's compliance testing for small-scale grid-connected generation — the same bar every legitimate plug-in solar inverter has to clear.

## Where you'll actually encounter it

Unlike Hoymiles or APsystems units, which some retailers sell as standalone components for DIY kit builders, the VaySunic VM800WE-P2 currently only appears bundled into complete kits — specifically Worldwide Solar's plug-in bundles, which pair it with AIKO Neostar monofacial panels and, in some listings, a mounting system. If you're comparing the full kit rather than the inverter in isolation, see our [Worldwide Solar Plug & Play review](/blog/worldwide-solar-plug-and-play-kit-review-uk) for the complete package breakdown including panel specs and pricing.

## How it compares on paper

Against the two most established micro-inverters on the UK market:

- **Hoymiles HMS-800-2WB** — the most widely deployed dual-channel unit in UK plug-in kits, with a long track record and broad app/monitoring support. See our [Hoymiles HMS800 review](/blog/hoymiles-hms800-review-uk).
- **APsystems EZ1-M** — a strong alternative with its own dedicated monitoring app, reviewed in our [APsystems EZ1-M review](/blog/apsystems-ez1-m-review-uk).
- **VaySunic VM800WE-P2** — newer to the registry, bundled rather than sold solo, and with a much shorter real-world track record in UK installations as a result.

On raw specification — 800W output, dual MPPT-style channel handling, G98 compliance — there's no meaningful gap between any of these three. The real differentiator for most buyers is track record and app ecosystem maturity, where Hoymiles and APsystems currently have a head start simply by being established longer. ## Should you buy a kit built around it?

Being newly listed isn't a red flag by itself — every micro-inverter on the registry had to clear the same G98 testing regardless of how long it's been on the list, and our guide on [spotting a non-compliant plug-in solar kit](/blog/how-to-spot-non-compliant-plug-in-solar-kit-uk) explains what actually matters: registry listing, correct fusing, and BS 1363 plug termination, not brand age. That said, if you'd rather buy from a micro-inverter with several years of UK deployment data and a mature monitoring app behind it, Hoymiles or APsystems-based kits are the lower-risk choice today. If you're comfortable being an early adopter of a newer component backed by full ENA compliance, the VaySunic-based Worldwide Solar kits are a legitimate option at a competitive price point.

Either way, always cross-check the exact model reference against the [live ENA registry](/blog/uk-compliant-plug-in-solar-devices-live) before buying, since kit bundles and part numbers do change as manufacturers update firmware and hardware revisions.

### Is the VaySunic VM800WE-P2 ENA-compliant?

Yes. It's listed on the Energy Networks Association's registry with G98 compliance for Great Britain, which means it has passed the required testing for safe grid-connected operation within the plug-in microgenerator spec.

### Can I buy the VaySunic VM800WE-P2 as a standalone inverter?

Not currently through mainstream UK retailers — it's sold bundled inside Worldwide Solar's complete plug-in kits alongside AIKO Neostar panels, rather than as a separate component for DIY builds.

### Is a newly-listed micro-inverter riskier than an established one like Hoymiles?

Not in terms of compliance — both have cleared the same G98 testing. The practical difference is track record: established brands have longer real-world deployment history and more mature monitoring apps, while newer entries simply haven't accumulated that history yet.
