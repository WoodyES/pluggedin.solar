Aberdeen doesn't come up often in plug-in solar conversations, and when it does, the first reaction is usually "surely it's too far north to be worth it." It's a fair question — the Granite City sits at roughly 57°N, well above London's 51.5°N, and the sunshine hours reflect that. But the answer isn't a flat no, and the specifics of living in Aberdeen (tenement flats, North Sea exposure, oil-and-gas shift work patterns) change the calculation in ways that latitude alone doesn't capture.

## How much sun does Aberdeen actually get?

Aberdeen averages around 1,300–1,400 hours of sunshine a year — slightly below the UK average and meaningfully below the south coast's 1,900+ hours. Winter days are genuinely short: sunrise after 8:30am and sunset before 3:45pm around the solstice. That's the honest starting point.

What the raw sunshine-hours figure misses is that Aberdeen's air is clean and often clear in spring and early summer, and long summer daylight (close to 18 hours around midsummer) partly offsets the low sun angle. An 800W plug-in kit here will generate noticeably less than the same kit in Cornwall or Kent, but it isn't generating nothing. For context on what a kit like this produces in better conditions, our guide on [how much electricity an 800W solar setup generates](/blog/how-much-electricity-does-800w-solar-generate-uk) is worth reading alongside this one — treat Aberdeen's output as 15-25% below the UK average rather than a separate calculation entirely.

## Tenement flats and balcony access

A large share of Aberdeen's housing stock is traditional granite tenement flats, many with shared back greens rather than private gardens, and balconies that are less common than in newer-build cities. If you're in a tenement without a private outdoor space, your realistic options are a south or west-facing window ledge mount (where the landlord or factor permits it) or negotiating access to a shared green — which usually means a conversation with neighbours and, in a factored block, the property factor.

Our [guide for renters without gardens](/blog/plug-in-solar-flats-no-garden-uk) covers window and balcony options in detail, and if you're renting rather than owning, the [renters' guide to plug-in solar](/blog/plug-in-solar-for-renters-uk) is essential reading before you buy anything — Scottish tenancy rules on fixtures differ slightly from the Renters' Rights Act provisions that apply south of the border.

## North Sea wind is the real design constraint

Aberdeen's exposure to North Sea weather systems means sustained wind speeds and gusts well above the UK average, particularly October through March. This matters more for plug-in solar than low sun angle does. Ground-mounted frames need serious ballast, and anything on a balcony rail needs properly rated clamps rather than improvised fixings.

- **Add 25-30% more ballast weight** than a standard inland recommendation for any ground or A-frame mount.
- **Avoid flimsy pound-shop cable ties and brackets** — Aberdeen's gusts will find every weak point within a season.
- **Check fixings monthly through winter**, not just at the twice-yearly standard interval.

If you're specifically dealing with coastal salt alongside the wind — much of Aberdeen is within a mile or two of the sea — our piece on [coastal areas and salt corrosion](/blog/plug-in-solar-coastal-areas-salt-corrosion-uk) covers the marine-grade fixings worth paying extra for.

## Is it legal and do I need to tell anyone?

Yes to both, and the rules don't change north of the border. Plug-in solar became legal across the whole of the UK, including Scotland, on 27 August 2026 under SI 2026 No. 848, which created the "plug-in microgenerator" category: 800VA/3.5A maximum, a standard BS 1363 plug, a 5A fuse, no battery in the plug circuit, and no extension leads. Our full explainer on [SI 2026/848](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk) breaks down the specification if you want the detail.

Aberdeen sits within SSEN (Scottish and Southern Electricity Networks) distribution territory, not National Grid's area that covers much of England. You still need to submit a G98 notification — the process is the same standard online form regardless of which DNO covers your postcode. Our [step-by-step G98 guide](/blog/g98-dno-notification-step-by-step) walks through exactly what to submit.

## Does the maths still work with less sun?

Payback takes longer in Aberdeen than in southern England, simply because there's less generation to offset against your bill. But Scottish electricity prices sit under the same UK-wide Ofgem price cap (around 24.5p/kWh as of autumn 2026) as everywhere else, so every kWh you don't buy from the grid is worth the same amount whether you're in Aberdeen or Brighton — you just generate fewer of them per year.

As a rough comparison: if a well-sited south coast installation pays back in 4-5 years, expect something closer to 5.5-7 years for a typical Aberdeen setup, longer again for a shaded or poorly oriented one. It's still a positive-return purchase over the kit's working life, just a slower one. Our [800W payback calculator guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-payback-calculator-800w-uk) lets you run your own postcode-adjusted numbers rather than relying on a national average.

If you're comparing Aberdeen to other Scottish cities before deciding where it's worth prioritising, [Edinburgh](/blog/plug-in-solar-edinburgh-guide-uk) and [Glasgow](/blog/plug-in-solar-glasgow-guide-uk) both get broadly similar sun hours to Aberdeen, and our wider [is plug-in solar worth it in Scotland](/blog/plug-in-solar-scotland-worth-it) piece covers the national picture.

### Is plug-in solar worth it in Aberdeen given the latitude?

Yes, but with realistic expectations. You'll generate 15-25% less than the UK average and meaningfully less than southern England, so payback takes longer — typically 5.5-7 years rather than 4-5. It's still a net-positive purchase, just a slower one, and summer's long daylight hours partly compensate for winter's short ones.

### What's the biggest practical challenge for Aberdeen installations?

Wind, not sun. North Sea exposure means sustained high winds that demand heavier ballast, sturdier fixings, and more frequent fixing checks than a typical inland UK installation needs. Coastal salt corrosion is a secondary factor worth planning for if you're near the water.

### Do tenement flats in Aberdeen support plug-in solar?

Many do, via window ledges, balconies where present, or shared back greens with factor/neighbour agreement, but it depends heavily on the specific building. Check your tenancy or ownership terms and talk to your factor before buying if you don't have clear private outdoor access.
