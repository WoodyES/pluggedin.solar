## The Short Answer

Yes — you should notify your home insurer when you install plug-in solar. Most policies require you to inform them of any changes to the property or its contents that could affect risk.

## Why Notify?

- **Policy compliance**: Failing to disclose material changes can void your cover
- **Contents coverage**: Your plug-in solar kit is a valuable item (£400-1,000)
- **Liability**: If the system caused damage to the property, undisclosed modifications could affect your claim
- **It's easy**: A quick phone call or email is all it takes

## What Insurers Typically Say

Most home insurers accept plug-in solar without any premium increase because:

- It's a portable consumer product, not a permanent modification
- It plugs into a standard 13A socket
- Certified kits meet the [DESNZ Interim Product Specification](/blog/interim-product-specification-v2-plug-in-solar-uk) safety requirements
- It's [legal from 27 August 2026](/blog/plug-in-solar-legal-27-august-2026-uk)

## Template Notification Letter

*Dear [Insurance Company],*

*I am writing to inform you that I have installed a plug-in solar system at [address]. The system is a [brand/model], certified to the DESNZ Interim Product Specification under SI 2026 No. 848. It generates up to 800W of electricity and connects to a standard 13A wall socket via a BS 1363 plug with a 5A fuse.*

*The system is portable (not permanently fixed to the building), does not modify the fixed wiring, and was installed without an electrician as designed by the manufacturer.*

*Please confirm whether this affects my policy terms or premium. The system's replacement value is approximately [£X].*

*Yours faithfully,*
*[Your name]*

## What If Your Insurer Objects?

This is unlikely but possible. If your insurer adds an exclusion or increases premium significantly:

1. Ask specifically what their concern is
2. Explain that certified kits meet government safety standards
3. Get quotes from other insurers — most won't have an issue
4. Check our [insurance guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-and-home-insurance-uk) for detailed advice

### Will my premium go up?

Unlikely. Most insurers treat plug-in solar the same as any other portable appliance. A few may add a small loading (£10-20/year).

### Should renters notify their contents insurer?

Yes. Your contents policy should cover the solar kit as a valuable portable item. The [landlord's buildings insurance](/blog/plug-in-solar-landlords-uk) isn't your responsibility.
