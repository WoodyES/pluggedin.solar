Swindon doesn't get talked about as a solar town the way Bristol or Oxford do, but it has two things working in its favour: a genuinely large stock of new-build estates with decent garden and driveway space, and a sunshine total that sits comfortably above the UK average thanks to its position on the Wiltshire–Thames Valley border. For plug-in solar specifically — the 800VA, socket-connected kits that became legal nationwide on 27 August 2026 — that combination makes Swindon one of the more straightforward places in the country to get a system up and generating.

## Sunshine and generation in Swindon

Swindon gets around 1,500–1,550 hours of sunshine a year, noticeably above the UK average of roughly 1,400 hours and in line with the wider South West and Thames Valley corridor. For what that actually means in kWh terms, our guide on [how much electricity an 800W solar setup generates](/blog/how-much-electricity-does-800w-solar-generate-uk) breaks down the maths by season. Many of Swindon's newer estates were built on a fairly uniform grid of south or south-west facing rear gardens, which is close to ideal, but older streets around the town centre and the Victorian railway village can run east-west — if that's your situation, our piece on [east vs west facing installations](/blog/east-west-facing-plug-in-solar-uk) explains the trade-off and why it's rarely a dealbreaker.

## New-build estates: Swindon's biggest advantage

Developments like Wichelstowe, Haydon End, Tadpole Garden Village, and the various phases around the old Nationwide and Rodbourne sites have added thousands of new homes to Swindon over the past decade, most with modern rear gardens, side access, and in many cases an allocated driveway or parking court. That matters for plug-in solar because it means two of the biggest installation obstacles elsewhere — no outdoor socket, and no safe ground space for a panel — are rarely a problem here. Our guide to [plug-in solar on new-build homes](/blog/plug-in-solar-new-build-homes-uk) covers the specific checks worth doing before you buy, including confirming your outdoor socket is on its own circuit rather than shared with an EV charger or hot tub.

## Semis, terraces, and the older housing stock

Away from the new estates, Swindon's older housing is dominated by 1930s–1960s semi-detached streets (particularly around Walcot, Park North, and Penhill) alongside the smaller Victorian terraces of the original railway village near the station. Both suit plug-in solar, just with different considerations. Semis typically have more garden space and can usually take a ground-mounted or fence-mounted kit without much trouble — see our [semi-detached guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-semi-detached-uk) for layout tips. The Victorian terraces have smaller gardens and more shared boundary walls, so a balcony rail or wall-mounted kit, as covered in our [terraced houses guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-terraced-houses-uk), is often the more practical fit.

## Legal status and notifying your DNO

The legal position in Swindon is the same as everywhere else in Great Britain. Plug-in solar — officially the "plug-in microgenerator" category — became legal on 27 August 2026 under [SI 2026 No. 848](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk), capped at 800VA/3.5A through a standard BS 1363 plug, with a 5A fuse, no battery permitted in the plug circuit, and no extension leads between the unit and the socket. Swindon sits in National Grid Electricity Distribution's SWEB licence area, and you'll need to submit a G98 notification before switching the system on — our [step-by-step G98 guide](/blog/g98-dno-notification-step-by-step) walks through the online form.

## Buying a kit that's actually compliant

Before buying anything, it's worth checking the specific inverter model against the official [ENA compliant devices registry](/blog/uk-compliant-plug-in-solar-devices-live) — only kits with a passed G98 type test can be lawfully notified to your DNO, and the list is updated regularly as new manufacturers get their kits approved. Several newer entrants, including Solarfy and various "plug and play" kits built around Hoymiles and VaySunic microinverters, have been added to the registry in recent months, widening the choice beyond the handful of brands that dominated the market at launch. If a retailer can't point you to the registry entry for the exact model they're selling, treat that as a warning sign rather than a technicality.

## Renting in Swindon

Swindon has a sizeable private rental market, particularly around the town centre and near the Great Western Hospital employment corridor. If you're a tenant rather than a homeowner, our guide to [plug-in solar for renters](/blog/plug-in-solar-for-renters-uk) covers what you can install without needing permanent alteration or landlord sign-off for structural work — a ground or balcony kit that simply plugs into an existing outdoor socket is usually the simplest route.

## The bottom line

Swindon's combination of above-average sunshine, a large stock of new-build homes with usable outdoor space, and straightforward access to SWEB's DNO notification process makes it one of the more frictionless places in the UK to get a plug-in solar kit running. The main homework is the same as anywhere: confirm the model you're buying is on the ENA compliant registry, check your outdoor socket's circuit, and pick a mount type that suits whether you're on a new estate or one of the town's older semis and terraces.

### Does Swindon get enough sunshine for plug-in solar to be worth it?

Yes — Swindon's roughly 1,500 hours of sunshine a year sits above the UK average, making it a solid location for an 800W plug-in system regardless of whether you're on a new estate or an older street.

### Which DNO covers Swindon for G98 notification?

Swindon falls under National Grid Electricity Distribution's SWEB licence area. Our [G98 notification guide](/blog/g98-dno-notification-step-by-step) covers the online form and what information you'll need.

### Are Swindon's new-build gardens suitable for ground-mounted kits?

Generally yes — most new estates in Swindon were built with usable rear gardens and outdoor sockets, which suits ground or fence-mounted plug-in kits well. Just confirm the socket isn't shared with another high-draw appliance before committing.
