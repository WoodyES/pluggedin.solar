If you're weighing up an 800W plug-in solar kit, the savings on your electricity bill are usually the headline reason to buy one. But there's a second benefit that gets far less attention: what it does to your home's Energy Performance Certificate (EPC). With EPC ratings increasingly tied to mortgage deals, rental compliance and resale value, it's worth understanding exactly how a plug-in system feeds into that score — and where its limits are.

This guide covers how EPC assessments treat solar generation, what a realistic rating uplift looks like for an 800W system, and why landlords in particular should be paying attention.

## What is an EPC, and why does it matter more in 2026?

Every home sold or let in England, Wales and Scotland needs an Energy Performance Certificate, rating the property from A (most efficient) to G (least efficient) on a scale of 1-100+ points. It's produced using the government's RdSAP methodology, which assesses insulation, heating systems, glazing, and on-site renewable generation.

EPC ratings have become more consequential over the past few years for three reasons: mortgage lenders increasingly offer "green" rate discounts for C-rated homes and above, some estate agents now flag EPC bands prominently in listings, and the Warm Homes Plan has put a firm deadline on landlords. From October 2030, most rented properties in England and Wales will need to hit at least a C rating, with penalties for non-compliance and an investment cap of £10,000 per property under the current proposals. That's a hard deadline that's now only a few years away, and it's pushing landlords to look at every efficiency measure available — including plug-in solar.

## How solar generation affects your EPC score

RdSAP assessors record any on-site electricity generation as a factor that reduces your modelled energy costs and carbon emissions, both of which feed into the overall score. Full rooftop solar PV installations are well understood by assessors and can be worth a meaningful jump — some studies suggest an average uplift of around 18 points, enough to move a typical home from a D to a C in some cases.

Plug-in solar is newer territory. Because plug-in microgenerators (the legal term introduced under SI 2026 No. 848, effective 27 August 2026) connect via a standard 13A socket rather than a fixed rooftop installation with a G98/G99 DNO notification on record, EPC assessors don't yet have a standardised way to record them in RdSAP in the same automatic fashion as a certified rooftop array. In practice, this means:

- An 800W plug-in system generating roughly 550-700 kWh a year in most of the UK is a much smaller contribution than a typical 3-4 kW rooftop array, so even where it's recorded, the point uplift will be modest — realistically low single digits to perhaps 3-5 points, not the 18-point jump associated with full rooftop systems.
- Assessors may ask for photographic evidence, an EcoFlow STREAM app screenshot showing generation history, or a receipt confirming the kit meets the BSI product standard published in July 2026, since RdSAP conventions for plug-in systems are still being finalised by BRE (the body that maintains the methodology).
- A proposed "smart readiness" score, discussed in ongoing EPC reform consultations, would better capture small-scale and app-connected renewable technology like plug-in solar — worth watching if you're timing a purchase around a future EPC review.

The honest summary: plug-in solar is unlikely to single-handedly shift your EPC band, but it can nudge your points total upward, and it stacks with other measures — loft insulation, a smart thermostat, LED lighting — that are cheaper to combine with a sub-£800 kit than with full rooftop installation. ## EPC bands and what they mean for your finances

| EPC Band | Points | Typical implication |
|---|---|---|
| A | 92-100 | Rare for existing homes; best mortgage rates |
| B | 81-91 | Strong resale value, green mortgage eligible |
| C | 69-80 | Landlord compliance threshold from Oct 2030 |
| D | 55-68 | Most common band for UK homes |
| E | 39-54 | Legal minimum for most rentals today |
| F-G | Below 39 | Rental restrictions already apply |

If your home currently sits in band D, close to the C threshold, a plug-in solar system combined with one or two other low-cost measures could be enough to tip you over. If you're several bands away, treat solar as one part of a wider retrofit plan rather than a silver bullet.

## Landlords: why this deadline changes the calculation

For landlords, the EPC C requirement by October 2030 turns plug-in solar from a nice-to-have into part of a compliance strategy. An 800W kit costs a fraction of full rooftop solar, needs no structural work in most cases, and — where a landlord retains ownership of a balcony, garden or roof space and the tenancy agreement allows it — can be fitted between tenancies with minimal disruption. It won't get a property to C on its own, but as one line item within a £10,000 improvement budget, it's a comparatively cheap way to pick up a few extra points, in addition to the electricity cost savings tenants may benefit from directly. ## Selling or remortgaging: what to tell your assessor

If you've had a plug-in solar kit fitted and you're due an EPC assessment for a sale or remortgage, a little preparation helps:

1. Keep your purchase receipt and any BSI compliance documentation for the kit.
2. Export a generation history screenshot from the manufacturer's app covering at least a few months.
3. Photograph the installed panels and the DNO notification confirmation if one was required.
4. Mention the system explicitly when booking the assessment — don't assume the assessor will spot it from the street.

None of this guarantees a band change, but it maximises the chance the assessor records the generation correctly rather than missing it entirely. Because plug-in solar isn't a permanent fixed installation in the way rooftop PV is, there's a genuine question of whether it goes with you when you move, or stays and contributes to the new owner's EPC. ## FAQs

### Will an 800W plug-in solar kit move my home from D to C?

On its own, unlikely. An 800W kit is a small fraction of a typical rooftop array, so its RdSAP point contribution is modest — realistically a few points rather than the 18-point average uplift associated with full rooftop solar. It's best treated as one part of a wider set of efficiency improvements.

### Do EPC assessors currently know how to record plug-in solar?

RdSAP conventions for plug-in microgenerators are still catching up with the law, which only changed on 27 August 2026. Bring evidence — app generation logs, purchase receipts, BSI compliance documents — and mention the system explicitly when booking your assessment.

### Does plug-in solar count toward the landlord EPC C deadline?

It can contribute a small number of points as part of a wider improvement package within the proposed £10,000 investment cap ahead of the October 2030 deadline, though it won't achieve compliance on its own for most properties.
