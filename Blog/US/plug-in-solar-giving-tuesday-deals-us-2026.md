Black Friday and Cyber Monday get all the attention, but Giving Tuesday — December 1, 2026 this year — has quietly become one of the better windows to buy plug-in solar gear in the US. Retailers use it to clear remaining Black Friday stock at a second discount, and several solar and power-station brands run their own Giving Tuesday-specific promotions on top of whatever didn't sell out the weekend before. If you missed [Black Friday](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-black-friday-deals-2026-us) or [Cyber Monday](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-cyber-monday-deals-2026-us), this is your next real shot before prices normalize for the holidays.

## Why Giving Tuesday deals exist at all

Giving Tuesday started as a charitable-giving counterpoint to Black Friday consumerism, but retailers have folded it into the broader post-Thanksgiving sales calendar. For plug-in solar specifically, three things tend to happen:

1. **Inventory clearance** — brands that didn't sell through Black Friday stock discount further rather than carry it into Q1
2. **Bundle deals** — power station + solar panel bundles appear at a steeper combined discount than buying each separately
3. **Charity tie-ins** — some brands donate a percentage of Giving Tuesday sales, which doesn't change the price but is worth knowing if it factors into your decision

None of this means every listed "deal" is real. Compare the sale price against the 90-day price history on the retailer's own page where available, and against our [buying checklist](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-buying-checklist-us) before clicking buy.

## What's actually worth buying this week

**Portable power stations with solar input** see the deepest real discounts on Giving Tuesday, because brands are trying to move holiday-gift inventory. A 1–2kWh LiFePO4 unit that normally runs $900–$1,400 commonly drops 20-30% during this window.

**400–800W panel kits** discount less aggressively (10-15% is typical) since margins are thinner to begin with, but it's still worth checking if you've been holding off on a purchase.

**Accessories — MC4 cables, mounting brackets, extra charge controllers** — rarely go on meaningful sale and are a low priority to time around any specific shopping event.

## Don't let the sale override the fundamentals

A discounted kit is still a bad purchase if it doesn't fit your setup. Before buying anything this week:

- **Confirm your utility's interconnection stance.** Rules vary enormously by state and provider — see our [utility interconnection guide](/us/blog/utility-interconnection-plug-in-solar-us) if you haven't checked yours yet.
- **Check NEC Article 705 compliance** on the specific microinverter, not just the panel. Our [NEC 705 explainer](/us/blog/nec-article-705-plug-in-solar-us) covers what "compliant" actually means in practice.
- **Run the payback math** at your real electricity rate, not a national average. Rates range from roughly 14c/kWh to 39c/kWh depending on state, and that swing changes payback by years. Our [payback period guide](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-payback-period-us) walks through the calculation.
- **Factor in the federal tax credit** where applicable — see our [federal tax credit guide](/us/blog/federal-tax-credit-plug-in-solar) for current eligibility, since it can meaningfully change the real cost of a Giving Tuesday purchase.

## Timing the rest of the season

If Giving Tuesday prices don't move you, the next realistic discount window is typically early-to-mid January, when retailers clear remaining holiday stock ahead of spring inventory resets. Prices rarely drop meaningfully between early December and January, so there's little reason to wait specifically for Christmas week unless a particular retailer announces its own promotion.

### Are Giving Tuesday solar deals actually better than Black Friday?

Not universally — Black Friday tends to have the widest selection of discounted items, while Giving Tuesday often has deeper discounts on a narrower set of items (mostly power stations) that didn't sell through the prior weekend. Check both before buying.

### Does the federal tax credit apply to Giving Tuesday purchases?

Eligibility depends on the system type and installation method, not the purchase date or sale event. See our [federal tax credit guide](/us/blog/federal-tax-credit-plug-in-solar) for the current rules — a Giving Tuesday discount doesn't change tax eligibility one way or the other.

### What should I avoid buying during Giving Tuesday solar sales?

Be skeptical of panel-only "deals" that don't include a UL 1741-listed microinverter, and of bundles that pad the discount with accessories you don't need. The core components — panel wattage and inverter compliance — matter more than the headline discount percentage.
