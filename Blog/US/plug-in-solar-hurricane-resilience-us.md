Every June-through-November, Gulf and Atlantic residents watch NOAA forecasts and wonder how many days without power they can absorb. A plug-in solar + portable battery setup won't run your central AC through a Category 4 landfall — but it will keep your fridge, phones, medical devices and lighting alive for the 3–7 day recovery window that follows most storms. This piece covers what to buy, how to configure it, and what plug-in setups explicitly cannot do.

## What plug-in solar can and can't do during an outage

Understand this upfront: **a grid-tied plug-in solar kit cannot power your home during a grid outage.** UL 1741's anti-islanding requirement forces the microinverter to shut off within 200ms of grid loss. This is a safety feature — it prevents your panels from electrocuting the line crew fixing the outage — and it applies to every listed microinverter.

To keep loads running during an outage, you need one of:

1. **A portable power station with solar input** (the plug-and-play route)
2. **An off-grid inverter + battery + manual transfer switch** (more capable, more complex)
3. **A hybrid inverter with backup mode** (professional install territory)

For plug-in solar economics under NEC compliance, see the [NEC 705 explainer](/us/blog/nec-article-705-plug-in-solar-us).

## The plug-and-play resilience setup

The simplest configuration that survives hurricane outages:

- **400–800W foldable or rigid solar panels** with MC4 connectors
- **1–2kWh LiFePO4 portable power station** (EcoFlow DELTA 2, Anker SOLIX C1000, Jackery Explorer 1000 Plus)
- **Solar charge cable** (usually included with the power station)

Total: $700–$1,400. Charges from grid when available, from solar during the day when grid is down. Runs a fridge (150W), a few LED bulbs (30W), phone/laptop chargers, and a small fan for 8–12 hours on a full 1kWh battery.

## Sizing for hurricane essentials

Match battery capacity to your must-run loads. Typical hurricane essentials:

| Load | Watts | Hours/day | kWh/day |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fridge/freezer | 150 (cycling avg) | 24 | 1.5 |
| LED lights (4 bulbs) | 40 | 6 | 0.24 |
| Phone/laptop chargers | 30 | 6 | 0.18 |
| Fan (medium) | 60 | 8 | 0.48 |
| CPAP machine | 30 | 8 | 0.24 |
| **Total daily draw** | | | **~2.6 kWh** |

A 1kWh battery covers ~9 hours; 2kWh covers most of a day. Solar recharge adds 3–4 kWh/day for a 400W panel in Florida sun. That combination sustains essentials indefinitely as long as it's not overcast for 3+ days consecutive.

## What about a whole-home battery?

Whole-home battery backup (Tesla Powerwall, EcoFlow DELTA Pro Ultra, Enphase IQ Battery) can support a full house through a hurricane recovery — but requires a licensed electrician, a $10–20k budget, and permits. It's a different product category from plug-in solar. If you're serious about whole-home resilience, that's the path.

## Pre-hurricane checklist

- **Fully charge the power station** 48 hours out from expected landfall
- **Have panels ready to deploy** post-storm (don't leave rigid panels out during the storm — winds destroy them)
- **Test the CPAP / medical device on battery output** before you actually need it
- **Buy a second solar charge cable** — the primary one always develops issues at the worst time
- **Keep a car USB-C fast charger** as fallback for phones

## What plug-in solar owners in FL/GA/LA/TX should not do

- **Don't leave a rigid microinverter kit deployed during the storm** — winds pick them up like frisbees
- **Don't try to backfeed your home via generator inlet** without a proper transfer switch (illegal and dangerous)
- **Don't buy sub-$300 no-name battery stations** — cheap NMC chemistry has real fire risk during hurricane humidity/heat

### Can plug-in solar keep my house powered during a hurricane outage?

Not directly — UL 1741 anti-islanding forces grid-tied inverters to shut off during outages. But panels feeding a portable power station work independently of the grid and can sustain essentials (fridge, lights, phones, CPAP) for the 3–7 day post-storm recovery window.

### What size battery do I need for hurricane resilience?

For essentials only (fridge, lights, phones, medical), 1–2kWh covers most of a day. Combined with 400W of solar for daytime recharging, a 2kWh setup can sustain essentials indefinitely as long as sun is available. Whole-home coverage requires 10kWh+ and a hardwired install.

### Is LiFePO4 safer than NMC for hurricane use?

Yes, significantly. LiFePO4 has no thermal runaway risk, tolerates humidity better, and lasts 3x longer under daily cycling. Every quality portable power station released since 2023 uses LiFePO4 — avoid older NMC-chemistry Jackery models for hurricane duty.
