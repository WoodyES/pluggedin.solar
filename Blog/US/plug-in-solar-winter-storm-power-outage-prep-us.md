Winter storm season in the US starts picking up right about now, and it brings a predictable pattern: ice accumulation snaps power lines, utilities issue multi-day restoration estimates, and people who've never thought about backup power start searching for options at 11pm with no electricity. Plug-in solar kits get recommended a lot in these threads, usually alongside generators and power banks. They're a legitimate piece of a winter outage plan — but the marketing around "solar backup power" oversells what an 800W panel and a small battery can actually do in December, when sun is scarce and most outages happen because of weather that also blocks what little sun there is.

## Why winter outages are a worse case for solar than summer ones

Hurricane-season outages at least come with strong late-summer sun before and after the storm to recharge a battery. Winter ice storms are the opposite: the weather event itself is usually several days of overcast skies, and short winter daylight hours compound the problem — you might get 4-5 usable sunlight hours in December versus 8-10 in July. Our [winter performance guide](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-winter-performance-us) covers the seasonal generation drop in detail, but the short version for storm planning is: don't assume your panel will meaningfully recharge a battery during the outage itself. Plan around the charge you have stored before the storm hits, not the charge you hope to generate during it.

## What a plug-in setup is actually good for in an outage

Where plug-in solar earns its place in a winter storm kit is running small continuous loads for days at a time without needing fuel, which matters when roads are too icy to get to a gas station anyway:

- **Phone and device charging** — trivial load, runs indefinitely off a small battery even with minimal solar input.
- **CPAP machines and medical devices** — low wattage, high importance, and the quiet, fumeless operation matters indoors.
- **Router and modem** — keeping internet/cell signal boosters running so you can actually get storm updates and check on utility restoration timelines.
- **Refrigerator cycling** — a stretch for a small kit, but a 1-2kWh battery paired with efficient panel input can keep a fridge cold enough over a multi-day outage if you're disciplined about not running much else.

What it's not good for: space heaters, well pumps, sump pumps, or anything pulling more than a few hundred watts continuously. If your winter storm risk includes losing heat in sub-freezing temperatures, a plug-in kit is a supplement to a real backup heating plan (wood stove, generator-fed furnace, or evacuation plan), not a replacement for one.

## The compliance and safety side

Most plug-in solar kits sold in the US sit in the grey area under [NEC Article 705](/us/blog/nec-article-705-plug-in-solar-us) — small enough (typically 200-800W) that enforcement is inconsistent, but using a [UL 1741-listed inverter](/us/blog/ul-1741-vs-ieee-1547-plug-in-solar-us) is the baseline safety requirement regardless of how your utility treats interconnection. During a grid outage specifically, a grid-tied plug-in inverter with anti-islanding protection will actually shut off rather than back-feed a dead circuit — which is correct and safe behavior, but means a straight grid-tied kit gives you zero power during the exact event you bought it for. If outage backup is your actual goal, you want a kit built around a battery with its own inverter (island mode), not a grid-tied microinverter panel alone. Check our [safety guide](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-safety-guide-us) before wiring anything into household circuits during an active outage.

## Pre-storm checklist

The single biggest mistake people make is waiting until the storm warning to think about any of this. By then, panels may already be iced over or the sky may already be solid grey for 48 hours straight. A few things to do before a storm is forecast:

1. **Fully charge every battery from the grid**, not from solar, the day before. Don't rely on panel input to top you off once the forecast turns.
2. **Bring portable panels indoors or secure them** — ice and high wind during the storm itself can damage an exposed panel, and a cracked panel does nothing for you afterward. Our [storm-proofing guide](/us/blog/storm-proofing-plug-in-solar-us) and [hurricane resilience guide](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-hurricane-resilience-us) both cover securing hardware, and most of the same principles (secure mounting, disconnect before extreme wind, protect cabling from ice) carry over from hurricane prep to ice storm prep.
3. **Know your utility's interconnection rules** before you need to improvise — check our [utility interconnection guide](/us/blog/utility-interconnection-plug-in-solar-us) so you're not trying to figure out notification requirements mid-outage.
4. **Test everything before the storm, not during it.** Verify your inverter, battery, and cabling actually work together under load. If something's wrong, our [troubleshooting guide](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-not-working-troubleshooting-us) is far more useful read in good weather with internet access than during the outage itself.
5. **Check federal incentive eligibility** if you're buying new gear specifically for storm resilience — some setups still qualify for the [federal tax credit](/us/blog/federal-tax-credit-plug-in-solar), which softens the cost of building out a proper battery-backed system rather than a bare grid-tied panel.

If you're shopping for your first kit with outage resilience specifically in mind, start with our general [buying checklist](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-buying-checklist-us) and filter hard for battery-inclusive, island-mode-capable systems — a grid-tied-only panel is the wrong purchase if backup power during an outage is the actual goal.

### Will my plug-in solar panel keep working during a grid outage?

A grid-tied-only inverter will shut off during an outage due to anti-islanding safety protection — that's required, correct behavior, not a malfunction. For actual backup power, you need a kit built around a battery with its own independent inverter.

### How long can a small plug-in solar + battery setup power a fridge during a winter outage?

With a 1-2kWh battery and disciplined use (nothing else running), often 1-3 days depending on the fridge's efficiency and ambient temperature, but don't expect meaningful solar recharge during an overcast winter storm itself — plan around stored charge, not generation.

### Should I buy a grid-tied kit or a battery-based kit for storm backup?

Battery-based, island-mode kits for backup purposes. Grid-tied-only panels are designed to shut off during outages for safety and provide no power when the grid is down, which defeats the purpose of buying for storm resilience.
