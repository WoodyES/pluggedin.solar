Every RV forum has the same recurring thread: "how do I add solar without permanent modification?" The honest answer is a portable foldable panel plus a LiFePO4 battery. This guide covers what actually works on a Class C, a fifth wheel, or an Airstream Bambi.

## The three configurations

### 1. Suitcase panel + power station (simplest)
100–400W foldable panel with kickstand, MC4 to a portable power station (EcoFlow DELTA 2, Anker SOLIX C1000). Deploy at the campsite, charge the station, run 12V+120V loads from the station. $800–$1,400 total.

### 2. Roof panel + charge controller into RV house battery (permanent-ish)
200–400W rigid panel on the roof (or ladder-mounted flexible), MC4 to an MPPT charge controller, to the existing RV lead-acid or LiFePO4 house battery. Requires drilling one entry gland or using the fridge vent. Not "plug-in" in the strictest sense but common. $400–$800.

### 3. Fold-out awning panel (compromise)
Panel clips to the awning frame with adjustable straps. Removed when driving. Feeds either a power station or (via optional charge controller) the house battery. Best for people who camp weekly without moving between spots. $300–$600.

For US regulatory background see [NEC Article 705](/us/blog/nec-article-705-plug-in-solar-us).

## Sizing for RV loads

Typical RV daily loads at boondocking:
- Fridge (2-way, running on 12V): 500Wh
- Lighting (LED, evening): 100Wh
- Water pump (occasional): 50Wh
- Fan (roof vent): 200Wh
- Phone/laptop charging: 200Wh
- **Total: ~1,050Wh/day**

A 400W panel in Utah sun generates 1,800–2,200 Wh/day — comfortable margin for daily use with a 1kWh battery.

## What NOT to do

- **Don't wire panels to the roof and grid-tie via generator inlet** (illegal and dangerous)
- **Don't use car-style flexible panels** for permanent roof mounting — they delaminate in 2 years
- **Don't skip the MPPT charge controller** if going to house battery — PWM cuts output 20–30%
