## What actually matters in a 400W panel

**Efficiency rating.** Top-tier residential panels reach 21–23%. Budget panels sit at 18–19%. A more efficient panel produces the same wattage in a smaller area — critical for RV roof mounting or urban balconies.

**Temperature coefficient.** Every panel loses output when hot. A -0.30 to -0.35%/°C coefficient is typical for modern monocrystalline. On a 100°F Arizona summer day cell temperatures can reach 150°F — reducing output by 12–16% from rated.

**Physical dimensions.** A standard 400W panel is roughly 68" × 45" (about 21 ft²). Check dimensions against your RV roof, balcony, or ground-mount space *before* ordering.

**Warranty.** Look for **25-year product warranty** and **25–30-year performance warranty** (guaranteeing at least 80% of rated output at year 25). All tier-1 brands offer both. Unbranded panels typically offer 2–5 years and no meaningful recourse.

**Cell technology.** Monocrystalline PERC is the current baseline. TOPCon and heterojunction (HJT) cells give marginally better low-light performance and lower degradation. Worth $20–30 more for TOPCon over PERC on the same brand.

## Budget panels — a warning

Panels from unknown brands on Amazon and eBay sell for $70–120. These are typically **rebranded factory-second panels** without tier-1 quality control. They may work fine. They may also have higher degradation, inconsistent output between panels in the same batch, or inadequate junction-box sealing that fails in year three.

A $70 panel that generates 15% less than rated from year one is not saving you money. Stick with tier-1 brands ($189+).

## Building a complete 800W US setup

For a US 200–800W plug-in setup you need:

1. **1–2× 400W panels** ($199–$500 for tier-1)
2. **UL 1741-listed microinverter or MPPT charge controller** ($180–$400)
3. **Mounting hardware** — tilt frame or wall bracket ($40–$150)
4. **MC4 extension cables** if needed ($20–$40)
5. **For grid-tie**: utility notification per your provider's simplified interconnection process — see [NEC Article 705 explained](/us/blog/nec-article-705-plug-in-solar-us).

For off-grid battery-based setups (no grid interconnection needed), pair with a portable power station — see [best portable power stations US 2026](/us/blog/best-portable-power-stations-us-2026).

### Can I install a 400W panel without an electrician in the US?

For off-grid setups (panel feeding a portable power station or battery), yes — no electrician needed, no utility notification, no permits. For grid-tied setups, most utilities require notification (a simplified interconnection form) and some jurisdictions require a licensed electrician for the final connection. Check your local utility and AHJ.
