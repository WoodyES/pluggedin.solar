About 66 million Americans live in an HOA-governed community. If you're one of them, your first question about plug-in solar is probably: **can my HOA stop me?** The answer depends on your state, your specific covenants, and whether the kit qualifies as "solar" under your state's Solar Rights Act.

## State solar rights laws

**23 states plus DC have Solar Access or Solar Rights laws** that limit an HOA's ability to prohibit rooftop solar. The list: AZ, CA, CO, DE, FL, HI, IL, IA, LA, MD, MA, ME, NJ, NM, NC, NV, OR, RI, TX, UT, VT, VA, WA, plus DC.

Where these laws exist, HOAs typically **cannot outright ban** solar but **can regulate reasonable aesthetics** — panel placement, colour, screening. Plug-in kits often benefit from these laws even more than rooftop systems because they're smaller, removable, and don't require structural modification.

## What HOAs typically allow (and don't)

- **Ground-mounted panels behind the house or fence line** — almost always allowed
- **Balcony rail-mounted panels** — allowed in most states, sometimes with aesthetic conditions
- **Portable panels used seasonally** — no HOA has grounds to restrict these
- **Front-yard panels visible from the street** — commonly restricted or prohibited
- **Roof-mounted rigid panels** — subject to same rules as full rooftop solar

## The practical approach

1. Read your CC&Rs (Covenants, Conditions & Restrictions) — search for "solar", "energy", "outdoor equipment"
2. If ambiguous, submit an ARC (Architectural Review Committee) application with a photo of the intended install
3. If denied, cite your state's Solar Rights law if applicable — most HOAs back down when this is flagged
