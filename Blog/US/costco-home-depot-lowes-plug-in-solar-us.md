Unlike the UK, where plug-in solar has its own legal category and big-box retailers like B&Q and Currys now stock dedicated plug-in kits on the shelf, the US market is messier. There's no equivalent "plug-in microgenerator" product line at American big-box stores — instead you're piecing together a portable panel, a UL 1741-listed microinverter or a power station, and sometimes mounting hardware, from whatever a given retailer happens to carry. Here's what that actually looks like at the three chains people ask about most.

## Costco: strong on power stations, weak on grid-tied gear

Costco's warehouse model means its solar-adjacent inventory rotates seasonally and skews heavily toward portable power stations — Jackery, EcoFlow and Anker SOLIX units show up regularly, often bundled with a folding solar panel for off-grid or backup use. What you generally won't find at Costco is a genuine [UL 1741-listed grid-tie microinverter kit](/us/blog/ul-1741-vs-ieee-1547-plug-in-solar-us) sold as a complete product — Costco's solar business is almost entirely rooftop installer referrals (through its member services program) rather than DIY plug-in gear. If your interest is a battery-backed portable setup rather than anything that connects to your home's wiring, Costco is worth checking seasonally, but don't expect consistent year-round stock of any specific model.

## Home Depot: the broadest DIY solar aisle

Home Depot carries the widest range of components relevant to a DIY plug-in-style setup: portable panels, charge controllers, MC4 connectors, and increasingly a few 800W-class microinverter and panel bundles from brands expanding into the US retail channel. It's also the most likely of the three to have in-store staff who can talk through basic wiring, though — as with any big-box retail staff — depth varies a lot by store. Home Depot's online marketplace carries more SKUs than what's on the physical shelf, including some of the same UL 1741-listed micro-inverter kits reviewed in our [EcoFlow PowerStream review](/us/blog/ecoflow-powerstream-review-us), so it's worth checking both in-store and online before assuming a product isn't carried.

## Lowe's: similar to Home Depot, slightly thinner selection

Lowe's product range overlaps heavily with Home Depot — portable panels, basic solar accessories, some power stations — but tends to carry fewer of the newer micro-inverter kit brands that have started appearing in US retail over the past year. It's a reasonable second stop if Home Depot is out of stock locally, but rarely the first place worth checking for anything beyond mainstream portable panels and well-known power station brands.

## Why online retailers often beat all three

Because US big-box stores haven't built out a dedicated plug-in solar category the way UK retailers have, online marketplaces currently carry a far deeper and more consistently stocked range of UL 1741-listed microinverter kits and portable panels than any single physical chain. That matters most if you've already read our [NEC Article 705 compliance guide](/us/blog/nec-article-705-plug-in-solar-us) and know exactly what inverter certification and sizing you need — you're more likely to find the specific model in stock online than by visiting three stores in person.

## Check your state and utility rules first

Wherever you buy, the equipment is only half the picture. Whether you can legally interconnect anything to your home's wiring depends heavily on your state and utility — see our [state-by-state legality guide](/us/blog/is-plug-in-solar-legal-in-my-state) before buying anything you intend to grid-tie rather than run as a standalone off-grid setup. If your home has an HOA, check our [HOA rules guide](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-and-your-hoa) too, since covenants can restrict visible equipment regardless of what your utility allows.

## Budget and buying-checklist basics

If you're comparing specific models and prices across these retailers and others, our [buying checklist](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-buying-checklist-us) and [cheapest plug-in solar kits](/us/blog/cheapest-plug-in-solar-kits-us) roundup cover what to look for beyond just sticker price — warranty terms, inverter certification, and panel wattage tolerances all vary more between budget and premium kits than most buyers expect.

### Does Costco sell grid-tied plug-in solar kits?

Not really — Costco's in-store and warehouse inventory is almost entirely portable power stations and folding panels for off-grid or backup use, plus rooftop installer referrals through its member services program, rather than DIY grid-tie microinverter kits.

### Which retailer has the best selection for a DIY plug-in solar setup?

Home Depot generally carries the broadest range of DIY-relevant components in-store, but online marketplaces currently stock a wider and more consistent range of UL 1741-listed microinverter kits than any single big-box chain.

### Do I need to check anything before buying, regardless of retailer?

Yes — confirm your state and utility's interconnection rules first. Equipment availability doesn't tell you whether grid-tying it is actually permitted where you live.
