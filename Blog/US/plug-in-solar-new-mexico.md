# Plug-In Solar in New Mexico: Sun, Savings and What's Legal in 2026

New Mexico rarely comes up first when people talk about solar states — Arizona, California and Texas get most of the attention — but it quietly has some of the best solar resource in the entire country. Albuquerque and Santa Fe both average well over 6.5 peak sun hours a day, on par with or better than Phoenix, and the state's clear, dry high-desert climate means fewer cloudy-day losses than almost anywhere else in the Lower 48. For anyone considering a small plug-in solar kit, that combination of strong sun and a low-density, low-bureaucracy state makes New Mexico one of the more favorable places to run the numbers.

## New Mexico's Solar Resource Is Exceptional

Albuquerque averages around 6.5 to 7 peak sun hours per day, and most of the state outside the far northern mountains sits close behind. That's a meaningfully better resource than Texas, Florida or most of the Southeast, and roughly on par with Arizona — without Arizona's extreme summer heat penalty on panel efficiency.

A 600W plug-in kit facing south in Albuquerque can realistically generate 950 to 1,100 kWh a year, more than the same kit would produce in most of the country. For a small apartment balcony or patio setup, that's a meaningful dent in a typical household's annual usage.

## Electricity Rates and What That Sun Is Worth

New Mexico's average residential electricity rate sits around $0.14 to $0.15 per kWh, close to the national average and well below California or Hawaii. That means the dollar value of each kWh generated is lower than in high-rate states — but the higher generation volume largely offsets that. A 600W kit producing 1,000 kWh a year at $0.145/kWh is worth roughly $145 annually, with payback on a sub-$1,000 kit landing in the 5 to 7 year range before incentives, faster with the federal tax credit applied.

PNM (Public Service Company of New Mexico) and El Paso Electric are the state's two largest investor-owned utilities, both operating under net metering rules set by the New Mexico Public Regulation Commission. Rural electric cooperatives cover much of the state outside Albuquerque and Las Cruces, and co-op interconnection rules can vary — check with your specific provider before assuming utility-scale net metering rules apply to a small plug-in setup.

## Where Plug-In Solar Sits Legally

As with most states, New Mexico has no specific statute addressing small plug-in solar kits the way the UK's SI 2026/848 does for plug-in microgenerators. Small systems under roughly 200-800W, paired with a UL 1741-listed inverter, generally fall under the same practical grey area covered in our guide to [plug-in solar legality by state](/us/blog/is-plug-in-solar-legal-in-my-state) — tolerated in practice because formal utility interconnection review is typically triggered by system size and application, not by the existence of a small panel on a patio.

New Mexico does have a solar rights law (NMSA 47-3-1 et seq.) that limits how restrictive an HOA or deed covenant can be toward solar access, though it's written primarily with rooftop systems in mind. If you're in an HOA community, that law is worth citing if a board pushes back on a balcony or patio kit, alongside the interconnection basics covered in our [NEC Article 705 guide](/us/blog/nec-article-705-plug-in-solar-us) and [UL 1741 vs IEEE 1547 explainer](/us/blog/ul-1741-vs-ieee-1547-plug-in-solar-us).

## Dry Climate, Dust and Panel Care

New Mexico's dry climate means far less mold, moss or algae buildup than humid Southeastern states, but wind-blown dust from the high desert is a real factor — especially during spring windstorm season (March through May) when dust events can cut output noticeably within days of a clean. A quick rinse every few weeks during dust season keeps output close to rated capacity; outside of that period, monthly cleaning is usually enough.

## Albuquerque, Santa Fe and Las Cruces: Where Density Meets Sun

Albuquerque's population (around 560,000) includes a significant renter and apartment-dwelling share, concentrated in areas like Nob Hill, Downtown and the University area — exactly the kind of housing stock where a balcony or patio plug-in kit makes more sense than a full rooftop install. Santa Fe's historic district has stricter aesthetic rules that can complicate even small visible installations, so a low-profile, ground or railing-mounted kit tends to draw less attention than panels mounted on a visible wall. Las Cruces, in the south of the state, gets slightly more sun than Albuquerque on average and has a lower cost of living, making the payback math even more favorable.

## Winter Performance

Unlike much of the northern US, New Mexico's winter sun remains strong — Albuquerque still averages around 5.5 peak sun hours in December and January, well above what most of the country sees in midwinter. Combined with cooler temperatures actually improving panel efficiency slightly (the same temperature-coefficient effect covered in our [Arizona guide](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-arizona), but working in your favor rather than against you), New Mexico sees far less of a winter generation drop-off than most states.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is plug-in solar legal in New Mexico?

There's no state law explicitly legalizing or banning small plug-in solar kits. Like most of the US, New Mexico's regulatory framework wasn't written with 200-800W plug-in kits in mind, so they occupy a practical grey area — widely sold and used, provided the inverter is UL 1741-listed and the system doesn't trigger formal utility interconnection review. See our [state-by-state legality guide](/us/blog/is-plug-in-solar-legal-in-my-state) for more detail.

### How much can a plug-in solar kit save in New Mexico?

A 600W kit generating around 1,000 kWh a year at New Mexico's average residential rate (roughly $0.145/kWh) is worth about $145 annually. Albuquerque, Las Cruces and most of the southern and central state see strong enough sun that generation estimates run higher than the national average for an equivalent kit.

### Does PNM or El Paso Electric allow plug-in solar?

Neither utility has a published policy specifically addressing small plug-in kits, which generally fall below the thresholds that trigger formal net metering applications. Larger grid-tied systems go through standard interconnection review under each utility's tariff; a small plug-in kit typically doesn't reach that threshold, but check with your provider directly if you're unsure.
