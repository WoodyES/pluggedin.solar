The 30% federal Residential Clean Energy Credit (ITC) makes a $30,000 rooftop system effectively cost $21,000. That's a meaningful subsidy — but it doesn't automatically make rooftop the right choice for every household. Plug-in kits sit in a different economic zone: no ITC access, but 30–50× lower upfront cost.

## The comparison

**Rooftop 10kW system with ITC**
- Gross cost: ~$30,000
- After 30% ITC: $21,000
- Annual generation: 14,000 kWh
- Annual savings (at 16.5¢): $2,300
- Payback: ~9 years
- Warranty: 25 years

**Plug-in 800W kit**
- Gross cost: ~$800
- ITC eligible? No (system must be permanently installed and permitted)
- Annual generation: 1,000 kWh
- Annual savings (at 16.5¢): $110–$165
- Payback: ~5–7 years
- Warranty: 5–10 years

Rooftop wins on absolute savings ($/year). Plug-in wins on:
- **Capital risk** — $800 vs $30,000 is a much smaller commitment
- **Renter friendly** — rooftop needs owned property; plug-in doesn't
- **Move-friendly** — take plug-in with you when you sell
- **No roof modifications** — critical for HOAs, historic districts, tile roofs

## When plug-in is the right call

- You rent (no rooftop option period)
- You plan to move within 5 years
- Your roof is unsuitable (heavy shading, wrong orientation, tile that installers avoid)
- You want to dip a toe in before committing $30k
- You can't access the ITC (low tax liability)
