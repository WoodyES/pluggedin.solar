Net metering (NEM) and net billing (NBT) sound similar. Economically, they're wildly different. This piece explains both, which US states use which, and why most plug-in solar owners fall outside both frameworks entirely.

## Net metering (NEM)

Under classic net metering, every kWh you export to the grid earns a credit at your **retail rate** — the same 16–35¢/kWh you pay for import. Your utility bills you for the *net* consumption over the billing period. If you generate 500 kWh and consume 400, you owe nothing for energy and might carry forward a credit.

States that still use classic NEM (or close approximations): most of the East Coast and Midwest.

## Net billing (NBT)

Under net billing, exports earn a **wholesale rate** — typically 3–8¢/kWh, vs 25–35¢/kWh retail for import. Suddenly, every kWh you export bleeds ~25¢ of value vs consuming it directly. California's NEM 3.0 (April 2023 onwards) is the highest-profile net billing environment.

States moving to NBT: California (implemented), Hawaii (implemented), Arizona (partial), Nevada (partial). More expected 2026–2028.

## Why plug-in solar owners often fall outside both

To qualify for net metering or net billing, your utility typically requires:

- A formal **interconnection agreement**
- A **bi-directional revenue-grade meter** (some utilities install for free, others charge)
- Proof of **UL 1741-listed inverter**
- Sometimes: proof of **licensed electrician** install

Most small (200–800W) plug-in kits don't clear all four hurdles. The practical answer: focus on **self-consumption** (using solar as it generates) which captures the full retail-rate offset with no interconnection paperwork.
