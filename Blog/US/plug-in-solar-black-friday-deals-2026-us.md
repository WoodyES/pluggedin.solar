Black Friday has become one of the two or three biggest buying windows of the year for portable solar kits and power stations in the US, and 2026 should be no exception. If you've been putting off a purchase while researching [whether plug-in solar is worth it](/us/blog/is-plug-in-solar-worth-it-us) or [how the economics actually work](/us/blog/how-much-can-plug-in-solar-save-us), the next few weeks are typically when the best pricing of the year shows up. Here's what to expect, which retailers tend to move first, and how to avoid the common mistakes that turn a good deal into a wasted purchase.

## Why Black Friday pricing on solar kits is genuinely different

Unlike a lot of Black Friday "deals" that are really just regular promotional pricing with a bigger sticker, portable power and small solar kits see real, meaningful discounts this time of year — often 20-35% off list price on power stations and bundled solar panel kits. Brands like EcoFlow, Anker, Jackery and Bluetti use the holiday shopping season to clear current-generation inventory ahead of new model releases in the new year, which is good news for buyers who don't need the absolute newest hardware. If you're new to how these kits work in the first place, our [US complete guide](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-us-complete-guide) and [how plug-in solar works at 120V](/us/blog/how-plug-in-solar-works-us-120v) are worth reading before you buy anything, deal or not.

## What tends to go on sale first

Entry-level and mid-tier bundled kits — a portable power station paired with one or two folding solar panels in the 100-400W range — are usually the first and deepest discounts, since they're the highest-volume sellers. Larger systems and the newest flagship power stations tend to hold their price longer into the shopping window, sometimes not dropping meaningfully until Cyber Monday or later. ## Don't let a discount override the compliance basics

A deep discount on a kit that isn't properly set up is no bargain at all. Whatever you buy on sale still needs to interconnect correctly under [NEC Article 705](/us/blog/nec-article-705-plug-in-solar-us), and if it's feeding power back into household circuits rather than charging a standalone battery, the inverter needs genuine [UL 1741 or IEEE 1547 compliance](/us/blog/ul-1741-vs-ieee-1547-plug-in-solar-us) with real anti-islanding protection — not just a sticker claiming it. Our [electrical safety guide](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-electrical-safety-120v) covers the basics worth checking on any listing before you add it to cart, sale price or not.

## State rules don't pause for Black Friday

If you're buying specifically to use what you get, remember that interconnection and net metering rules vary significantly by state and even by utility. California's [NEM 3.0 framework](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-california) treats exported solar very differently from net metering states, and if you're in an [HOA](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-and-your-hoa) or rental, check the rules before buying a system sized for a grid-tied setup you may not actually be able to use that way. A deal is only a deal if you can legally use what you bought — our [state-by-state legality guide](/us/blog/is-plug-in-solar-legal-in-my-state) is the fastest way to check before you commit to a cart.

## Timing your purchase within the sale window

Prices typically dip in stages: a first wave of discounts in the week before Thanksgiving, deeper cuts on Black Friday and Cyber Monday themselves, and occasionally a final clearance push in mid-December as retailers try to move remaining holiday inventory. If you're not in a rush, waiting until closer to Cyber Monday often gets a better price than jumping on the very first Black Friday listing you see, though popular bundles can also sell out before the window closes. Our [payback period guide](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-payback-period-us) is useful for working out roughly how long any given deal takes to pay for itself at your local electricity rate before you decide how much to spend.

## What to check before you click buy

- Confirm the inverter is genuinely UL 1741-listed, not just "compliant" in marketing copy
- Compare the sale price against several weeks of pre-sale pricing history, not just the advertised "was" price
- Check your state and utility's interconnection rules if you plan to grid-tie rather than run a standalone battery
- Factor in shipping times — popular bundles can take longer to arrive during the holiday rush than usual

### Is Black Friday really the cheapest time of year to buy plug-in solar kits in the US?

For most bundled power station and panel kits, yes — Black Friday through Cyber Monday is typically the single best pricing window of the year, though occasional mid-summer clearance sales on outgoing models can sometimes match it.

### Should I wait for Cyber Monday instead of buying on Black Friday itself?

It depends on the specific item. Popular bundles sometimes sell out on Black Friday before Cyber Monday pricing appears, while other items see their deepest discount later in the window. If you've found a genuinely good price on Black Friday for something you know you want, it's rarely worth the risk of waiting.

### Do Black Friday solar deals affect whether a kit is UL 1741 compliant?

No — sale pricing has no bearing on compliance. A discounted kit still needs a genuinely UL 1741-listed inverter if you plan to interconnect it with household wiring, so check the specification sheet rather than assuming a popular retailer's listing guarantees it.
