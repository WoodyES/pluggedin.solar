Hawaii residential electricity averages **39¢/kWh** — literally 2.4× the US average. That makes plug-in solar payback the fastest of any US state at ~1.5–3 years. But HECO's (Hawaiian Electric) grid rules are the strictest in the country, and Kauai's KIUC operates differently again. This guide covers what works island by island.

## Why Hawaii economics are extreme

- Retail rate: 34–45¢/kWh depending on island and tier
- Solar irradiance: 5.5+ kWh/m²/day statewide (top-tier US)
- Grid: island-scale, no interstate transmission, expensive fuel oil generation
- Net billing (not net metering): exports pay wholesale ~10¢, not retail

Any solar you self-consume displaces 34–45¢ import. That's the killer economic driver.

## Oahu (HECO)

Most restrictive residential interconnection rules in the US. Feed-in Tariff (FIT) programs are effectively closed. **CSS (Customer Self-Supply)** and **CGS+ (Customer Grid Supply Plus)** are the primary options — both require full interconnection paperwork.

Plug-in kits under 800W with UL 1741 microinverters are widely used in a grey zone — notify HECO or don't, enforcement is inconsistent.

## Maui, Big Island (also HECO)

Same rules as Oahu but often more relaxed enforcement given rural grid density.

## Kauai (KIUC)

KIUC is a member cooperative, not an investor-owned utility. Rules are simpler and more welcoming. Their **KIBS (Kauai Island Battery Storage)** program actually incentivises battery-paired solar.

## What to buy for Hawaii

- **UL 1741 SB (Rule 21 updated) inverters** — required by all HECO territories
- **LiFePO4 battery pairing** — critical given net billing economics
- **Flexible or lightweight rigid panels** — Hawaii wind and salt corrosion punish cheap frames

For US-wide context see the [NEC 705 explainer](/us/blog/nec-article-705-plug-in-solar-us) and [California guide](/us/blog/best-plug-in-solar-california-2026).
