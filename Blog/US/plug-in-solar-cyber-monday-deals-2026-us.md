Cyber Monday has quietly become the better day to buy portable solar and power station hardware in the US — not Black Friday. Retailers increasingly treat Black Friday as the loss-leader event for doorbuster electronics and treat Cyber Monday as the day they discount higher-margin categories, which is exactly where small solar kits and power stations tend to sit. If you've already looked at [Black Friday pricing](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-black-friday-deals-2026-us) and aren't sure whether to pull the trigger yet, here's what's worth knowing before Monday.

## Why Cyber Monday pricing can beat Friday's

Amazon, in particular, has a pattern of launching its deepest solar and power station discounts on Cyber Monday rather than Black Friday itself, since that's the day it competes most directly with other online-only retailers for the same shopper attention. Brands like EcoFlow, Anker, Jackery and Bluetti often save their steepest percentage-off deals — sometimes 30-40% rather than the 20-25% typical of Friday — for this one day, treating it as the actual peak of the holiday sales calendar rather than a footnote to Black Friday.

That's good news if you haven't bought yet, but it also means buying on Friday "just to be safe" can occasionally leave money on the table. If the kit you want isn't about to sell out, it's usually worth waiting the extra three days.

## What actually matters when comparing deals

A lower price only matters if the hardware is actually suitable for your setup. Before comparing dollar amounts across retailers, check:

- Whether the inverter is UL 1741-listed — see our guide to [UL 1741 vs IEEE 1547](/us/blog/ul-1741-vs-ieee-1547-plug-in-solar-us) if you're unsure what that means for your interconnection
- Total wattage against your actual usage — our [sizing calculator guide](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-sizing-calculator-us) walks through how to size a kit properly rather than just buying the biggest discount
- Whether your state's net metering or net billing rules change the payback math — this varies a lot; see [net metering vs net billing](/us/blog/net-metering-vs-net-billing-plug-in-solar-us) and the [federal tax credit guide](/us/blog/federal-tax-credit-plug-in-solar) for how the incentive side works
- Whether your HOA or condo board has existing restrictions — a Cyber Monday impulse buy is a bad time to discover [your HOA doesn't allow it](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-and-your-hoa)

## State-specific timing considerations

If you're in a state with a looming interconnection or rate change — California's NEM 3.0 transition being the most-discussed example — buying earlier rather than later in the sales window can matter more than squeezing out the last few dollars of discount. Getting a kit installed and generating before a rate structure shifts is usually worth more than an extra 5% off. Check our [California guide](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-california) if that applies to you. For storm-exposed states, Cyber Monday also lands well ahead of hurricane season planning for next year — see our [hurricane resilience guide](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-hurricane-resilience-us) if backup power capability is part of why you're buying.

## Retailers worth checking before Monday

Amazon tends to be the single biggest venue for Cyber Monday solar and power station deals simply because of the sheer number of brands competing for placement, but it's not the only one worth watching. Costco often runs its own bundled power-station-and-panel deals around the same week, Home Depot and Lowe's sometimes discount mounting hardware and extension cables that complement a kit you already own, and direct brand sites — EcoFlow, Anker, Jackery and Bluetti all sell direct — occasionally beat the marketplace price by throwing in a free accessory instead of a straight discount. It's worth having two or three tabs open rather than buying from the first listing you see, since the same model frequently shows up at different effective prices once bundled accessories are accounted for.

If you're buying for a specific use case rather than general backup power, match the kit to that use rather than the discount. Someone outfitting a [cabin or off-grid property](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-off-grid-cabin-us) has different priorities — total runtime, battery capacity — than someone buying for [RV or van life](/us/blog/plug-in-solar-rv-airstream-us), where weight and portability matter more than raw wattage. A deeply discounted kit that's the wrong shape for your actual use isn't a good deal no matter the percentage off.

### Does Cyber Monday really beat Black Friday for solar kits?

Often, yes — particularly on Amazon and direct brand sites, where percentage discounts on power stations and solar kits are frequently deeper on Cyber Monday than on Black Friday itself.

### Should I buy the biggest kit I can afford during the sale?

Not necessarily. Buy based on your actual load and your state's interconnection rules, not the discount size — an oversized kit that your utility won't let you interconnect, or that you can't use fully, isn't a bargain.

### What if I miss Cyber Monday deals entirely?

You haven't missed your only chance. Retailers typically run a second, smaller wave of discounts around New Year — and the underlying federal tax credit and state incentives don't disappear just because the sale ends.
