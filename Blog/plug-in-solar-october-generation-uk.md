If you installed a kit during the launch rush after [plug-in solar became legal on 27 August 2026](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk), October is the first full month where the seasonal drop becomes obvious on the app. September held up reasonably well — as we covered in our [September generation guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-september-generation-uk) — but October is where the sun angle, cloud cover, and shorter days all move against you at once. Here's what to actually expect, region by region, and how to keep self-consumption up as generation falls.

## October output by region

For a standard 800W plug-in kit (two 400W panels, no battery, or an [EcoFlow STREAM](/blog/ecoflow-stream-uk-launch-september-2026) with storage), realistic October generation looks like this:

| Region | October daily average | October total |
|--------|------------------------|----------------|
| South East & South West | 1.6-2.2 kWh/day | 50-68 kWh |
| Midlands & East Anglia | 1.4-1.9 kWh/day | 43-59 kWh |
| North England & Wales | 1.2-1.7 kWh/day | 37-53 kWh |
| Scotland & Northern Ireland | 1.0-1.5 kWh/day | 31-46 kWh |

That's roughly a **30-40% drop** from September output, and around half of what the same system produced in high summer. It's not a fault with your panels — it's the calendar. Days shorten by nearly 90 minutes over the course of October, and the sun sits noticeably lower in the sky even at midday, which matters more than most people expect for a fixed-angle panel. If your numbers look worse than the table above, our [low-output troubleshooting guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-generating-less-than-expected-uk) and [shading guide](/blog/shading-plug-in-solar-output-uk) are worth checking before assuming anything's wrong — newly bare trees and lower sun angles create shadows that weren't there in July.

## The clocks change complicates the picture

The clocks go back on the last Sunday of October, and that catches a lot of first-year owners off guard when they check their app and see generation apparently "start" an hour later overnight. Nothing about your panels changes — only the clock label on when sunrise happens. We've covered the mechanics in detail in our [October clocks change guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-october-clocks-change-uk), but the practical takeaway is this: your generation window shifts earlier relative to the clock, which means any appliances you've been running mid-afternoon to catch peak output may need nudging earlier once British Summer Time ends.

## Why panel angle matters more this month

October is the point in the year where a fixed panel angle set up for summer starts costing you real output. The sun's elevation at solar noon in most of the UK drops from around 55-60° in July to roughly 25-30° by late October, which means a panel mounted flat or at a shallow tilt is now catching sunlight at a much less efficient angle. If your mounts are adjustable, this is the month to act on it — our [panel angle guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-panel-angle) and [seasonal repositioning guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-move-panels-seasonally-uk) both recommend increasing tilt by roughly 15-20° for autumn and winter compared to your summer setting. It's a five-minute job that can recover a meaningful chunk of the seasonal drop, particularly for south-facing balcony or ground-mounted kits.

## Keeping self-consumption high as output falls

The upside of lower generation is that self-consumption becomes easier — you're less likely to be exporting surplus power you can't use. To make the most of a smaller daily output:

1. Shift washing machine, dishwasher, and tumble dryer cycles to late morning through early afternoon, when what little sun there is will actually be hitting the panels
2. Check your [energy monitor](/blog/best-energy-monitor-plug-in-solar-uk) daily rather than weekly — October's variability (one sunny day, three overcast ones) makes it easy to miss patterns if you're not watching closely
3. Clean panels once this month — autumn grime, fallen leaves, and bird activity build up fast and a dirty panel loses more output in low-light conditions than in bright summer sun
4. If you have an [EcoFlow STREAM](/blog/ecoflow-stream-review-uk) with battery storage, review your charge schedule now — October's shorter, weaker charging window means the battery may not fully top up on cloudier days, so prioritise essential loads

## What this means for your bill

With the [Ofgem price cap sitting around 24.5p/kWh](/blog/plug-in-solar-energy-price-cap-q4-2026-uk) heading into the winter quarter, even October's reduced output is still worth banking. At 55-65% self-consumption, a South East household generating 60 kWh across the month saves roughly £8-10; a Scottish household at 35 kWh is closer to £5-6. It's a smaller number than the summer months delivered, but it's the same principle as [how much plug-in solar saves overall](/blog/how-much-does-plug-in-solar-save-uk) — every kilowatt-hour you don't buy from the grid at the winter cap rate is money kept, even if the totals are more modest than July.

## Looking ahead to winter

October is really a transition month, not the trough. Output keeps falling through November and bottoms out around December, as covered in our [winter guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-in-winter-uk). If you haven't already, this is a sensible time to work through our [winter preparation checklist](/blog/plug-in-solar-winter-preparation-2026-uk) — checking cable glands and connections while the weather is still mild is far easier than doing it in a December downpour, and it's worth doing before the shorter days and worse weather make maintenance a chore rather than a five-minute check.

### Is a 30-40% drop in October normal?

Yes. It matches the seasonal pattern every UK solar system follows, plug-in or otherwise — daylight hours shrink and the sun sits lower in the sky. If your drop is significantly larger than 40%, check for new shading from bare trees, a dirty panel, or an angle that hasn't been adjusted since summer before assuming there's a fault.

### Should I increase my panel's tilt angle for October?

If your mounts are adjustable, yes — increasing tilt by around 15-20° from your summer setting helps the panel catch the lower autumn sun more directly. Ground-mounted and balcony-rail kits with adjustable brackets benefit the most; fixed flat mounts will simply see the full seasonal drop with no way to compensate.

### Will the clocks changing affect how much I generate?

No — the clocks change only shifts what time your generation appears to start and stop on the clock, not the total amount of sunlight your panels receive. The real driver of October's lower output is shorter days and a lower sun angle, not the switch from British Summer Time to GMT.
