## 8 Reasons for Low Solar Output

### 1. Shading
The #1 cause of underperformance. Even [partial shading](/blog/shading-plug-in-solar-output-uk) from a chimney, tree branch, or neighbouring building can halve output. Microinverters handle shading better than string inverters, but any shade is bad.

**Fix**: Observe your panels throughout the day. Move or trim shade sources if possible.

### 2. Wrong Panel Angle
Panels at the wrong [tilt angle](/blog/plug-in-solar-panel-angle) lose output. A wall-mounted panel (90°) produces ~30% less than one at the optimal 30-35°.

**Fix**: Use [adjustable brackets](/blog/best-adjustable-solar-panel-tilt-brackets-uk) to achieve the right angle for your latitude.

### 3. Dirty Panels
Dust, bird droppings, pollen, and tree sap reduce output by 5-25%. See our [cleaning guide](/blog/best-solar-panel-cleaning-tools-uk).

**Fix**: Clean panels every 2-3 months with plain water and a soft brush.

### 4. Cloudy Weather
UK weather is variable. A week of heavy cloud can reduce output by 70-80%. This is normal — don't compare cloudy-week output to clear-sky estimates.

**Fix**: None needed. Weekly and monthly averages will align with [PVGIS estimates](/calculator).

### 5. Orientation (Not South-Facing)
[East or west-facing](/blog/east-west-facing-plug-in-solar-uk) panels produce 15-25% less than south-facing. [North-facing](/blog/plug-in-solar-north-facing-garden-uk) panels lose 40-50%.

**Fix**: Reposition panels south-facing if possible. Otherwise, accept the lower output as normal for your orientation.

### 6. High Temperature
Solar panels lose ~0.3-0.5% efficiency per °C above 25°C. On a 35°C summer day, output drops by about 3-5%.

**Fix**: Ensure airflow behind panels. Avoid mounting flat against a wall with no gap.

### 7. Cable Losses
Long DC cable runs lose power as heat. A 20m run with thin cables can lose 3-5% of output.

**Fix**: Use appropriately rated [cables](/blog/best-solar-extension-cables-uk) — 4mm² for runs under 10m, 6mm² for longer runs.

### 8. Inverter Limiting
If your panels produce more than 800W DC, the inverter clips output to 800VA. This isn't a fault — it's [the legal limit](/blog/interim-product-specification-v2-plug-in-solar-uk).

**Fix**: No fix needed — this is normal and expected. The excess DC capacity improves performance in low light.
