Black Friday gets all the attention, but it isn't the only — or even necessarily the best — discount window for plug-in solar. If you missed the [Black Friday deals](/blog/plug-in-solar-black-friday-deals-2026-uk) in late November, or you're simply not in a rush to install before winter, Boxing Day and the January sales are worth planning around too. Here's how they actually compare, retailer by retailer.

## Why Boxing Day is different from Black Friday

Black Friday discounts are driven by pre-Christmas sales targets — retailers want units out the door before the holiday. Boxing Day and January sales are driven by something else: clearing stock ahead of new financial-year ranges and, increasingly for plug-in solar specifically, clearing out pre-spring inventory before the demand spike that typically starts in March as daylight hours lengthen.

That difference matters for plug-in solar because stock levels are tighter than for most electronics. Retailers like [B&Q, Screwfix, Currys and Wickes](/blog/bandq-vs-screwfix-vs-currys-wickes-plug-in-solar-uk) order ENA-compliant kits in batches, and once a batch sells through in a discount window, replacement stock can take weeks. That's the key practical difference from Black Friday: Boxing Day discounts tend to be on **existing warehouse stock**, so the specific model on sale depends heavily on what didn't sell in November.

## What tends to discount on Boxing Day

Based on how UK retail typically treats small-appliance and renewables stock around Christmas:

- **Previous-generation kits** — if a retailer introduced a new 800W model in autumn, the older 400W version is the one likely to be discounted to clear
- **Bundle deals** — mounting brackets, extension cables and energy monitors bundled with a panel kit rather than straight percentage-off the inverter itself
- **Returned/open-box stock** — Christmas gift returns in early January sometimes get resold at a discount, particularly through Currys and Amazon UK
- **EcoFlow promotions** — EcoFlow has run January promotional pricing on the [STREAM range](/blog/ecoflow-stream-review-uk) in past years tied to "new year, new energy bills" messaging

## January sales vs Boxing Day: which is better?

| | Boxing Day (26 Dec–early Jan) | January sales (mid–late Jan) |
|---|---|---|
| Stock breadth | Narrower — picking over Christmas leftovers | Wider — new stock often lands by mid-January |
| Discount depth | Can be deep on specific clearance lines | Usually smaller but more predictable |
| Best for | Bargain-hunting on a specific model you've already decided on | Comparing several kits with fresh stock available |

If you already know which kit you want — say, you've read our [EcoFlow STREAM review](/blog/ecoflow-stream-review-uk) and decided that's the one — Boxing Day is worth checking first. If you're still comparing options, waiting until mid-January usually gives you a fuller range to choose from.

## Does buying in winter even make sense?

This is the real question, and it's one a lot of bargain-hunters skip past. A plug-in solar kit bought in December or January will sit through the lowest-generation months before [spring performance](/blog/when-does-plug-in-solar-get-good-uk-spring) kicks in. That's not a reason to avoid buying — kits don't degrade sitting unused for a few weeks, and getting your [DNO notification](/blog/g98-dno-notification-step-by-step) submitted early means you're ready to mount panels as soon as weather allows — but it does mean the savings clock starts slower than if you'd bought in April.

Our [savings calculator](/calculator) can estimate what a kit bought now would actually generate and save between now and next autumn, which is a more useful number than the sticker discount alone.

## What to check before buying on price alone

A discount isn't a reason to skip due diligence. Before you buy, regardless of which sale you're buying in:

1. **Confirm it's on the ENA registry** — check the model against our [compliant device list](/blog/uk-compliant-plug-in-solar-devices-live). A discounted non-compliant kit is still non-compliant.
2. **Check the fuse and plug spec** — [800VA/3.5A, BS 1363 plug, 5A fuse](/blog/plug-in-solar-no-battery-rule-uk-explained) are non-negotiable regardless of price.
3. **Factor in your [energy tariff](/blog/best-energy-tariff-plug-in-solar-uk-2026)** — a cheap kit on a tariff with poor export rates saves less than a pricier kit on a better one.
4. **Read the return policy** — Christmas-period returns windows are often extended, which is useful if you want to test fit before committing.

## Where to watch for deals

Set price alerts or bookmark these for late December through January:

- **B&Q** — typically runs January "new year energy saving" promotions
- **Screwfix** — trade-focused but often undercuts on bundle pricing
- **Currys** — broadest range, most likely to have open-box clearance stock
- **Amazon UK** — fastest-moving prices, worth checking daily during sale windows
- **EcoFlow direct** — occasionally beats retailer pricing on STREAM kits during its own promotional windows

## The bottom line

Black Friday usually offers the single deepest discount window of the year, but it isn't the only one. Boxing Day can beat it on specific clearance models, and January sales tend to offer the broadest stock to compare from. If you're not in a rush, it's reasonable to wait and watch both — just don't let chasing a discount delay your [DNO notification](/blog/g98-dno-notification-step-by-step) once you've actually bought, since that part of the process takes time regardless of season.

### Is Boxing Day actually cheaper than Black Friday for plug-in solar?

Not always. Black Friday tends to have the deepest headline discounts because retailers plan stock specifically for it. Boxing Day discounts are more about clearing whatever's left, so pricing is less predictable — sometimes deeper on a specific model, sometimes barely discounted at all.

### Will stock run out if I wait until January?

It can for the most popular models, since UK retailers order ENA-compliant kits in limited batches. If you've already settled on a specific kit, buying it during whichever sale appears first is lower-risk than waiting for a hypothetical better price.

### Does it matter when I buy if I won't install until spring?

Not for the equipment itself — a plug-in solar kit doesn't lose performance sitting boxed over winter. It can be worth submitting your [DNO notification](/blog/g98-dno-notification-step-by-step) as soon as you buy, though, since that paperwork takes time and doesn't need to wait for installation weather.
