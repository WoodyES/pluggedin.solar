## Why Flat-Dwellers Need a Different Buying Guide

Most plug-in solar buying guides assume you have a garden, a south-facing fence, or a flat roof to work with. If you live in a flat, none of that applies. You're working with a balcony rail, a Juliet balcony, or a few square metres of decking, and the panels have to be light enough to lift, narrow enough to fit the rail, and secure enough that they won't go anywhere in a gust.

Since plug-in solar became legal across Great Britain on [27 August 2026](/blog/plug-in-solar-legal-27-august-2026-uk) under [SI 2026 No. 848](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk), the "plug-in microgenerator" category has grown fast — but not every kit on the shelf at B&Q or Currys is a sensible fit for a balcony. Here's what actually works, and what to check before you buy.

## What Makes a Kit "Flat-Friendly"

Before comparing products, it's worth being clear on what actually matters for a balcony or small outdoor space setup:

- **Weight per panel.** Anything over 10-12kg per panel is awkward to lift onto a rail bracket alone, and a non-starter on upper floors if you're carrying it up a stairwell.
- **Mounting flexibility.** Rail clamps, ratchet straps, or a flush wall mount beat ground-stake kits designed for gardens.
- **Cable length and routing.** A short run between panel and inverter means less loose cable flapping over a railing — see our [balcony solar installation guide](/blog/balcony-solar-installation-guide-uk) for the exact routing rules.
- **Legal compliance.** The kit must sit within the [800VA / 3.5A output cap](/blog/is-plug-in-solar-legal-uk), use a proper BS 1363 plug, and have no battery wired into the plug circuit — the full specification is in our [G98 notification guide](/blog/g98-notification-guide-uk-homeowners).
- **Tenancy status.** If you rent, check our [guide for renters](/blog/plug-in-solar-for-renters-uk) and, if you're leasehold, the [leasehold flat permission guide](/blog/leasehold-flat-solar-permission-uk) before buying anything.

## What to Avoid

Skip any kit marketed primarily for ground or roof mounting that only offers rail brackets as a separate accessory purchase — the extra cost and extra parts to lose track of rarely make sense for a balcony-only setup. Also be wary of kits that bundle a battery wired directly into the plug circuit: that configuration falls outside the legal "plug-in microgenerator" definition entirely, as we explain in our [battery vs no battery comparison](/blog/battery-storage-vs-no-battery-plug-in-solar-uk).

## Checking Before You Buy

Every kit sold legally in the UK needs to appear on the ENA's compliant device registry. We maintain an [up-to-date list of ENA-compliant plug-in solar devices](/blog/uk-compliant-plug-in-solar-devices-live) — it's worth a quick check against the exact model name on the box before you hand over any money, since retailers occasionally list older, non-compliant stock alongside certified kits.

### Can I mount a plug-in solar panel on a rented balcony?

In most cases yes, provided you use a rail clamp rather than drilling into the structure, and your tenancy agreement doesn't specifically prohibit exterior fixtures. Always check with your landlord or freeholder first — see our full [renters' guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-for-renters-uk) for the details.

### How much can one balcony panel realistically generate?

A single well-sited 400W panel on a south-facing balcony in the UK typically generates 1-1.5 units (kWh) a day in summer and considerably less in winter. Our [balcony output guide](/blog/how-much-does-balcony-solar-actually-generate) breaks down the numbers by orientation and season.

### Do I still need to notify my DNO for a balcony kit?

Yes — the G98 notification requirement applies regardless of where the panel is mounted, as long as the system connects to your home's electrical circuit. Follow our [step-by-step G98 guide](/blog/g98-dno-notification-step-by-step) to get it done in a few minutes online.
