## The October Shift

When the clocks go back (last Sunday in October), sunset moves from ~6pm to ~5pm. Your solar system loses roughly **one hour of evening generation**, and morning start-up is earlier in clock time.

## Output Expectations

| Period | Daily output (800W, Midlands) |
|--------|------------------------------|
| Early October | 2.0-2.5 kWh |
| Late October | 1.4-1.8 kWh |
| November | 1.0-1.5 kWh |

The drop from summer (~3.5 kWh/day) is noticeable but gradual.

## Adjustments to Make

### Shift Load Schedules
If you've been running washing machines at 1-2pm, move to **11am-1pm**. The solar peak narrows and shifts earlier in autumn.

### Increase Panel Tilt
If using [adjustable brackets](/blog/best-adjustable-solar-panel-tilt-brackets-uk), increase tilt by 10-15° from summer angle. The lower sun benefits from steeper panels.

### Self-Consumption Gets Easier
Ironically, lower generation means less export waste. Your [self-consumption ratio](/blog/plug-in-solar-self-consumption-tips-uk) may actually improve in autumn — you use a higher percentage of what you generate.

### Monitor for New Shading
Trees still in leaf combined with low sun angles create new [shading patterns](/blog/shading-plug-in-solar-output-uk). Check if nearby vegetation now casts shadows that weren't there in summer.

### Is October output worth worrying about?

Yes — October alone generates ~50 kWh (800W, Midlands), worth roughly £7 in savings. Small but cumulative.

### When does solar really drop off?

November-January is the low period. From February, generation recovers quickly. See our [monthly performance guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-uk-monthly-performance-guide).
