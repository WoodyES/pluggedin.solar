Two brands dominate UK plug-in solar search interest in the weeks before the [27 August 2026 legalisation date](/blog/plug-in-solar-legal-27-august-2026-uk): Bright Saver, backed by a heavy paid social campaign, and EcoFlow STREAM, backed by an established global power-electronics brand with a launched UK affiliate programme. This piece runs them side-by-side on the criteria that actually matter — regulatory fit, real-world output, ecosystem depth, price, and post-purchase support — so you can make the call without wading through marketing.

For background on each brand individually, see the [Bright Saver UK review](/blog/bright-saver-plug-in-solar-review-uk) and the [EcoFlow STREAM UK launch guide](/blog/ecoflow-stream-uk-launch-september-2026).

## Hardware, at the panel level

Both kits are built around monocrystalline silicon panels — typically two 400W panels for an 800W total — feeding a small microinverter that converts DC to grid-frequency AC and pushes it out through a UK three-pin plug. The panels themselves come from the same handful of Tier 1 OEMs that supply most of the European plug-in solar market. Expect broadly similar peak output, degradation curves, and temperature coefficients from either kit. Our [temperature coefficient guide](/blog/solar-panel-temperature-coefficient-uk) explains why the hardware differences are smaller than the marketing suggests.

Where they differ:

- **Microinverter.** EcoFlow uses its own microinverter with proprietary firmware and a cloud-connected app. Bright Saver typically ships with an OEM inverter (often Hoymiles or APsystems-family silicon) with basic on-device monitoring only.
- **Mounting hardware.** STREAM Garden Ground kits include a tilt frame with a factory-set angle range that suits most UK gardens. Bright Saver's included mounts vary by SKU — some are fixed-angle, some adjustable. Verify on the product page.
- **Cabling.** Both use standard MC4 connectors between panel and inverter. Length varies. If you need more reach, see the [MC4 extension cables guide](/blog/best-mc4-connectors-extension-cables-uk).

## UK regulatory fit

Both brands sell UK-configured kits that meet the requirements of [SI 2026 No. 848](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk) and the [interim product specification](/blog/plug-in-solar-interim-product-specification-uk). The critical checks are identical for either brand: 800VA maximum output, BS 1363 plug with 5A fuse, no battery in the plug circuit, no extension leads, one unit per household. Whichever you buy, verify the individual listing shows a UK plug and 800VA cap — do not buy from an EU listing and adapt.

Both require a G98 DNO notification after connection. Neither brand files this for you. Our [G98 step-by-step guide](/blog/g98-dno-notification-step-by-step) walks through the process.

## Ecosystem and app

This is the biggest real difference between the two kits.

**EcoFlow STREAM** ships with a full app: real-time generation data, historical logging, export tracking, and integration with the wider EcoFlow ecosystem including power stations and the [STREAM Ultra Pro battery variant](/blog/ecoflow-stream-800w-vs-stream-ultra-uk). If you plan to add battery storage down the line, STREAM gives you a native upgrade path without swapping hardware.

**Bright Saver** does not publish a first-party app in the UK market. Monitoring is either through the OEM inverter's basic interface or through a third-party smart plug like the [TP-Link Tapo P110](https://amzn.to/4m9Yh9U) (~£15) that you buy separately. It works, but it is a manual bolt-on rather than a native feature.

If app monitoring matters to you — and it matters more than most buyers expect, because it is the only way to spot a partial-shade or connector fault before you lose weeks of generation — STREAM wins.

## Realistic UK output

Both kits generate roughly 650–900 kWh per year in a typical UK location with south-facing installation. The variance is driven by shading, tilt and location, not by brand. See the [monthly performance guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-uk-monthly-performance-guide) for the seasonal breakdown and the [regional guides](/blog/best-uk-regions-plug-in-solar-generation) for London, Yorkshire, Wales and the South West figures.

At the current [Ofgem cap of ~24.5p/kWh](/blog/plug-in-solar-energy-price-cap-q4-2026-uk), that is £160–£220 a year off your bill if you self-consume most of the generation. Households out during the day without a battery see closer to £100–£140. The [self-consumption tips guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-self-consumption-tips-uk) covers how to close that gap.

## Price

Pricing moves week to week, but the typical UK street price for an 800W kit sits in the £600–£900 range for both brands. Bright Saver has been running introductory discounts through its paid social funnel; EcoFlow has been running structured discounts through its UK site and Amazon storefront. Neither has a durable price advantage.

Where EcoFlow pulls ahead on total cost of ownership is the upgrade path — you can add a STREAM Ultra Pro battery later without replacing the base kit. With Bright Saver, adding storage means a separate solution that does not integrate.

## Support and warranty

**EcoFlow** has a well-documented UK support process, a global warranty network, and years of history serving UK customers through its power station business. RMAs are handled through a UK or EU logistics partner. **Bright Saver** has a shorter track record and is still building out its UK support operation. Both offer standard 5-year warranties on the hardware — the difference is how easy it will be to actually claim in year four.

## The bottom line

Both kits work. Both are legal in the UK from 27 August 2026 provided you buy the UK-configured SKU. Both will generate 650–900 kWh a year on a well-sited install. The decision comes down to whether you value ecosystem depth (STREAM) or the simplest possible one-box proposition at the lowest introductory price (Bright Saver). For most UK buyers we recommend STREAM because the app, support and battery upgrade path pay off well beyond year one.

### Is Bright Saver cheaper than EcoFlow STREAM?

Sometimes, depending on which brand is running a promotion. Both typically sit in the £600–£900 range for an 800W kit at UK street prices. Bright Saver's paid-social discounts occasionally undercut STREAM by £50–£100, but STREAM's Amazon and UK-store deals close the gap regularly.

### Which kit has the better app?

EcoFlow STREAM has a native first-party app with real-time generation, historical logging, and export tracking. Bright Saver does not publish a UK-market app — monitoring is either through the OEM inverter's basic interface or a third-party smart plug you buy separately.

### Can I add a battery to either kit?

Only cleanly to EcoFlow STREAM, via the STREAM Ultra Pro variant. Bright Saver has no native battery integration, so adding storage means a separate solution that does not share monitoring or control. Note that under the UK interim spec, batteries cannot sit in the plug circuit itself — they must be handled outside the microgenerator connection.
