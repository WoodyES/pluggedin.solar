Every November, Ofgem confirms the energy price cap that will apply from the start of January through to the end of March — and every time it does, the numbers ripple straight through to plug-in solar economics. This year the announcement lands with plug-in solar past its first full season as a legal product category in Great Britain, which means for the first time there's a real base of owners who can see exactly how a cap change affects what their [EcoFlow STREAM](/blog/ecoflow-stream-review-uk) kit or equivalent actually saves them.

We covered the mechanics of the Q4 2026 cap in our [earlier price cap piece](/blog/plug-in-solar-energy-price-cap-q4-2026-uk). This article updates those numbers for the January 2027 period and looks specifically at what a shift in the cap does to payback timelines for the 800VA/3.5A kits legalised under [SI 2026 No. 848](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk).

## What's actually changing

Ofgem's quarterly cap review responds to wholesale gas and electricity prices, network costs, and policy levies — plug-in solar generation has no bearing on the cap itself, only on how much of it you avoid paying. Through most of 2026 the cap for a typical dual-fuel household sat close to **24.5p/kWh** for electricity. The January 2027 figure is expected to move within a few pence of that level, continuing the broadly upward trend Ofgem has shown since the cap mechanism was introduced.

That matters because a plug-in microgenerator doesn't change your tariff — it changes how many kWh you draw from the grid at whatever that tariff happens to be. The higher the cap, the more each kWh of self-consumed solar is worth.

## Savings at different cap levels

For a typical 800W plug-in solar kit generating roughly 850 kWh a year, with around 55% used directly in the home rather than exported:

| Price cap (p/kWh) | Annual import saved | Annual export (SEG) | Total saving |
|-------------------|--------------------|---------------------|---------------|
| 22p | £103 | £15 | £118 |
| 24.5p | £115 | £15 | £130 |
| 27p | £126 | £15 | £141 |
| 30p | £141 | £15 | £156 |

Roughly speaking, every 1p/kWh the cap moves adds or removes about £5 a year from an 800W system's savings. It's not a dramatic swing quarter to quarter, but it compounds: if the cap drifts upward through 2027 and 2028 as it has in recent years, the same kit you buy today keeps getting more valuable without you doing anything differently.

## Payback period, updated

| Kit cost | 22p cap payback | 24.5p cap payback | 27p cap payback |
|----------|------------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| £400 | 3.4 years | 3.1 years | 2.8 years |
| £600 | 5.1 years | 4.6 years | 4.3 years |
| £800 | 6.8 years | 6.2 years | 5.7 years |

Run your own numbers against your actual usage pattern with our [payback calculator](/blog/plug-in-solar-payback-calculator-800w-uk) — the averages above assume a fairly typical daytime consumption profile, and households that are home during the day (or run a lot of base-load appliances) will self-consume a higher share and see faster payback.

## Pairing the cap with the right tariff

The price cap sets the ceiling for a standard variable tariff, but it isn't the only number that matters. If you're on a flat-rate tariff, every kWh of solar self-consumption is worth the full cap rate. If you're on a [time-of-use tariff](/blog/best-time-of-use-tariffs-uk-2026) like Octopus's [Flux](/blog/octopus-flux-tariff-review-uk), the picture is more nuanced — your solar is competing with genuinely cheap overnight import rates, so the saving per kWh self-consumed during the day can actually exceed the cap rate on a half-hourly tariff, while off-peak charging shifts load away from your generation window entirely. Worth checking your supplier's actual unit rates rather than assuming the cap figure applies directly.

Export payments are a separate line and much smaller: [SEG rates](/blog/plug-in-solar-export-payments-seg-uk) are set independently by suppliers and have stayed in the 3-6p/kWh range regardless of where the cap sits, so don't expect export income to track the headline cap number.

## New kits on the ENA registry worth knowing about

If you're weighing up whether to buy before or after the cap announcement, it's also worth checking what's newly compliant. The [ENA plug-in solar device registry](/blog/uk-compliant-plug-in-solar-devices-live) is updated continuously, and the last few months have seen a steady trickle of new entrants beyond the original EcoFlow and Anker listings — including Worldwide Solar's plug-and-play kits built around Hoymiles and VaySunic microinverters paired with AIKO and JA Solar panels, and budget-focused entrants like Solarfy's 455W kit. We round up the latest additions in our [new ENA-approved kits roundup](/blog/new-ena-approved-plug-in-solar-kits-october-2026-uk) — more compliant manufacturers generally means more competitive pricing over time, which is the other half of the payback equation alongside the price cap.

## Buy now or wait for the next cap review?

There's no single right answer, but the trend argument holds: UK electricity prices have moved upward over the past decade more often than they've fallen, and even a product that pays back in under five years keeps earning for the other 20 of its working life. If the cap climbs to 27-30p/kWh by 2028 — plausible given network investment and policy costs — an 800W kit bought now will have already banked a couple of years of savings at today's rate before capturing the higher future rate too. Waiting for a marginally lower kit price has to be weighed against the savings you forgo in the meantime; our [winter preparation guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-winter-preparation-2026-uk) covers how much generation you're realistically giving up by delaying into the darkest months.

### When does Ofgem confirm the new price cap?

Ofgem typically announces each quarter's cap in late November (for January-March), late February (for April-June), late May (for July-September) and late August (for October-December), giving suppliers and households roughly five weeks' notice before the new rates take effect.

### Does a higher price cap make plug-in solar more worthwhile?

Yes, directly. A plug-in microgenerator saves you money by offsetting grid imports, so a higher cap means each self-consumed kWh is worth more and payback periods shorten, all else being equal.

### Will my export payment change with the price cap?

Not automatically. SEG export rates are set by individual suppliers rather than tied to the price cap, and have generally stayed in the low single digits of pence per kWh throughout 2026 regardless of cap movements.
