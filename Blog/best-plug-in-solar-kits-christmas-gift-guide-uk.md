Plug-in solar has had an odd first few months as a legal product in the UK. Since 27 August 2026, when SI 2026 No. 848 made the "plug-in microgenerator" a recognised category, retailers have scrambled to stock compliant kits, and buyers have scrambled to work out which ones are actually worth the money. With Christmas six weeks out, this is the point where two groups collide: people who want to give a plug-in solar kit as a genuinely useful present, and people who want to get one installed before the shortest, darkest weeks of the year make the decision feel pointless.

Both groups are asking the same underlying question — is now a sensible time to buy? The honest answer is yes, but with caveats about what you're buying and why.

## Why buying now (rather than waiting for spring) still makes sense

It's tempting to assume plug-in solar is a spring purchase — buy in March, install in April, enjoy summer generation. There's truth in that: output in December and January in most of the UK is a fraction of what you'll see in June. But waiting has real costs too.

Retailers tend to run their best plug-in solar discounts around Black Friday and the post-Christmas sales window, not in spring when demand is highest. If you buy in November or December, you lock in a kit at a lower price and have it ready to go the moment daylight starts stretching out again in February. You also avoid the spring rush, when fitters booking balcony or garden installs get backed up and ENA-compliant stock briefly runs short, as it did in the first weeks after the law changed.

There's also a quieter argument: a plug-in solar kit under the tree (or as a joint household present) is a genuinely useful gift in a way that a lot of consumer electronics aren't. It pays for itself, typically within two to four years depending on your [region's generation](/blog/best-uk-regions-plug-in-solar-generation) and how much of the output you self-consume.

## What's actually worth buying this Christmas

Not every "compliant" kit on sale is equally good, and not every discount is a real one. A few principles for anyone buying now:

**Stick to the ENA registry.** Every legally sellable plug-in solar kit in the UK has to appear on the Energy Networks Association's G98 compliant devices list. We maintain a live, continuously updated version of that registry [here](/blog/uk-compliant-plug-in-solar-devices-live) — check any kit against it before buying, especially if it's from a retailer you don't recognise or a marketplace seller rather than a named brand.

## New manufacturers worth knowing about

The ENA registry has grown steadily since August, and the most recent additions worth watching are smaller UK-based manufacturers rather than the big three everyone already knows. Solarfy and Worldwide Solar have both had new plug-and-play kits approved in the past month, often pairing well-known microinverters (Hoymiles, VaySunic) with panels from AIKO and JA Solar. These aren't household names yet, but they're on the registry, which is the only test that actually matters. If you want to see what's been newly approved, our [round-up of October's newly ENA-approved kits](/blog/new-ena-approved-plug-in-solar-kits-october-2026-uk) tracks this as it happens.

## What to skip

Avoid anything advertised as plug-in solar that includes an integral battery wired into the plug circuit itself — the law is explicit that there's no battery permitted in the plug-in circuit, only downstream of it via a separate connection. Avoid anything that ships with an extension lead as standard "for convenience" — extension leads are banned under the same regulation, full stop, because they defeat the point of a dedicated, fused, BS 1363 socket connection. And avoid any listing that doesn't show a visible ENA reference number; if a seller can't produce one when asked, don't buy it, however good the price looks.

## Setting expectations for winter output

If you do buy and install before Christmas, be realistic about what you'll see in the first few weeks. December and January generation in most of the UK is roughly a fifth to a quarter of what you'll get in June, even with a perfectly sited 800W kit. That's not a fault with the system — it's geometry and day length. The benefit of buying now is having everything fitted, registered with your DNO under [G98](/blog/g98-notification-guide-uk-homeowners), and ready to go, so that when generation picks up from late February onward, you're not scrambling to install in the one week of decent weather you get.

### Is it legal to buy and install plug-in solar as a gift before Christmas?

Yes. Plug-in solar has been legal UK-wide since 27 August 2026 under SI 2026 No. 848, with no seasonal restriction. The only requirement is that the specific kit is on the ENA's G98 compliant devices registry and that whoever installs it notifies their Distribution Network Operator afterwards.

### Will a plug-in solar kit bought now actually save money by Christmas?

Not meaningfully. Winter generation is low across the UK, so don't expect a kit installed in December to visibly dent your bill before February or March. The value in buying now is price and availability, not immediate winter output.
