Brighton & Hove sits on England's south coast at roughly 50.8°N — one of the sunniest latitudes in the country, on a par with Devon and Cornwall for annual sunshine hours. Since plug-in microgenerators became legal nationwide on 27 August 2026 under [SI 2026 No. 848](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk), Brighton has quickly become one of the more active plug-in solar markets outside London — partly the sun, partly the demographics: a young, renting, flat-dwelling population that fits the plug-in-solar model better than most UK cities.

This guide covers what actually changes when you install plug-in solar in Brighton specifically: generation numbers, the housing stock you're working with, and the coastal quirks that don't show up in a national buying guide.

## Brighton's sunshine numbers

Brighton averages around **1,900-2,000 hours of sunshine a year**, comfortably above the UK average of roughly 1,400 hours, and among the highest of any English city. Translated into generation, an 800W plug-in kit facing south with a clear run to the horizon (easy to get on the seafront, harder a few streets back) should produce:

| Location type | Annual kWh (800W) | Annual saving (~24.5p/kWh) |
|---|---|---|
| Seafront/clear aspect | 830-870 | £112-£118 |
| Typical Regency terrace garden | 760-800 | £103-£108 |
| North-facing flat balcony | 480-540 | £65-£73 |

That's a payback of roughly 5-5.5 years on a typical £600 kit even without perfect siting — see our [payback calculator](/blog/plug-in-solar-payback-calculator-800w-uk) for a figure specific to your postcode and panel angle.

## The housing stock problem (and opportunity)

Brighton & Hove has one of the highest proportions of flats and shared housing of any city outside London — well over half of homes are flats, maisonettes or converted Regency terraces split into multiple units. That cuts two ways for plug-in solar:

- **Balconies and Juliet balconies** on seafront and Kemptown terraces are a genuine growth area — see our [balcony solar guide](/blog/balcony-solar-uk-guide) for rail-mounting options that suit the narrow iron balconies common on Regency Square and Brunswick Terrace.
- **Conservation areas** cover large parts of central Brighton and Hove, including most of the Regency seafront. Plug-in solar doesn't need planning permission in the way roof-mounted panels can, but check our [listed buildings guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-listed-buildings-uk) before mounting anything to a facade — freestanding garden or balcony-rail kits avoid the issue entirely.
- **Shared gardens and courtyards** behind converted terraces are common. If you're not the sole occupant, get written agreement from co-residents or the freeholder before installing, even though the kit itself is portable and non-permanent.

## Salt air and coastal corrosion

This is the one factor a generic UK guide won't tell you: Brighton's seafront and Kemptown properties sit directly in a salt-laden marine environment, and standard aluminium panel frames and cheap MC4 connectors corrode noticeably faster here than inland. Our [coastal corrosion guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-coastal-areas-salt-corrosion-uk) covers what to look for, but the short version for Brighton specifically:

- Choose kits with anodised or marine-grade aluminium frames, not painted steel
- Rinse panels with fresh water every 4-6 weeks if you're within half a mile of the seafront
- IP68-rated connectors matter more here than almost anywhere else in the UK

## DNO notification in Brighton

Brighton & Hove sits within UK Power Networks' distribution area. The G98 notification process is identical to the rest of the country — see our [step-by-step G98 guide](/blog/g98-dno-notification-step-by-step) — but UKPN's online portal has generally been processing plug-in microgenerator notifications within a few working days since the September 2026 surge in applications, faster than some other DNOs during the same period.

## What to buy

Given the flat-heavy, balcony-heavy nature of Brighton housing, a balcony-rated kit is the most commonly fitted option in the city centre, while seafront and Hove houses with proper gardens tend to go for the standard 800W ground/wall kit.

### Is Brighton actually sunnier than the rest of the UK?

Yes — Brighton averages around 1,900-2,000 sunshine hours a year against a UK average nearer 1,400, making it one of the better-performing locations for plug-in solar generation in England.

### Do I need permission to fit a balcony kit on a Regency building?

Not for a freestanding, non-drilled rail-mounted kit in most cases, but conservation area rules can still apply to anything fixed to the facade itself. Check with your freeholder or the council's conservation team before drilling, and see our [listed buildings guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-listed-buildings-uk) for the detail.

### Does sea air really damage solar panels faster?

Yes. Salt-laden air accelerates corrosion of untreated aluminium frames and standard connectors. Marine-grade or anodised frames and IP68 connectors last significantly longer within a mile or so of the Brighton seafront.
