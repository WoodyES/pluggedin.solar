Black Friday gets most of the attention, but for a lot of UK shoppers Cyber Monday is where the real decision gets made — not because the discounts are necessarily deeper, but because it's the last big push before retailers switch focus to Christmas delivery deadlines. For plug-in solar, this is the first year the category has existed for either event, so there's no history to lean on. If you've been tracking [Black Friday pricing](/blog/plug-in-solar-black-friday-deals-2026-uk) and wondering whether to hold out three more days, here's what actually tends to happen and what's worth checking before you commit.

## Why Cyber Monday is a different animal to Black Friday

Black Friday discounting in the UK has historically been led by in-store and bricks-and-mortar retailers — B&Q, Currys and Screwfix all run physical promotions alongside their online pricing. Cyber Monday, by contrast, is an online-only event that originated in the US and has been adopted here mainly by e-commerce-first sellers. For plug-in solar specifically, that means Amazon UK and direct-to-consumer brand sites (EcoFlow's own UK store among them) are more likely to move on Cyber Monday than the high-street retailers, who've typically already committed to their Black Friday pricing and don't discount further three days later.

That has a practical implication: if you're shopping at a physical retailer like B&Q or Wickes, Black Friday is probably your best shot — Cyber Monday is unlikely to improve on it there. If you're buying online, particularly from Amazon UK or EcoFlow UK directly, it's worth checking both dates before pulling the trigger, since the two events can have genuinely different stock and pricing.

## What to actually compare between the two events

Don't just compare headline prices — compare what's included. A kit advertised at a lower price on Cyber Monday sometimes drops the mounting hardware or extension cable that was bundled in on Friday. Before buying on either date, confirm:

- The total output matches what you need (most compliant kits are 400W or 800W — see our [400W vs 800W comparison](/blog/400w-vs-800w-plug-in-solar-uk-guide) if you're unsure which suits your space)
- The inverter is on the [ENA-approved compliant devices list](/blog/uk-compliant-plug-in-solar-devices-live) — this matters more during sale periods, when unfamiliar or newly-listed brands sometimes appear at aggressive discounts
- The plug and fuse meet the BS 1363 / 5A fuse spec required under [SI 2026 No. 848](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk)
- Delivery timing — a Cyber Monday order placed on the Monday can sometimes arrive later than a Black Friday order placed four days earlier, simply because of order volume

## Should you actually wait?

If you've already found a compliant kit at a genuinely good Black Friday price, there's no strong reason to hold out — the risk of Cyber Monday simply repeating the same price, or selling out of the exact configuration you wanted, is real. Where waiting makes more sense is if you're specifically watching an online-only retailer or a direct brand site that historically saves its best pricing for the Monday rather than the Friday. It's also worth factoring in the [Ofgem price cap change for Q1 2027](/blog/plug-in-solar-energy-price-cap-q4-2026-uk), which lands in the same window — a slightly higher unit rate makes the payback case for any kit marginally stronger regardless of which day you buy.

One thing that doesn't change between the two events: delivery lead times tend to stretch as Christmas approaches. If you want a system generating before the shortest day, don't leave the decision to the very last discount window — see our [first week setup guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-first-week-setup-guide-uk) for how long installation actually takes once the kit arrives.

Retailers worth checking on both dates include B&Q, Currys, Amazon UK, Screwfix, Wickes and Lidl — our [retailer comparison](/blog/bandq-vs-screwfix-vs-currys-wickes-plug-in-solar-uk) breaks down which tends to discount hardest and which keeps the broadest compliant range in stock.

### Does Cyber Monday ever beat Black Friday pricing on plug-in solar?

Sometimes, but mainly at online-only sellers like Amazon UK or direct brand stores. Physical retailers rarely drop prices further after their Black Friday campaign ends, so if you're buying in-store, Friday is usually your best price.

### Is it risky to buy a plug-in solar kit during a flash sale?

Only if you skip the compliance check. Verify the inverter model against the [ENA compliant devices registry](/blog/uk-compliant-plug-in-solar-devices-live) before buying, especially for unfamiliar brands that appear only during sale periods.

### What happens if I miss both Black Friday and Cyber Monday?

Nothing drastic — compliant kits are now a permanent retail category, not a one-off promotion. You'll simply be buying at full price until the next seasonal sale, likely around Boxing Day; see our [Boxing Day and January sales guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-boxing-day-january-sales-uk) for what tends to come next.
