## December: The Low Point

December has the shortest days and lowest sun angle. An 800W system in the Midlands generates roughly **0.5-0.9 kWh per day** — enough to power your fridge and a few LED lights, but not much more.

## Christmas Week Generation

| Day | Typical generation | Covers |
|-----|-------------------|--------|
| Short, overcast | 0.3-0.5 kWh | Router + standby devices |
| Average December day | 0.6-0.9 kWh | Fridge + router + standby |
| Clear, sunny December day | 1.0-1.5 kWh | Basic base load |

## Christmas Energy Consumption

Christmas is a high-consumption period — lights, cooking, heating, and entertainment all spike. Your solar contribution will be minimal, but every kWh still counts.

Typical Christmas Day electricity use: **15-25 kWh** (cooking turkey, lights, TV, heating). Your panels might contribute 0.5-1.0 kWh — roughly 3-5% of the day's needs.

## The Silver Lining

You're almost certainly **home during Christmas** — meaning self-consumption is high. Even low generation is fully used.

## January Recovery

By mid-January, days are noticeably longer. February sees generation roughly **double** December's output, and by March you're back to meaningful daily savings.

### Should I worry about low December output?

No. December represents about 2-3% of annual generation. The other 10 months more than compensate.

### Can solar power my Christmas lights?

Partially. LED Christmas lights use 5-20W — well within your system's December output. But only during daylight hours unless you have a battery.
