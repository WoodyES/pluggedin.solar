Plug-in solar has been legal across the whole of the UK since [27 August 2026](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk), and York — a city built almost entirely inside and around a medieval walled centre, with a huge amount of listed and historic housing stock — is a market where the rules around what you can and can't mount matter more than in most places. Between the Georgian terraces of Bootham and Clifton, the Victorian streets of South Bank and Bishopthorpe Road, and the newer estates spreading out towards Acomb, Fulford and Haxby, York has a genuinely mixed housing picture. Here's what actually applies if you're anywhere from the city walls to the outer ring road, or out towards Selby, Tadcaster or Malton.

## Does York get enough sun for plug-in solar to be worth it?

York sits in the Vale of York, broadly flat and open compared with the Pennine fringes further west, which helps generation slightly relative to hillier parts of [Yorkshire](/blog/plug-in-solar-yorkshire-guide-uk). A well-sited [800W kit](/blog/how-much-electricity-does-800w-solar-generate-uk) on a south-facing wall or garden frame typically produces somewhere around 580–630 kWh a year in and around York — close to [Leeds](/blog/plug-in-solar-leeds-guide-uk) and the rest of West and North Yorkshire, and a touch ahead of more cloud-prone spots further north. The [regional generation guide](/blog/best-uk-regions-plug-in-solar-generation) has the full city comparison if you're weighing York against Leeds, Sheffield or further south.

Local topography is less of an issue here than in Leeds, since York's flood plain setting keeps most of the city relatively flat. The bigger constraint is building density and overshadowing in the oldest parts of town — narrow streets inside and just outside the walls, like those around Micklegate and Walmgate, can lose a surprising amount of midday sun to neighbouring rooflines. The [shading solutions guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-shading-solutions-uk) is worth a read before committing to a mounting spot in the older terraces.

## York's historic housing stock and what it means for mounting

This is the part where York genuinely differs from most other UK cities. A very large proportion of the city centre and surrounding conservation areas — Bootham, Clifton, South Bank, Fulford — sit inside designated conservation areas, and a smaller but significant number of individual properties are listed buildings in their own right. Neither status bans a compliant plug-in microgenerator kit outright, since the legal framework treats it as a plug-in appliance rather than a permanent electrical installation, but both can bring additional planning considerations around visible alterations to the exterior of a property. Our [conservation areas guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-conservation-areas-uk) and [listed buildings guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-listed-buildings-uk) cover exactly what you need to check with the council before mounting anything to brickwork, a wall, or a visible part of a roof in York's historic core.

For most buyers in these areas, a freestanding ground-mount frame placed discreetly in a back garden or yard, rather than anything wall-fixed or roof-fixed, is the simplest way to avoid the question altogether — nothing is attached to the fabric of the building, so there's nothing for a conservation officer to object to. Outside the historic centre, in the Victorian terraces of South Bank or the interwar semis around Acomb and Tang Hall, wall-mounting and pitched-roof options open up as normal; the [terraced houses guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-terraced-houses-uk) covers the practical side for properties with little outdoor space.

## The rules are the same in York as everywhere else

York households follow the same [SI 2026 No. 848](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk) framework as the rest of the country: 800VA/3.5A maximum output through a standard BS 1363 plug into a 5A-fused socket, no battery permitted in the plug circuit, no extension leads between panel and socket, and one plug-in microgenerator unit per household. Your kit still needs notifying to your local Distribution Network Operator under the simplified [G98 process](/blog/g98-dno-notification-step-by-step) — for York and the wider Vale of York that's Northern Powergrid, and in practice the notification is usually handled automatically by the retailer or manufacturer's app when you set the unit up, rather than something you need to file yourself.

It's also worth checking the ENA's live [compliant plug-in solar devices registry](/blog/uk-compliant-plug-in-solar-devices-live) before buying — the list of approved inverters and kits is updated regularly as new manufacturers get G98 sign-off, and buying something that isn't on it is the most common way people end up with a kit that technically can't be legally connected.

## Autumn generation in York

Buying in October means you're heading straight into the steepest part of the autumn daylight drop-off rather than catching the tail end of summer. The [northern England generation guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-northern-england-generation) gives a realistic month-by-month curve for York's latitude, and it's worth setting expectations now rather than being surprised when output falls noticeably through November and December. Most owners find it much easier to judge whether a kit is performing well once they've got a full month of comparable data rather than the first week or two after installation.

### Do I need planning permission for plug-in solar in a York conservation area?

No planning permission is required for a compliant plug-in microgenerator kit, since it's classed as a plug-in appliance rather than a permanent electrical installation — this applies inside conservation areas too. Listed buildings can still bring additional considerations around visible alterations to the fabric of the building, so check with the council or use a freestanding ground-mount frame in a garden or yard to avoid the question entirely.

### How much does an 800W plug-in solar kit cost in York?

Pricing is set nationally rather than locally — a compact kit typically runs from around £400 for a balcony-style setup to £800–£900 for a full ground-mount kit with a tilt frame. York retailers carry the same prices as elsewhere in the country; the main local variable is in-store stock and delivery speed.

### Is York a good location for plug-in solar compared to the rest of Yorkshire?

Yes, broadly in line with Leeds and the rest of the Vale of York, and slightly ahead of hillier parts of West Yorkshire thanks to the flatter, more open terrain. The main practical constraint for many York buyers isn't generation — it's the city's historic housing stock, which makes a freestanding ground-mount kit the simplest option in conservation areas and around listed buildings.
