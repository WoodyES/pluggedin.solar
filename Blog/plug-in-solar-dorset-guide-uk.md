Dorset doesn't get talked about much in the plug-in solar conversation, which is a shame, because it quietly sits among the better counties in England for it. Long sunshine hours along the Jurassic Coast, a mild maritime climate, and a housing stock that ranges from thatched cottages in Blandford Forum to new-build estates around Poole and Bournemouth make it a genuinely varied testbed for the technology. Since [plug-in solar became legal across Great Britain on 27 August 2026](/blog/plug-in-solar-legal-27-august-2026-uk) under [SI 2026 No. 848](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk), we've had a steady stream of questions from readers in Dorchester, Weymouth, Poole and Bridport, so here's the local picture.

## Dorset's sunshine advantage

Dorset averages around 1,750–1,900 hours of sunshine a year, comfortably above the England average and only a shade behind the sunniest parts of the south coast like Sussex and Kent. That puts it in similar territory to our [south west England guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-south-west-england-guide), though Dorset edges out inland Devon and Somerset slightly thanks to less cloud rolling in off Dartmoor and Exmoor. Coastal towns — Weymouth, Swanage, Lyme Regis — tend to do best, with fewer overcast days than the Blackmore Vale further inland. If you're deciding between a garden kit and a wall-mounted one, coastal exposure usually means you can afford a slightly less optimal aspect and still generate well.

## Salt air and coastal mounting

Being a coastal county cuts both ways. The upside is sunshine; the downside is salt-laden air corroding exposed metalwork faster than inland counties, particularly for properties in Weymouth, Swanage and along the Isle of Purbeck. Our [coastal areas and salt corrosion guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-coastal-areas-salt-corrosion-uk) covers this in detail, but the short version for Dorset residents: stick to marine-grade or well-sealed connectors, check cable glands annually, and rinse panels occasionally if you're within a mile or two of the sea. None of this makes coastal plug-in solar a bad idea — it just means a bit more upkeep than a kit in, say, Dorchester's town centre.

## Thatched roofs and conservation villages

Dorset has one of the highest concentrations of thatched roofs in England, especially in villages around Blandford Forum, Beaminster and the Piddle Valley. Thatch and roof-mounted solar don't mix well for fire-safety reasons, but this is exactly where plug-in solar earns its keep: a ground-mounted frame on a lawn or driveway, or a wall-mounted bracket on a garden wall, keeps the kit entirely off the thatch while still catching full sun. Many of these villages also sit in conservation areas, so if you want to keep a kit out of sight from the lane, our [conservation areas guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-conservation-areas-uk) and [listed buildings guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-listed-buildings-uk) go through what's sensible versus what's legally required (spoiler: plug-in kits need neither planning permission nor listed building consent anywhere in Great Britain, since they're classed as portable appliances).

## Poole and Bournemouth: dense housing, small gardens

The Poole–Bournemouth–Christchurch conurbation is Dorset's most densely populated stretch, with plenty of flats, maisonettes and terraced houses with little or no garden. This is squarely where [balcony solar](/blog/balcony-solar-uk-guide) and [flat-roof kits](/blog/plug-in-solar-flat-roof-no-drilling-uk) come into their own — a balcony rail mount or a small ground frame on a shared patio can still generate meaningfully, even on a modest south-facing aspect. Renters in the area should also check our [plug-in solar for renters guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-for-renters-uk), since a portable kit that unplugs and moves with you is one of the few home-energy upgrades that doesn't need landlord sign-off for installation itself (DNO notification and tenancy agreement checks still apply).

## Caravans and the Dorset coast's holiday trade

Dorset has one of the highest concentrations of caravan parks and holiday lets in England, stretching from the Isle of Purbeck to Christchurch Bay. For static caravan owners and seasonal let hosts, a portable plug-in kit is a popular way to offset daytime electricity use without rewiring a holiday unit. Our [caravans and motorhomes guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-caravans-motorhomes-uk) and [Airbnb hosts guide](/blog/plug-in-solar-airbnb-hosts-uk) cover the practical side — notification responsibilities, weatherproofing for a site that's often unattended between lets, and how exposed coastal pitches hold up against wind.

### Is Dorset a good location for plug-in solar?

Yes — Dorset's sunshine hours are above the England average and its mild coastal climate suits plug-in solar well. The main local considerations are salt corrosion near the coast and avoiding roof-mounting on thatched properties, both easily managed with ground or wall mounts.

### Can I mount plug-in solar on a thatched cottage in Dorset?

Not on the roof itself — thatch and solar mounting don't mix for fire-safety reasons. A ground-mounted or wall-mounted kit keeps the system entirely off the thatch while still generating well, and needs no planning permission even in a conservation village.

### Does salt air affect plug-in solar kits near the Dorset coast?

It can accelerate corrosion on exposed metal connectors over time. Marine-grade or well-sealed hardware and an occasional rinse for properties very close to the sea largely solves this — it doesn't rule out coastal installation.
