## The short answer: no, it won't

If the grid goes down, your plug-in solar kit shuts off automatically — even in the middle of a sunny afternoon. This catches a lot of new owners out, especially anyone who bought a kit half-expecting it to double as backup power during a storm-related outage. It's worth understanding why, because it's not a fault or a design flaw. It's a deliberate, legally mandated safety feature.

## Why it happens: anti-islanding protection

Every [plug-in microgenerator](/blog/plug-in-microgenerator-uk-what-is-it) sold legally in the UK under [SI 2026 No. 848](/blog/si-2026-848-plug-in-solar-explained-uk) must include anti-islanding protection. This is a safety mechanism that detects when the grid connection is lost and immediately stops the inverter exporting any power into your home's wiring.

The reason is straightforward: engineers repairing a "dead" line after a power cut assume it really is dead. If your solar panel kept quietly feeding electricity into your ring main, it could re-energise a cable an engineer believes is safe to touch. Anti-islanding exists specifically to prevent that scenario — it's a legal requirement for any grid-connected generator, not just the plug-in category, and it applies whether the outage is a fault, planned maintenance, or a National Grid-wide event.

For [plug-in solar specifically](/blog/is-plug-in-solar-legal-uk), this ties directly into the [no-battery-in-circuit rule](/blog/plug-in-solar-no-battery-rule-uk-explained). The 800VA/3.5A limit and the ban on integrated storage in the plug circuit mean there's no way for the system to "island" itself and keep running independently — it's built to fail safe, not to fail useful.

## What this means in practice

- A power cut at 2pm on a sunny day still cuts your solar output to zero, instantly
- The system doesn't "store up" the interrupted generation — that energy is simply lost while the outage lasts
- Your panels will resume generating automatically once the grid supply is restored and the inverter completes its reconnection checks (usually a few minutes)
- This applies regardless of which retailer or brand you bought from — [B&Q, Currys, Screwfix, Wickes, Lidl or Amazon](/blog/retailers-stocking-plug-in-solar-uk-2026) all sell kits certified to the same standard

If your kit seems to stay off longer than expected after power is restored, that's usually just the reconnection delay working as intended — see our guide on [why a system isn't generating](/blog/plug-in-solar-not-generating-troubleshooting-uk) before assuming something's broken.

A [portable power station](/blog/best-portable-power-station-plug-in-solar-uk) charged from its own solar panel (or from the mains in advance) can run a router, lamps, a laptop or a fridge for a few hours during a cut, because it's a closed system that never touches your house wiring. ## Does this affect the economics?

Not meaningfully. UK power cuts are rare and typically brief — most households see under an hour of unplanned outage per year on average. Losing generation during that window has a negligible effect on the [savings a plug-in system delivers](/blog/how-much-does-plug-in-solar-save-uk) over a full year. It's more of an expectations issue than a financial one: buy plug-in solar to cut your bill, not as an emergency backup plan.

Storms are the one scenario worth flagging separately, since outages cluster around bad weather — which is also when you're least likely to be generating much solar anyway. If storm resilience is a priority, pair your [plug-in solar setup](/blog/plug-in-solar-uk-complete-guide) with a charged power station rather than relying on the panels for backup.

## Related safety checks worth doing anyway

Since anti-islanding depends on your inverter correctly sensing grid status, it's worth keeping the basics in order:

1. Don't bypass or disable any part of the supplied inverter or control unit — it invalidates both the safety certification and your warranty
2. If your [RCD trips](/blog/plug-in-solar-rcd-tripping-uk) or the [breaker trips](/blog/plug-in-solar-tripping-breaker-uk) frequently, get it checked rather than assuming it's related to grid events
3. Keep the unit's firmware and app updated, as reconnection-timing fixes are sometimes pushed as software updates
4. If you're unsure whether an outage is local (a blown fuse in your own home) or a wider grid cut, check your supplier's power cut line or the Energy Networks Association's outage map before troubleshooting your solar kit

### Will my plug-in solar panel work if only my house loses power but the street doesn't?

No — the same anti-islanding rule applies to any loss of your local grid connection, including a fault on your own supply, a tripped main fuse, or work being done on your specific connection. The inverter can't distinguish "my house" from "the whole street" — it simply detects that it no longer sees a live grid reference and shuts down.

### Can I get an exemption or override this for emergencies?

No. Anti-islanding is a mandatory safety requirement, not a configurable setting, and disabling it would make the kit illegal to use and dangerous to engineers working on the network. If backup power matters to you, use a separate battery-based power station instead.

### Will I lose my stored solar credit or export payments during an outage?

No — outages don't affect [export payments](/blog/plug-in-solar-export-payments-seg-uk) or your smart meter's historical readings. You simply generate nothing for the duration of the cut, exactly as if it were an overcast day, and normal generation resumes once power is back.
